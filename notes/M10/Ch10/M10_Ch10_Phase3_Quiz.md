**Q1. (Short answer)**
State the TWO limbs of the rule in Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461, and name the type of action by which a member may nevertheless sue on the company's behalf where the wrongdoers control the company.

**Q2. (MCQ)**
Beacon Bank lends HK$8 million to Marble Ltd. The facility agreement is signed by two directors of Marble Ltd. Unknown to Beacon Bank, Marble Ltd's articles prohibit the directors from approving any borrowing above HK$2 million without an ordinary resolution of the members, and no such resolution was passed. Beacon Bank knew nothing of the restriction. Which section of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) codifies the indoor management rule so that, in favour of a person dealing with a company in good faith, the power of the company's directors to bind the company is regarded as free of any limitation under any relevant document of the company?

A. Section 115
B. Section 117
C. Section 124
D. Section 461

**Q3. (MCQ)**
Kelvin is the sole director and 95% shareholder of Harvest Logistics Ltd ('Harvest'), which operates a cold-storage business. Harvest loses a breach-of-contract claim brought by a former customer, Pearl Ltd, and judgment is entered against Harvest for HK$2.4 million. One week after judgment, Kelvin incorporates Frost Logistics Ltd ('Frost'), of which he is also the sole director and shareholder. He transfers Harvest's customer contracts, refrigerated trucks and goodwill to Frost for HK$1, deliberately leaves Harvest with no assets, and instructs Harvest's solicitors not to defend any enforcement proceedings. Pearl Ltd now seeks to enforce its judgment against Frost and Kelvin. Which statement BEST reflects the legal position?

A. The court is likely to pierce the corporate veil because Frost was interposed as a façade to evade Harvest's existing legal obligation to Pearl Ltd.
B. The court cannot pierce the corporate veil because Frost was not incorporated with any deceptive intent at the time of its incorporation.
C. The court cannot pierce the corporate veil because Kelvin's control of both companies is, by itself, never a sufficient ground to disregard separate legal personality.
D. The court cannot pierce the corporate veil because veil-piercing requires proof of conduct punishable as a criminal offence, and no crime has been committed.

**Q4. (MCQ)**
Doris is a director of Summit Retail Ltd ('Summit'), which operates a chain of gift shops. The landlord of a prime shop premises approaches Doris in her capacity as a director and offers Summit a five-year lease at a below-market rent. The Summit board genuinely declines the offer because the company lacks the funds to refurbish the premises. Doris then forms her own company, takes up the lease through it, and earns substantial profits. Doris acted honestly throughout, believed Summit could not have taken up the lease, and never disclosed her actions to the members in general meeting. Summit's new management discovers the facts and sues Doris. Which statement is CORRECT?

A. Doris is not liable because Summit could not itself have taken up the lease, so Summit suffered no loss.
B. Doris is not liable because she honestly believed that taking the lease was not harmful to Summit.
C. Doris is liable to account to Summit for the profits because the opportunity came to her by reason of her position as a director, and good faith and the company's inability to take the opportunity are irrelevant.
D. Doris is liable only for breach of the statutory duty of care, skill and diligence under s.465 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), and Summit may recover damages for its loss.

**Q5. (MCQ)**
Marcus holds 20% of the shares of Nova Components Ltd ('Nova'), a purely commercial electronics distributor with twelve unrelated shareholders. Marcus is also employed by Nova as its warehouse manager, but he has never been a director and there has never been any understanding that he would participate in management. Following a dispute with the majority shareholders, Nova summarily dismisses Marcus from his employment, cutting off his salary. His shares continue to carry full voting rights, dividends are declared as usual, and the value of his shares is unaffected. Marcus petitions the court under s.724 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), complaining that the dismissal is unfairly prejudicial to his interests. Which statement BEST reflects the likely outcome?

A. The petition is likely to fail because the dismissal prejudices Marcus in his capacity as an employee, not in his capacity as a member of Nova.
B. The petition is likely to succeed because any wrongful exclusion of a person associated with the company from its operations is automatically unfairly prejudicial.
C. The petition is likely to fail because unfair prejudice relief is only available where the petitioner holds at least 25% of the voting shares.
D. Marcus must instead bring a statutory derivative action under ss.732–733 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), because dismissal of an employee is misconduct committed against the company.
