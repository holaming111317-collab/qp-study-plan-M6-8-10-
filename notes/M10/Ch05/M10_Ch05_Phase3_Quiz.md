**Question 3 (MCQ — scenario)**

In relation to agency law: Kelvin is the purchasing supervisor of TeaHarvest Trading Limited ("THT"), a tea importer. His written authority is limited to purchasing tea leaves. At a trade fair, Kelvin signed a contract with PorcelainCo to buy 200 boxes of ceramic teapots for THT at a very favourable price, without seeking any approval. PorcelainCo knew Kelvin was THT's purchasing supervisor but had never dealt with THT before. When the teapots arrived, THT's managing director examined the goods, was told the full details of the contract, decided the teapots were a bargain, and sold half of them to a retailer at a profit. PorcelainCo has not been paid.

Which of the following is the **best** analysis of THT's liability to PorcelainCo?

A. THT is bound by agency by estoppel, because it held Kelvin out as its purchasing supervisor to the trading community
B. THT is bound by the contract because it ratified Kelvin's unauthorised act with full knowledge of all material facts, and Kelvin's personal liability is absolved
C. THT is not bound, because Kelvin had neither actual nor apparent authority to buy teapots, so PorcelainCo's only remedy is against Kelvin for breach of warranty of authority
D. THT is bound by agency by necessity, because Kelvin acted in THT's commercial interest

**Question 4 (MCQ — scenario)**

In relation to agency law: Gloria was the showroom manager of BathLux Sanitaryware Limited ("BathLux") for five years. She regularly ordered tiles and fittings on BathLux's account from TileMart Supplies Limited ("TileMart"), and BathLux always settled the invoices. Gloria resigned on 1 March after a dispute with the board. BathLux did not inform TileMart or any other supplier of her departure. On 10 March, Gloria visited TileMart, ordered HK$80,000 of designer tiles on BathLux's account, collected them in a rented van, and disappeared. TileMart now demands payment from BathLux.

Which of the following is the **best** analysis?

A. BathLux is not bound, because Gloria's actual authority ended on 1 March when her employment terminated
B. BathLux is bound only if it chooses to ratify the purchase, and it may freely refuse to ratify
C. BathLux is bound, because Gloria had apparent authority: BathLux's conduct in past dealings represented to TileMart that she had authority, and TileMart relied on that representation to its detriment
D. TileMart's only remedy is to sue Gloria for breach of warranty of authority, because an agent whose authority has ended can never bind the former principal

**Question 5 (MCQ — scenario)**

In relation to agency law: Alice, Ben and Carmen decided to set up a coffee-roasting business. Two weeks before Peak Brew Coffee Limited ("PBC") was incorporated, Alice signed a two-year coffee bean supply contract with Andes Beans Limited ("Andes"), signing "for and on behalf of Peak Brew Coffee Limited (a company to be incorporated)". PBC was duly incorporated a fortnight later. When Andes delivered the first shipment of beans, the newly appointed board of PBC refused to accept delivery or pay, arguing that PBC had never authorised the contract and had never adopted it after incorporation. Andes has sued PBC for breach of contract.

Which of the following is the **best** analysis?

A. PBC is bound, because it ratified the contract simply by being incorporated and carrying on the coffee-roasting business contemplated by the contract
B. PBC is not bound: a principal that did not exist at the time of the transaction cannot ratify it, so the contract cannot be enforced against PBC; Andes should pursue Alice personally
C. PBC is bound by agency by necessity, because Alice had to secure the bean supply urgently before incorporation
D. PBC is bound because Alice had apparent authority as a promoter, and Andes relied on her representation to its detriment
