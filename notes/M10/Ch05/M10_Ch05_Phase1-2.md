# M10 Chapter 5: Aspects of Agency Law — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

## Phase 1 速查表

### Learning Objectives
1. Analyse the essential elements of an agency relationship and the four ways an agency can be created (by written agreement, by estoppel, by ratification, by necessity).
2. Distinguish between actual authority (express and implied), usual authority and apparent authority, and apply them to third-party scenarios.
3. Explain the fiduciary duties, rights and liabilities of an agent and the corresponding duties and rights of a principal.
4. Apply the disclosed principal and undisclosed principal rules to determine who may sue and be sued on the contract.
5. Advise on an agent's personal liability, including breach of warranty of authority, with statutory and case authority.

#### B. Leading cases（full citation 以 LP 所引為準）

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park Property Ltd (1964) | Agency by estoppel / ostensible (apparent) authority: the company held out director K as MD by paying past contracts he signed; the company was bound. |
| Kelner v Baxter (1866) | Ratification impossible where the principal did not exist at the time of the contract (pre-incorporation); promoters personally liable. |
| Gimex Development Ltd v Cua Wai Tai & Another (2000) | Ratification by acquiescence: principal's failure to object to appointment of a sub-agent amounted to ratification. |
| Great Northern Railway v Swaffield (1874) | Agency by necessity — three conditions: (1) agent entrusted with principal's property; (2) emergency and principal could not be contacted; (3) agent acted bona fide in the principal's interest. |
| Ireland v Livingston (1872) | Where a written order to an agent is ambiguous (two possible meanings) and the agent honestly acts on one, the principal cannot repudiate the act as unauthorised. |
| Wong Wai Hing & Another v Hui Wei Lee (2001) | Where the agent (debt collector) was expressly authorised and the agreement did not restrict the class of acts, the principal was liable for the agent's acts (even criminal acts in the course of the authorised task). |
| Hely-Hutchinson v Brayhead Ltd (1968) | Implied actual authority: board's consent/acquiescence in a chairman acting as MD over a long period gave him implied actual authority to bind the company. |
| Panorama Developments (Guildford) Ltd v Fidelis Furnishing Fabrics (1971) | Apparent authority by holding out: a company secretary had apparent authority to hire cars on the company's credit; the company had to pay. |
| Chaudhry v Prabhakar (1988) | Agent's duty of care and skill: even an unpaid agent friend with car knowledge breached the duty by recommending a car he should have known was accident-damaged. |
| Boardman v Phipps (1966) | Fiduciary may not earn secret profit or allow conflict between personal interest and fiduciary duty; liable to account for the profit. |
| Cook v Deeks (1916) | Directors must account to the company for secret profit; a self-serving resolution ratifying their own act was ineffective. |
| Regal Hastings Ltd v Gulliver (1942) (also cited in LP exam practice as Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver [1967] 2 AC 134) | Directors/agents must not make unauthorised profit from their position; must account for the profit to the principal. |
| Collen v Wright (1857) | Breach of warranty of authority: agent who wrongly believed he had authority was liable in damages to the third party. |
| Watteau v Fenwick (1893) | Undisclosed principal: once the principal is established, ordinary agency rules apply; the principal is liable for the agent's acts even though the third party did not know of the agency. |

#### C. IRAC application points

**1. Formation of agency（4 ways — Issue: "how was the agency created?"）**
- **By written agreement**: express appointment (written or verbal), agent acts for remuneration/commission.
- **By estoppel (holding out)**: principal, by words or conduct, represents to a third party that the agent has authority; the third party relies on it in good faith and incurs loss. Key: the representation must come from the **principal** (or someone authorised by the principal), **not from the agent himself**.
- **By ratification**: agent acts without/exceeding authority; principal later approves with full knowledge. Four conditions:
  1. The principal must exist at the time of the transaction and the agent must have been expressly contracted as an agent.
  2. The principal must have been made aware of **all material facts** of the transaction.
  3. The principal must ratify the **whole** transaction, not part of it.
  4. The principal must ratify within a **reasonable time**.
  Effect: contract binding on principal and third party; agent's liability absolved.
- **By necessity**: urgent circumstances; authority presumed to protect the principal's property (three conditions in *Great Northern Railway v Swaffield* (1874)).

**2. Types of authority（Issue: "was the principal bound?"）**
- **Express actual authority**: expressly agreed acts; scope derived from the contract.
- **Implied actual authority**: implied from the relationship of the parties or from the agent's position (e.g. law firm charging disbursements; *Hely-Hutchinson v Brayhead Ltd* (1968)).
- **Usual authority**: a type of implied actual authority that comes with the position the agent occupies in a trade/business/profession; may exceed actual authority to get the work done.
- **Apparent (ostensible) authority** — three requirements:
  1. The principal (or someone authorised) represented to the third party that the agent had authority — by words, conduct, or previous dealings.
  2. The third party relied on the representation.
  3. The third party relied on it to his/her detriment.
  Note: apparent authority can survive the end of actual authority if the principal does not notify third parties (LP analysis of the ex-MD ordering wine after leaving).

**3. Duties of agent（fiduciary in nature）**
1. Follow the principal's instructions (breach = breach of agency; damages, e.g. the price difference).
2. Exercise duty of care and skill (*Chaudhry v Prabhakar* (1988)).
3. Avoid conflict of interest — even if the principal benefits from the transaction (*Boardman v Phipps* (1966)).
4. Avoid secret profit — no benefit without the principal's consent (*Regal Hastings Ltd v Gulliver* (1942)); possible POBO (Cap. 201) s.9 offence.
5. Account for transactions — account for all money received and keep proper records (*Cook v Deeks* (1916)).

**4. Rights of agent / Duties of principal（mirror image）**
- Agent's rights: remuneration (only when the transaction is **complete**; merely introducing a purchaser is not enough), indemnity for reasonable expenses/losses (not for unauthorised or negligent acts), and **lien** over the principal's goods/documents lawfully in the agent's possession until outstanding amounts are settled.
- Principal's duties: pay remuneration (agreed or customary), pay indemnity (except unauthorised actions or agent's default/negligence).

**5. Liabilities of agent & termination consequences**
- Personal liability: (1) acting for a **non-existing principal** (*Kelner v Baxter* (1866)); (2) acting **in excess of authority** (if not ratified → breach of warranty of authority); (3) acting for an **undisclosed principal**; (4) committing a **tortious act outside authority**.
- **Breach of warranty of authority** (*Collen v Wright* (1857)): agent represents he/she has authority but has none; third party relies and suffers loss; remedy = damages (actual loss). Agent's honest but mistaken belief is no defence.
- Principal's rights on breach: sue for breach of agency (agency terminated, damages per contract law) and recover any secret profit.
- **Disclosed principal** (named or unnamed): agent drops out; only principal and third party can sue/be sued (privity of contract).
- **Undisclosed principal** (*Watteau v Fenwick* (1893)): agent may sue and be sued; the third party, on discovering the principal, may elect to sue the principal instead; the undisclosed principal may intervene and sue the third party directly, whereupon the agent loses his/her right of action.

### 易混淆概念對比

**Table 1 — Ratification vs Agency by Estoppel / Apparent Authority（Jun 2024 考生大伏位：ratification 誤答為 estoppel）**

| 分辨點 | Ratification | Agency by Estoppel / Apparent Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Time of the key act | Agent acts **without authority first**; principal **approves afterwards** | Principal's **representation comes first** (words/conduct/past dealings); agent then acts within that appearance of authority |
| Who makes the representation? | No prior representation needed — the principal simply adopts the act later | The **principal** (not the agent) must have held the agent out to the third party |
| Third party's reliance | Not required; ratification works even if the third party knew the agent had no authority | Third party must **rely on the representation to his/her detriment** |
| Principal's existence | Principal must **exist at the time of the transaction** (*Kelner v Baxter* (1866)) | Principal exists and makes the representation before the contract |
| Leading case | *Kelner v Baxter* (1866); *Gimex Development Ltd v Cua Wai Tai* (2000) | *Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park Property Ltd* (1964); *Panorama Developments* (1971) |
| 考試伏位（繁中） | 見到「agent 越權 → principal 事後知情認可」就要答 ratification，唔好因為有「principal 嘅行為」就衝去答 estoppel | 見到「principal 一早通知咗 supplier 呢個人係 MD/經理」先係 holding out；如果 representation 只係來自 agent 自己，estoppel 唔成立 |

**Table 2 — Disclosed Principal vs Undisclosed Principal（Dec 2024 考生完全唔識 undisclosed principal）**

| 分辨點 | Disclosed Principal | Undisclosed Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Third party's knowledge | Knows the agent acts for a principal (name disclosed **or** existence disclosed without name) | No actual notice of the principal's existence; agent appears to be the principal |
| Who can sue/be sued on the contract? | Only principal and third party (privity of contract); agent drops out | Agent may sue and be sued; third party may **elect** to sue the principal instead after discovering him/her |
| Principal's right to intervene | Not needed — principal is already a party | Undisclosed principal may intervene and sue the third party; the agent then **loses** his/her right of action |
| Authority analysis | Actual/apparent authority in the normal way | Ordinary agency rules still apply once the principal is established (*Watteau v Fenwick* (1893)) |
| 考試伏位（繁中） | 「disclosed」唔一定要講出名——講咗「我係代人簽」但冇講名都係 disclosed | 第三人發現 principal 之後係二揀一（agent 或 principal），唔係兩個都告埋；principal 介入後 agent 就冇得再告 third party |

## Phase 2 閃卡

### Card 1
**Front:** What are the four ways in which an agency relationship can be created?

**Back:**
- By written agreement (express appointment, written or verbal)
- By estoppel (agency by holding out)
- By ratification (principal's subsequent approval of an unauthorised act)
- By necessity (urgent circumstances, no prior agreement needed)

💬 記口訣「W-E-R-N」：Written、Estoppel、Ratification、Necessity。考試通常第一句就問 agency 點形成，呢四個係必背框架。

### Card 2
**Front:** What are the four conditions for an effective ratification, and which case shows what happens if the principal did not exist?

**Back:**
- The principal must exist at the time of the transaction and the agent must have been expressly contracted as an agent
- The principal must be aware of all material facts of the transaction
- The principal must ratify the whole transaction, not part of it
- The principal must ratify within a reasonable time
- *Kelner v Baxter* (1866): pre-incorporation contract cannot be ratified; promoters personally liable

💬 四個條件：存在、知情、全盤、及時。公司未註冊就「代公司」簽約，ratify 唔到，promoters 自己孭。

### Card 3
**Front:** When does agency by estoppel arise, and whose representation counts?

**Back:**
- The principal holds out a person as agent to a third party, by words or conduct, as having authority to contract on the principal's behalf
- The representation must come from the **principal** (or someone authorised by the principal) — a representation made by the agent himself is not enough
- The third party must rely on it in good faith and incur a loss
- The principal is then prevented (estopped) from denying the agency
- *Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park Property Ltd* (1964)

💬 重點係「holding out 要由 principal 做」。Agent 自己話自己有權係冇用嘅——呢個係考試最常見嘅陷阱位。

### Card 4
**Front:** What are the three conditions for agency by necessity under *Great Northern Railway v Swaffield* (1874)?

**Back:**
- The agent must have been entrusted with the principal's property
- There was an emergency and the principal could not be contacted
- The agent acted bona fide in the interest of the principal

💬 三個條件：管有財產、緊急兼聯絡唔到、真誠為 principal 行事。例子：凍肉車壞雪櫃，司機自己買冰保住啲貨，可以向 principal 攞返啲使費。

### Card 5
**Front:** What are the three requirements to establish apparent (ostensible) authority?

**Back:**
- The principal (or someone authorised) represented to the third party that the agent had authority — by words, conduct, or previous dealings
- The third party relied on the representation
- The third party relied on it to his/her detriment
- *Panorama Developments (Guildford) Ltd v Fidelis Furnishing Fabrics* (1971): company secretary had apparent authority to hire cars

💬 Apparent authority 係「third party 眼中所見嘅權限」。Employee 離職後如果 principal 冇通知 third party，apparent authority 可能仍然存在，principal 照樣被綁住。

### Card 6
**Front:** List the five duties an agent owes to the principal, with one supporting case for the fiduciary duties.

**Back:**
- To follow the principal's instructions
- To exercise duty of care and skill (*Chaudhry v Prabhakar* (1988))
- To avoid conflict of interest (*Boardman v Phipps* (1966))
- To avoid secret profit (*Regal Hastings Ltd v Gulliver* (1942))
- To account for transactions (*Cook v Deeks* (1916))

💬 Fiduciary duties 係 strict obligation：就算 principal 喺交易中有著數，只要 agent 有利益衝突或者收咗 secret profit 都係 breach，利潤要全數交出。

### Card 7
**Front:** What must a third party prove to sue an agent for breach of warranty of authority, and what is the remedy?

**Back:**
- The agent represented/warranted to the third party that he/she had authority to act for the principal, but in fact had none
- The third party relied on that representation and incurred a loss
- Remedy: damages equal to the actual loss suffered
- *Collen v Wright* (1857): agent liable even though he honestly (but wrongly) believed he had authority

💬 唔使證明 agent 存心呃人——honest mistake 都照賠。所以呢個係 third party 告唔到 principal 嗰陣嘅後備武器。

### Card 8
**Front:** In an undisclosed principal situation, who may sue and be sued on the contract?

**Back:**
- The agent may sue and be sued on the contract (the third party thought he/she was dealing with the agent as principal)
- On discovering the principal, the third party may elect to sue the principal instead
- The undisclosed principal may intervene and sue the third party directly; the agent then loses his/her right of action
- *Watteau v Fenwick* (1893)

💬 Undisclosed = third party 完全唔知有 principal。佢發現之後可以「二揀一」；principal 都可以主動跳出嚟告 third party，一跳 agent 嘅告人權就冇咗。
