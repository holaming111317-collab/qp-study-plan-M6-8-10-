# M10 Chapter 2: Principles of Contract and Consumer Law in Hong Kong (Part 1) — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

## Phase 1 速查表

### Learning Objectives
1. Analyse the essential elements in the formation of a contract (offer, acceptance, capacity, intention to create legal relations, consideration) and apply them to practical business scenarios.
2. Distinguish offers from invitations to treat, and apply the communication rules of acceptance (including the postal rule and its exceptions).
3. Evaluate the enforceability of promises through the doctrine of consideration and promissory estoppel.
4. Apply the rules on express and implied terms, and assess the validity of exemption clauses under the Control of Exemption Clauses Ordinance (Cap.71) and related statutes.
5. Advise on third-party rights under the Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Ordinance (Cap.623) and on unconscionable consumer contracts under the Unconscionable Contracts Ordinance (Cap.458).

#### B. Leading cases（full citations，按主題分組）

**Offer vs invitation to treat / unilateral contracts**
- Harvey v Facey (1893) AC 552 — a statement of the lowest price is a mere indication of price, not an offer.
- Clifton v Palumbo (1944) 2 All ER 497 — vague statement 'I am prepared to offer you my estate…' not sufficiently definite, especially for land.
- Partridge v Crittenden (1968) 1 WLR 1204 — advertisements are generally invitations to treat ('limited stock' rationale).
- Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co (1893) 1 QB 256 — reward advertisement = unilateral offer to the world; performing the stipulated act = acceptance; £1,000 bank deposit showed intention to be bound; inconvenience of use = consideration.
- Pharmaceutical Society of Great Britain v Boots Cash Chemists (Southern) Ltd (1953) 1 QB 401 — goods on self-service shelves are invitations to treat; contract forms at the check-out.
- Fisher v Bell (1961) 1 QB 394 — shop-window display with price tag is an invitation to treat.
- Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd (1971) 2 QB 163 — automatic/vending machines are standing offers (no negotiation possible); contract forms when money is inserted, before the ticket issues.
- Harvela Investments Ltd v Royal Trust Co of Canada (CI) Ltd (1986) AC 207 — an invitation to tender promising to accept the most competitive bid is binding; referential bids impliedly forbidden.

**Termination of offers**
- Byrne & Co v Van Tienhoven (1880) 5 CPD 344 — revocation is effective only when communicated to the offeree before acceptance.
- Shuey v US (1875) 92 US 73 — a reward offer to the public can be revoked via the same medium.
- Routledge v Grant (1828) 4 Bing 653 — an offer can be revoked before acceptance unless an option contract (supported by consideration) keeps it open.
- Ramsgate Victoria Hotel Co Ltd v Montefiore (1866) LR 1 Ex 109 — an offer lapses after a reasonable time.

**Acceptance**
- Hyde v Wrench (1840) 3 Beav 334 — a counter-offer destroys the original offer (mirror-image rule).
- Stevenson, Jacques & Co v McLean (1880) 5 QBD 346 — a mere request for information does not destroy the offer.
- Butler Machine Tool Co Ltd v Ex-Cell-O Corp (England) Ltd (1979) 1 WLR 401 — battle of forms: the 'last shot' wins; sellers accepted the buyers' counter-offer by returning the signed acknowledgment slip.
- Adams v Lindsell (1818) 1 B & Ald 681 — postal rule: posted acceptance is effective on posting.
- Holwell Securities Ltd v Hughes (1974) 1 WLR 155 — general rule: acceptance must be communicated; postal rule excluded where receipt is expressly/impliedly required.
- Household Fire and Carriage Accident Insurance Co Ltd v Grant (1879) 4 Ex D 216 — postal acceptance effective even if the letter is lost.
- R v Clarke (1927) 40 CLR 227 — a person cannot accept an offer in ignorance of it.

**Capacity / intention**
- Proform Sports Management Ltd v Proactive Sports Management Ltd (2006) EWHC 2903 — a minor's contract (Wayne Rooney's agency contract) was voidable, not a contract for necessaries/apprenticeship.
- Hart v O'Connor (1985) AC 1000 — mental incapacity makes a contract voidable only if the other party knew of it; otherwise the incapacity is ignored.
- Balfour v Balfour (1919) 2 KB 571 — domestic agreements presumed not legally binding.
- Jones v Padavatton (1969) 1 WLR 328 — mother–daughter agreement: presumption not rebutted.
- Wu Chiu Kuen v Chu Shui Ching (1992) HKCU 29 — HK example where the domestic/social presumption WAS rebutted.
- World Food Fair Ltd v Hong Kong Island Development Ltd (2007) 1 HKLRD 498 — 'subject to contract' rebuts (though not conclusively) the commercial presumption.

**Consideration**
- Currie v Misa (1875) LR 10 Ex 153 — classic definition: some right/interest/profit/benefit to one party, or forbearance/detriment/loss/responsibility of the other.
- Tweddle v Atkinson (1861) 1 B & S 393 — consideration must move from the promisee; also an authority for privity.
- Chappell & Co Ltd v Nestle Co Ltd (1960) AC 87 — consideration must be sufficient (some value, even chocolate wrappers) but need not be adequate.
- Roscorla v Thomas (1842) 3 QB 234 — past consideration is no consideration.
- Pao On v Lau Yiu Long (1980) AC 614 — three requirements for the 'implied assumpsit' exception (act at promisor's request; understanding of remuneration; enforceability if promised in advance); also: performance of a duty owed to a third party is good consideration.
- Lampleigh v Brathwait (1615) Hob 105 — request exception to past consideration.
- Collins v Godefroy (1831) 1 B & Ad 950 — performance of an existing duty imposed by law is no consideration.
- Glasbrook Brothers Ltd v Glamorgan County Council (1925) AC 270 — doing MORE than a legal duty is good consideration.
- Scotson v Pegg (1861) 6 Hurl & N 295 — performance of an existing duty owed to a third party is good consideration.
- Stilk v Myrick (1809) 2 Camp 317 — performance of an existing duty owed to the promisor is no consideration (deserting sailors).
- Hartley v Ponsonby (1857) 7 E & B 872 — contrast: voyage became so dangerous that remaining crew did more than their existing duty.
- Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd (1991) 1 QB 1 — 'practical benefit' exception for promises to pay more (six strict criteria; no economic duress/fraud).
- UBC (Construction) Ltd v Sung Foo Kee Ltd (1993) 2 HKLR 207 — HK application of Williams v Roffey.
- Pinnel's Case (1602) 5 Co Rep 117a — part-payment of a debt is no consideration for discharging the whole debt; needs fresh consideration (e.g. horse, hawk, robe, or earlier payment).
- Foakes v Beer (1884) 9 App Cas 605 — House of Lords confirmed Pinnel's Case; Re Selectmove Ltd (1995) 1 WLR 474 — practical-benefit analysis does NOT apply to promises to accept less (stare decisis).

**Promissory estoppel**
- Central London Property Ltd v High Trees House Ltd (1947) KB 130 — the foundational case; estoppel suspended (not extinguished) the landlord's right to full rent for 1940–1945.
- Combe v Combe (1951) 2 KB 215 — estoppel is a shield, not a sword; consideration remains 'a cardinal necessity'.
- Tool Metal Manufacturing Co Ltd v Tungsten Electric Co Ltd (1955) 1 WLR 761 — promisor may withdraw the promise on reasonable notice (suspensory effect).
- Collier v P & MJ Wright (Holdings) Ltd (2008) 1 WLR 643 — estoppel may extinguish the balance where creditor voluntarily accepted part-payment and debtor relied; NOT yet followed in HK.
- Luo Xing Juan v Estate of Hui Shui See (2009) 12 HKCFAR 1 — HK CFA on the inequitability element.

**Mistake / privity**
- Smith v Hughes (1871) LR 6 QB 597 — objective approach; mistake as to quality/collateral matter ≠ mistake as to terms; caveat emptor.
- Hartog v Colin and Shields (1939) 3 All ER 566 — if one party knows the other is mistaken as to a term, the contract cannot be enforced against the mistaken party.
- Cundy v Lindsay (1878) 3 App Cas 459 — written contract with a rogue impersonating a KNOWN, identifiable firm: void ab initio for mistake as to identity.
- King's Norton Metal Co v Edridge Merrett & Co Ltd (1897) 14 TLR 98 — no known existing entity impersonated: mistake only as to attributes (creditworthiness); contract not void.
- Ingram v Little (1961) 1 QB 31 — face-to-face presumption rebutted where identity was essential to the contract (not just payment); of questionable authority.
- Lewis v Avery (1972) 1 QB 198 — face-to-face presumption NOT rebutted; mistake as to attribute only.
- Shogun Finance Ltd v Hudson (2004) 1 AC 919 — the face-to-face presumption is strong; rebutted only in rare cases (e.g. impersonating a family member/personal acquaintance).
- Saunders v Anglia Building Society (sub nom Gallie v Lee) (1971) AC 1004 — non est factum: document must be essentially different in substance or kind; signer must also have exercised reasonable care.
- Lewis v Clay (1897) 67 LJ QB 224 — non est factum succeeded: signer mistaken as to the CAPACITY in which he signed (thought he was witnessing).
- L'Estrange v E Graucob Ltd (1934) 2 KB 394 — a signature binds the signer to unread terms.
- Dunlop Pneumatic Tyre Co Ltd v Selfridge & Co Ltd (1915) AC 847 — privity: only a party to the contract can sue on it.
- Beswick v Beswick (1968) AC 58 — widow could not sue personally (no privity) but succeeded as administratrix of the estate.
- Shanklin Pier Ltd v Detel Products Ltd (1951) 2 KB 854 — collateral contract as a common law circumvention of privity.

**Terms / exemption clauses**
- Chan Yeuk Yu v Church Body of the Hong Kong Sheng Kung Hui (2001) 1 HKC 621 — 'regal surroundings for the select few' = mere puff (HK).
- Heilbut Symons & Co v Buckleton (1913) AC 30 — term vs representation determined objectively by intention; guiding principles are rebuttable.
- Dick Bentley Productions Ltd v Harold Smith (Motors) Ltd (1965) 1 WLR 623 — statement by a party with specialist knowledge (car dealer on mileage) = term.
- Oscar Chess v Williams (1957) 1 WLR 370 — contrast: private seller without specialist knowledge = representation.
- Bannerman v White (1861) 10 CB NS 844 — statement of crucial importance to the buyer (sulphur-free hops) = term.
- Curtis v Chemical Cleaning and Dyeing Co (1951) 1 KB 805 — misrepresentation of the extent of an exemption clause defeats reliance on it, despite signature.
- Parker v South Eastern Railway Co (1876–1877) LR 2 CPD 416 — reasonable notice test for unsigned documents.
- Chapelton v Barry Urban District Council (1940) 1 KB 532 — a mere receipt/voucher has no contractual status.
- Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd (1949) 1 KB 532 — notice after formation (hotel bedroom notice) is too late.
- Interfoto Picture Library Ltd v Stiletto Visual Programmes Ltd (1989) QB 433 — particularly onerous/unusual terms need a HIGHER degree of notice.
- McCutcheon v David MacBrayne Ltd (1964) 1 WLR 125 — course of dealings requires CONSISTENT past dealings on the same terms.
- The Moorcock (1889) 14 PD 64 — business efficacy test (implied in fact).
- Shirlaw v Southern Foundries (1926) Ltd (1939) 2 KB 206 — officious bystander test ('Oh, of course!').
- Marks & Spencer Plc v BNP Paribas Securities Services Trust Co (Jersey) Ltd (2015) UKSC 72 — implied terms: reasonableness alone is not sufficient.
- Kensland Realty Ltd v Whale View Investment Ltd (2001) 4 HKCFAR 381 — HK CFA: a party cannot take advantage of its own breach of an implied term.
- Liverpool City Council v Irwin (1977) AC 239 — term implied in law as essential to landlord–tenant relations.
- R & B Customs Brokers Co Ltd v United Dominions Trust Ltd (1988) 1 WLR 321 — a company can 'deal as a consumer' if the transaction is not integral to its business and not regularly entered into.
- George Mitchell (Chesterhall) Ltd v Finney Lock Seeds Ltd (1983) 2 AC 803 — reasonableness is fact-dependent; appellate courts interfere only if plainly and obviously wrong.
- Shum Kit Ching v Caesar Beauty Centre Ltd (2003) 3 HKLRD 422 — HK case on the UCO: 'no refund' clause unenforceable; pre-payment not a forfeitable deposit.

#### C. IRAC application points（落 scenario 嘅分析框架）

1. **Offer vs invitation to treat** — Issue: is the communication an offer or an ITT? Rule: definitions + established categories (advertisements/shop displays/websites/tenders/auctions). Application: check the exact wording ('will go to the first 500 replies' → unilateral offer; 'for sale' price tag → ITT); objective intention from the language. Conclusion: state who makes the offer and when the contract forms (e.g. at check-out — Boots).
2. **Counter-offer vs request for information** — Rule: mirror-image rule (Hyde v Wrench); even a slight variation destroys the offer; asking about flexibility is not a counter-offer (Stevenson v McLean). Application: compare the reply word-by-word with the offer; in battle-of-forms scenarios, identify the 'last shot' and any acceptance by conduct (Butler Machine Tool).
3. **Revocation** — Rule: effective only when communicated before acceptance (Byrne v Van Tienhoven); for unilateral offers to the world, revocation via the same medium suffices (Shuey v US). Application: timeline analysis — date of offer, posting of acceptance, receipt, revocation; check whether the postal rule is excluded ('replies received').
4. **Capacity** — Rule: minors void subject to necessaries (s.4(2) SOGO) / beneficial employment / voidable contracts (Cap.410 s.2(1)); mental incapacity voidable only if the other party knew (Hart v O'Connor). Application: classify the contract first (necessaries? employment? interest in land/shares/partnership?).
5. **Intention to create legal relations** — Rule: domestic/social → presumed not binding (Balfour v Balfour); commercial → strong presumption of binding, rebuttable e.g. by 'subject to contract'. Application: identify context, then look for rebutting evidence (detrimental reliance, bank deposit as in Carlill).
6. **Consideration** — Rule checklist: moved from promisee? sufficient (not adequate)? past (Roscorla v Thomas; Pao On exception)? existing duty (which type — by law / owed to third party / owed to promisor)? part-payment (Pinnel/Foakes; exceptions: composition agreements, third-party payment)? Application: map the promise to one of the categories before concluding enforceability; do not confuse with privity or promissory estoppel.
7. **Promissory estoppel** — Rule: (1) clear promise not to insist on strict rights; (2) reliance; (3) inequitable to renege; suspensory only; shield not sword (High Trees; Combe v Combe). Application: estoppel can only DEFEND; if the promisee is the plaintiff claiming extra payment, estoppel fails — look to Williams v Roffey consideration instead.
8. **Exemption clauses** — Three-step analysis: (1) incorporation (signature — L'Estrange, subject to misrepresentation — Curtis; reasonable notice before/at formation — Parker, Olley, Thornton; higher notice for onerous terms — Interfoto; consistent course of dealings — McCutcheon); (2) construction — does the wording cover the liability (contra proferentem)?; (3) statutory validity — CECO: death/PI negligence can never be excluded (s.7(1)); other negligence loss needs reasonableness (s.7(2)); consumer/standard-terms breach liability needs reasonableness (s.8); SOGO implied undertakings in consumer contracts cannot be excluded (s.11(2)); reasonableness judged at formation, burden on the party relying (s.3(1), Sch.2).
9. **Third-party rights** — Rule: privity burden/benefit rules (Dunlop v Selfridge); CRTPO s.4 two tests + s.4(2) identification + s.4(3) rebuttal. Application: check first whether the contract expressly excludes CRTPO ('A person who is not a party… shall have no right…'), then whether the third party is identified by name/class/description.
10. **UCO (Cap.458)** — Rule: consumer goods/services only; unconscionability is a HIGH threshold (higher than 'unfairness'); court's three powers under s.5(1). Application: raise UCO only after CECO/MO/SSITO fail; expect rare success (LRC: 'extreme cases, very rare').

## Phase 2 閃卡

### Card 1
**Front:** What distinguishes an offer from an invitation to treat, and how are (a) newspaper advertisements, (b) shop-shelf displays and (c) reward advertisements classified?

**Back:**
- Offer = an expression of willingness to contract on specified terms without further negotiation; ITT = a statement indicating a willingness to receive offers.
- (a) Newspaper advertisements: generally ITTs (Partridge v Crittenden (1968) 1 WLR 1204) — the 'limited stock' rationale.
- (b) Shop-shelf displays: ITTs; the contract forms at check-out (Pharmaceutical Society of Great Britain v Boots Cash Chemists (Southern) Ltd (1953) 1 QB 401; Fisher v Bell (1961) 1 QB 394 for window displays).
- (c) Reward advertisements: offers in unilateral contracts — performance of the stipulated act is acceptance (Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co (1893) 1 QB 256).

💬 白話解釋：記住大原則——廣告、貨架陳列、網站基本上都係「請你出價」(ITT)，即係話客人才係 offeror。唯一大例外係懸賞廣告：因為做咗個動作已經係 acceptance，如果當佢 ITT，登廣告嘅人就可以免費享受成果，唔公平，所以 Carlill 案訂明呢類係 unilateral offer。

### Card 2
**Front:** State the mirror-image rule and its consequences, and distinguish a counter-offer from a request for information, with authority.

**Back:**
- Acceptance must be an unconditional assent to ALL terms of the offer — any variation, however minor, is a counter-offer (mirror-image rule; Jones v Daniel (1984) 2 Ch 332).
- A counter-offer destroys the original offer, which can no longer be accepted (Hyde v Wrench (1840) 3 Beav 334).
- A mere request for information (e.g. asking about flexibility on terms) does NOT destroy the offer (Stevenson, Jacques & Co v McLean (1880) 5 QBD 346).
- Battle of forms: the party whose correspondence was the 'last shot' wins; acceptance can occur by conduct, e.g. taking delivery (Butler Machine Tool Co Ltd v Ex-Cell-O Corp (England) Ltd (1979) 1 WLR 401).

💬 白話解釋：Acceptance 要一模一樣，改少少都當你開咗個新 offer，原本嗰個即刻冧咗。但「問吓價」唔算還價——關鍵係個回覆係咪一個可以即時被接受嘅確定提議。商業上雙方用自己 standard terms 你來我往，邊個「最後一槍」就用邊個嘅條款。

### Card 3
**Front:** When is a revocation of an offer effective, and how can an offer made to the public be revoked? What is the postal rule and its limits?

**Back:**
- Revocation is effective only when communicated to the offeree BEFORE acceptance (Byrne & Co v Van Tienhoven (1880) 5 CPD 344); an option contract (consideration given to keep the offer open) prevents revocation (Routledge v Grant (1828) 4 Bing 653).
- A public/unilateral offer can be revoked through the same medium, e.g. the same newspaper (Shuey v US (1875) 92 US 73).
- Postal rule: a posted acceptance is effective on posting, even if lost or delayed (Adams v Lindsell (1818) 1 B & Ald 681; Household Fire and Carriage Accident Insurance Co Ltd v Grant (1879) 4 Ex D 216).
- Limits: applies only to acceptances (not offers/revocations); only where post is a reasonable mode; excluded where the offeror requires receipt, expressly or impliedly (Holwell Securities Ltd v Hughes (1974) 1 WLR 155); not applicable if the letter was wrongly addressed by the offeree.

💬 白話解釋：撤銷 offer 要對方收到先算數，而且必須喺 acceptance 之前。郵寄規則係特例：acceptance 一放入郵筒就生效——但只限 acceptance，offer 同 revocation 唔適用；如果 offer 話明「收到為準」，郵寄規則就被排除。

### Card 4
**Front:** What are the rules on minors' capacity to contract in Hong Kong, and how does mental incapacity affect a contract?

**Back:**
- Under s.2(1) of the Age of Majority (Related Provisions) Ordinance (Cap.410), a person under 18 generally lacks capacity; contracts with minors are void, subject to exceptions.
- Binding: contracts for necessaries (s.4(2) SOGO — goods suitable to condition in life and actual requirements) and beneficial employment/apprenticeship contracts.
- Voidable: contracts acquiring interests in land, shares, or partnerships — the minor may repudiate before 18 or within a reasonable time after; money paid is recoverable only on total failure of consideration (Steinberg v Scala (Leeds) Ltd (1923) 2 Ch 452; Proform Sports Management Ltd v Proactive Sports Management Ltd (2006) EWHC 2903).
- Mental incapacity: contract voidable only if, at formation, the person could not understand the nature of the transaction AND the other party knew of the incapacity (Hart v O'Connor (1985) AC 1000); bound by necessaries (s.4 SOGO).

💬 白話解釋：未成年合約三大類——必需品同有益僱傭有效；土地、股份、合夥係「可撤銷」，未成年一方可以話唔玩；其他一概無效。精神無行為能力就睇兩樣嘢：當時係咪真係唔明白，同埋對方知唔知——對方唔知就當你正常。

### Card 5
**Front:** State the rules on past consideration and its exception, with authority.

**Back:**
- Past consideration is no consideration: an act performed before the promise was not given in exchange for it (Roscorla v Thomas (1842) 3 QB 234; Re McArdle (1951) Ch 669).
- Exception ('implied assumpsit'): per Pao On v Lau Yiu Long (1980) AC 614 — (1) the act was done at the promisor's request; (2) the parties understood the act would be remunerated; (3) the promise would have been legally enforceable if made before the act.
- Classic illustration: Lampleigh v Brathwait (1615) Hob 105.
- Also: consideration must move from the promisee but need not move to the promisor; sufficient but need not be adequate (Chappell & Co Ltd v Nestle Co Ltd (1960) AC 87).

💬 白話解釋：「做完先講」唔計數——個動作唔係為咗換個承諾而做，就唔係 consideration。例外係：你按對方要求做、大家心知肚明有報酬、而報酬承諾本身合法——咁即使承諾喺行動之後先講，都可以強制執行。

### Card 6
**Front:** To what extent can performance of an existing obligation be good consideration?

**Back:**
- Duty imposed by law: NO consideration (Collins v Godefroy (1831) 1 B & Ad 950); but doing MORE than the legal duty is good consideration (Glasbrook Brothers Ltd v Glamorgan County Council (1925) AC 270; Ward v Byham (1956) 1 WLR 496).
- Duty owed to a third party: YES, good consideration (Scotson v Pegg (1861) 6 Hurl & N 295; New Zealand Shipping Co Ltd v AM Satterthwaite & Co Ltd (The Eurymedon) (1975) AC 154; Pao On v Lau Yiu Long (1980) AC 614).
- Duty owed to the promisor: NO consideration (Stilk v Myrick (1809) 2 Camp 317); exceptions — doing more than obliged (Hartley v Ponsonby (1857) 7 E & B 872) or promisor obtains a practical benefit under the six strict criteria, absent duress/fraud (Williams v Roffey Bros & Nicholls (Contractors) Ltd (1991) 1 QB 1; UBC (Construction) Ltd v Sung Foo Kee Ltd (1993) 2 HKLR 207).
- Part-payment of a debt: never consideration for discharging the whole debt (Pinnel's Case (1602) 5 Co Rep 117a; Foakes v Beer (1884) 9 App Cas 605); practical benefit does not apply (Re Selectmove Ltd (1995) 1 WLR 474).

💬 白話解釋：三步分類法——法定義務：唔得，超額先得；欠第三方嘅約定義務：得；欠承諾人自己嘅：唔得，除非做超咗或者對方有實際利益。借錢還少咗永遠唔算 consideration，呢條線千祈唔好用 Williams v Roffey 兜過去。

### Card 7
**Front:** What is promissory estoppel, what are its criteria, and what are its two key limitations?

**Back:**
- Definition: where a promisor promises not to enforce strict contractual rights and the promisee relies on it, the promisor may be prevented from reneging (Central London Property Ltd v High Trees House Ltd (1947) KB 130).
- Criteria: (1) a clear promise not to insist on existing rights; (2) reliance by the promisee (detriment not strictly necessary); (3) it would be inequitable to go back on the promise (Luo Xing Juan v Estate of Hui Shui See (2009) 12 HKCFAR 1).
- Limitation 1 — suspensory, not extinctive: rights are suspended, not extinguished; the promisor may resume them on reasonable notice (Tool Metal Manufacturing Co Ltd v Tungsten Electric Co Ltd (1955) 1 WLR 761). For instalment debts, rights accruing during the suspensory period may be extinguished.
- Limitation 2 — a shield, not a sword: it is a defence only, never a cause of action (Combe v Combe (1951) 2 KB 215).

💬 白話解釋：Estoppel 係平衡法上嘅「反口唔得」原則——你應承咗唔追，對方信咗你，你就要守信用。但記住兩點：佢只係暫停你嘅權利（可以合理通知後恢復），而且只可以做擋箭牌，唔可以拎嚟告人。

### Card 8
**Front:** Under the Control of Exemption Clauses Ordinance (Cap.71), which liabilities can never be excluded, which require reasonableness, and how is reasonableness assessed?

**Back:**
- Never excludable: liability for death or personal injury resulting from negligence (s.7(1) CECO); seller's implied undertakings as to title under s.14 SOGO (s.11(1) CECO); SOGO ss.15–17 liabilities as against a person dealing as a consumer (s.11(2) CECO); liability to a consumer under the SSITO (s.8(1) SSITO).
- Reasonableness required: other loss/damage from negligence (s.7(2) CECO); breach-of-contract liability against a consumer or on written standard terms (s.8 CECO); indemnity clauses against consumers (s.9(1)); SOGO ss.15–17 liabilities in B2B contracts (ss.11(3), 3(2)); misrepresentation exemption/non-reliance clauses (s.4 MO (Cap.284) + s.3(1) CECO).
- Reasonableness test (s.3(1) CECO): fair and reasonable to include, judged at the time the contract was MADE; burden on the party relying on the clause; Sch.2 guidelines (bargaining strength, inducement, knowledge of the term, practicability of compliance, special-order goods); appellate deference unless plainly and obviously wrong (George Mitchell (Chesterhall) Ltd v Finney Lock Seeds Ltd (1983) 2 AC 803).

💬 白話解釋：記住金字塔——頂層係「永遠唔可以豁免」：疏忽致死傷、賣方業權、消費者嘅 SOGO 貨品質素責任。中層係「過到 reasonableness test 先得」：其他疏忽損失、消費者/標準條款違約責任、misrepresentation 免責條款。合理性以簽約當刻衡量，舉證責任喺靠條款嗰方。
