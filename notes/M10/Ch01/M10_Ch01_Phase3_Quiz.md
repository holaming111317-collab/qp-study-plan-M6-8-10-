### Question 1 (MCQ)

Mandy runs a small florist shop. A corporate customer owes her **HK$68,000** for flower arrangements delivered under a service agreement. The customer refuses to pay. Mandy wants to recover the debt quickly and cheaply, and she does **not** wish to instruct a lawyer to represent her at the hearing.

Before which forum should Mandy commence her claim?

A. The Small Claims Tribunal
B. The District Court
C. The Court of First Instance of the High Court
D. The Labour Tribunal

### Question 2 (MCQ)

A hypothetical Public Transport (Conduct) Ordinance provides in s.5: *"No person **in the vicinity of** any railway station shall obstruct any member of the station staff engaged in crowd-control duty."*

Oscar was inside the concourse of a railway station (i.e. **within** the station premises, not merely near it) when he deliberately blocked a station supervisor who was directing passengers during an evacuation drill. Oscar is charged under s.5. His counsel argues that Oscar committed no offence because he was **in** the station, not **"in the vicinity of"** it.

If the court rejects counsel's argument and convicts Oscar, which rule of statutory interpretation has the court most likely applied?

A. The literal rule, because "in the vicinity of" clearly covers persons inside the station
B. The golden rule, because a literal reading would produce the absurd result that obstructing staff outside the station is an offence but doing so inside the station is not
C. The ejusdem generis rule, because "station" must be read in the same class as "vicinity"
D. The mischief rule, because the court must always consider the four questions in Heydon's Case before applying any other rule

### Question 3 (MCQ)

In 2019, the District Court decided *Chan v Lee* (a hypothetical case), holding that a particular contractual clause was enforceable. In 2023, the District Court decided *Wong v Cheung*, a different dispute raising the same point of law, and followed *Chan v Lee*. The losing party in *Wong v Cheung* appealed, and the **Court of Appeal** has now declared that the legal principle laid down in *Chan v Lee* was wrong and is no longer a binding authority.

In relation to the doctrine of precedent, what has the Court of Appeal done to the principle in *Chan v Lee*?

A. It has reversed the principle in *Chan v Lee*
B. It has distinguished the principle in *Chan v Lee*
C. It has overruled the principle in *Chan v Lee*
D. It has treated the principle in *Chan v Lee* as decided per incuriam

### Question 4 (MCQ)

The Food and Health Bureau has made the *Fruit Juice (Labelling) Regulation* under powers conferred by a parent ordinance. The parent ordinance says **nothing** about whether the Regulation requires the Legislative Council's approval. The Regulation was published in the Government Gazette on 1 June and laid on the table of the Legislative Council at its next sitting. On 20 June (within 28 days of that sitting), the Legislative Council passed a resolution amending one schedule to the Regulation.

Which of the following statements is correct?

A. The Regulation is invalid because the parent ordinance did not expressly provide for Legislative Council scrutiny
B. The Regulation was subject to the positive vetting procedure, and the Legislative Council could only approve or reject it, not amend it
C. The Regulation was subject to the negative vetting procedure, and the Legislative Council was entitled to amend it by resolution within 28 days of the sitting
D. The Regulation could only take effect after the Legislative Council had passed the resolution on 20 June

### Question 5 (Short answer — apply IRAC)

Kelvin signed a contract to purchase a pre-war *tong lau* (tenement building) in Sheung Wan from Doris for HK$40 million. The building is one of only a handful of its kind left in Hong Kong and has unique architectural features. A week before completion, Doris tells Kelvin she will not complete the sale because a third party has offered her HK$45 million. Kelvin does not want monetary compensation; he wants the building itself, and claims he is **automatically entitled** to a court order forcing Doris to complete the sale.

Advise Kelvin on the nature of the remedy he seeks and whether he is entitled to it as of right. Your answer should identify the relevant source(s) of law and apply the principle(s) to the facts.
