**Question 2 (MCQ)**

**Question 3 (Scenario MCQ)**

Cedar Trading Limited has a financial year-end of 31 December 20X1. The auditor's report is due to be signed on 15 March 20X2. On 10 February 20X2, one of Cedar's largest trade receivables customers was placed into liquidation. The audit team notes that the customer had been experiencing severe liquidity problems since October 20X1, and the balance was material to the financial statements. Separately, on 25 February 20X2, the Board declared a special dividend, which was not conditional on any event existing at year-end.

How should Cedar Trading Limited treat these two events in its financial statements for the year ended 31 December 20X1?

A. Adjust the financial statements for the customer's liquidation; make no adjustment and no disclosure for the special dividend.
B. Adjust the financial statements for the customer's liquidation; disclose the special dividend in the notes if material.
C. Disclose the customer's liquidation in the notes only; adjust the financial statements for the special dividend.
D. Neither event requires adjustment or disclosure because both occurred after the reporting date.

**Question 4 (Scenario MCQ)**

The auditor concludes that the use of the going concern basis of accounting is **appropriate**, but that a **material uncertainty exists** that may cast significant doubt on the company's ability to continue as a going concern. Management has included a note in the financial statements that **adequately discloses** the principal events and conditions and its plans to deal with them.

What is the effect on the auditor's report?

A. An unmodified opinion, with a separate section under the heading "Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern" drawing attention to the disclosure note.
B. A qualified opinion, with the going concern uncertainty described in the "Basis for Qualified Opinion" section.
C. An adverse opinion, because a material uncertainty exists in relation to going concern.
D. An unmodified opinion with no reference to going concern, because adequate disclosure has been made in the financial statements.
