# Exam Lens — HKICPA QP Module 8: Principles of Auditing
**Purpose:** Briefing for quiz item writers. Built from official past papers (Pilot CBE paper, Jun 2023 / Jun 2024 / Jun 2025) and the corresponding Examination Panelists' Reports.

---

## 1. Exam Structure

### Current format (Jun 2023 – Jun 2025 sessions — confirmed by papers + panelists' reports)
- **Duration:** 3 hours (implied by paper: ~1.8 minutes per mark; Section B's 80 marks ≈ 144–148 minutes + Section A ≈ 36 minutes).
- **Total:** 100 marks, all questions compulsory.
- **Section A — Multiple Choice Questions: 20 marks (20%).**
  - MCQs are NOT released in the published past papers; their coverage is only known from panelists' reports (see §3 and §4).
- **Section B — Written Questions: 80 marks (80%), exactly 4 compulsory scenario/discussion questions.**
  - Mark splits per session:
    - Jun 2023: Q1 22 / Q2 17 / Q3 17 / Q4 24
    - Jun 2024: Q1 27 / Q2 17 / Q3 23 / Q4 13
    - Jun 2025: Q1 17 / Q2 19 / Q3 19 / Q4 25
  - Each question broken into 4–6 sub-requirements of 2–10 marks each.
  - Suggested time is printed per question (e.g., "17 marks – approximately 30 minutes").

### New CBE format (per Pilot Examination Paper, published Oct 2025 — FORMAT CHANGE)
- **Duration reduced to 2 hours 30 minutes; 100 marks; all compulsory.**
- **Section A — Objective Type Questions (OTQ): 74 marks (74%)**, 37 questions (Q1–37), 2 marks each. OTQ types observed:
  - True/False statements (including paired sub-statements, e.g., Q1a/1b, 2a/2b, 3a/3b).
  - Drop-down / fill-in-the-blank ("Please Select" menus, e.g., "Substantive procedures are designed to ___ material misstatements").
  - Single-best-answer MCQ (A–D).
  - "Pick the correct combination" items using roman-numeral lists (e.g., "(1), (2) and (3) only").
  - Multiple-response ("Identify THREE…", e.g., answers "B, D, E").
- **Section B — Scenario-Based Questions: 26 marks (26%)**, 13 questions (Q38–50), 2 marks each, organised into 4 scenario groups (3+5+3+2 questions), each preceded by a scenario of ~150–300 words. Answers are still objective-type (MCQ / multi-select), NOT free text.
- **Implication:** the Institute is shifting M8 from long-form written answers to fully objective, scenario-anchored testing. Quiz writers should design for BOTH: written-style practice (current reality for recent sessions) and OTQ style (direction of travel per pilot).

### CBE features (from pilot paper)
- Computer-based; answer areas embedded per question; drop-down selection controls.
- Every item is 2 marks; no partial credit indicated.
- Scenario blocks displayed once, then 2–5 linked questions.

## 2. Question Style

### Command words (Section B written, ranked by frequency across Jun 23–25)
- **List** — the dominant command word ("List FOUR key matters…", "List FIVE factors…", "List THREE types…"). Pure recall; usually 2–6 marks.
- **Describe** — recall + slight elaboration ("Describe FOUR audit procedures…").
- **Explain** — must show why/how, applied to the scenario ("Explain the relationship between an overall audit strategy and an audit plan").
- **Define** — precise definitions ("Define Key Audit Matters", "Define the audit risk model").
- **Identify** — selection/recognition.
- **Contrast** — direct comparison ("Contrast an emphasis of matter paragraph with an other matter paragraph").
- **Analyse / Determine** — highest cognitive level, reserved for opinion-determination and risk-analysis requirements ("Determine the type of auditor's opinion… with detailed explanation", "Analyse the inherent risk of accounts receivable").

### Scenario style (Section B)
- Short Hong Kong-flavoured scenarios: 80–250 words, private HK companies ("Asia Pacific Limited", "Felix Limited", "Sunshine Limited") in familiar trades (F&B, computer hardware wholesale, manufacturing, dried seafood, property investment).
- Candidate role-played as audit manager / senior-in-charge / training manager.
- Typically a light scenario wrapper around largely knowledge-based requirements; only 1–2 sub-parts per question genuinely require application to case facts.
- One "big" question per paper (24–27 marks) usually on audit completion/reporting (going concern, subsequent events, opinion types).

### MCQ / OTQ design patterns (from pilot paper + panelist reports)
- Assertion↔procedure matching is the single most repeated mechanic ("Which procedure tests completeness of trade payables?", "Which assertion is NOT relevant to receivables confirmation?").
- Negation stems are common: "NOT relevant", "NOT appropriate", "would NOT be", "NOT explicitly stated".
- Combination items (roman-numeral lists) test lists from the LP (assertions, profitability ratios, elements of analytical procedures, general IT controls).
- Distractors are plausible-but-wrong swaps: existence vs completeness direction of testing; observation vs inspection; qualified vs adverse vs disclaimer.
- Scenario-based OTQs chain 2–5 items off one mini-case: ethics threats → opinion type → cooling-off period (pilot Q38–40); e-commerce risks → IT controls → inventory valuation procedures → opinion (pilot Q41–45).
- Topics flagged by panelists as MCQ-tested: professional ethics (every session), control activities, inherent risk, tests of control vs substantive procedures, audit risk model, CAATs, legal/regulatory framework, communication with TCWG.

- Recurring theme: **misinterpretation of question requirements** — candidates answer the question they wish had been asked.

### Cross-session patterns (what to test because students keep failing it)
- Procedure↔assertion mismatches (existence vs completeness direction; observation vs inspection).
- Confusing adjacent concepts: rights vs duties; EoM vs OM vs KAM vs other information; audit approach vs methodology; strategy vs plan.
- Failure to apply frameworks to case facts (control risk → substantive extent; inherent risk factors of the specific client).
- Under-studied "small" topics: sampling, KAM, CAATs, statutory/regulatory framework, HKSA 260.
- Not reading requirements precisely (wrong number of items, wrong assertion, wrong stage of audit).

## 5. Difficulty Calibration Guide (for 5-question OTQ chapter quizzes)

### What "exam-standard" means in M8
- **Baselines:** panelists repeatedly call most M8 items "straightforward" — the exam is primarily a **knowledge-recall + light-application** test, NOT a deep-case-analysis exam. Exam-standard = a competent student who studied the LP can answer in ~1.5–2 minutes per mark.
- **Cognitive mix to replicate** (per 5-question quiz):
  - ~2 questions: pure recall / definition (List/Define/Identify level) — e.g., "Which of the following is NOT an element of an assurance engagement?"
  - ~2 questions: discrimination between easily-confused concepts — the Institute's favourite trap zone (rights vs duties; EoM vs OM; observation vs inspection; existence vs completeness testing direction; qualified vs adverse vs disclaimer).
  - ~1 question: application to a 2–4-sentence mini-scenario (e.g., "Given control risk is assessed as low and inherent risk is not significant, what happens to the extent of substantive procedures?").
- **Difficulty ceiling:** do NOT exceed the hardest observed exam behaviour — opinion determination with justification (Jun 23 Q4(f) type) or multi-factor case analysis (Jun 24 Q4(c) type). Anything requiring synthesis of 3+ standards or open-ended judgement beyond the LP is above exam standard.
- **Difficulty floor:** single-fact trivia with no distractor plausibility (e.g., "What does HKSA stand for?") is below standard — every distractor must be a documented student confusion from §4.
- **Scenario length for OTQs:** max 3–5 sentences, one entity, one issue — mirroring pilot Section B items; full-page scenarios belong to written-question practice, not chapter quizzes.
- **Mark/time pacing cue:** 2-mark OTQs are the new standard unit; design quizzes as 5 × 2-mark items ≈ 5 minutes.
- **Answer-key rigour:** model answers must be traceable to the LP/HKSA wording (the Institute's suggested answers quote standards nearly verbatim — e.g., HKSA 200 overall objectives). Avoid ambiguity: negation stems ("NOT") must have exactly one defensible answer.
- **Chapter-quiz emphasis:** weight item difficulty toward the cross-session failure list in §4 — an exam-standard quiz should make students trip on the same traps the panelists keep reporting (assertion-procedure matching, concept adjacency, requirement precision).
