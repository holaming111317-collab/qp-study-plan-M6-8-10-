# M6 Chapter 13: Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors — Phase 3 Quiz

**Question 1**

Pearl Lighting Limited (Pearl), a Hong Kong lighting retailer, is reviewing its accounting treatments for the year ended 31 December 20X5. In accordance with HKAS 8, which of the following constitutes a **change in accounting policy**?

A. Pearl revises its warranty provision estimate from 2% to 3% of sales following an increase in products returned under warranty during 20X4.
B. Pearl changes its inventory cost formula from the first-in, first-out (FIFO) method to the weighted average cost method because management believes it provides more relevant information.
C. Pearl applies HKFRS 16 Leases for the first time to leasing arrangements that were previously immaterial but have become material in the current year.
D. Pearl applies its existing revenue recognition policy to a new line of business whose transactions differ in substance from those previously occurring.

**Question 2**

Golden Harvest Trading Limited (Golden Harvest) voluntarily changes its inventory cost formula during the year ended 31 December 20X6 because the new formula provides reliable and more relevant information. In accordance with HKAS 8, how should Golden Harvest account for this change?

A. Prospectively, by applying the new cost formula only to purchases made from the date of the change.
B. Retrospectively, by adjusting the opening balance of each affected component of equity for the earliest prior period presented and the other comparative amounts disclosed for each prior period presented, to the extent practicable.
C. As a change in accounting estimate, by including the cumulative effect in profit or loss of the current period only.
D. In accordance with the transitional provisions of HKAS 2 Inventories, since the change relates to inventory measurement.

**Question 3**

Cascade Home Appliances Limited (Cascade) offers a one-year warranty on all products sold. For the year ended 31 December 20X3, Cascade estimated warranty expense at 2% of sales. For the year ended 31 December 20X4, based on an analysis showing an increase in products returned under warranty, Cascade revised the estimate to 3% of sales. Sales for 20X4 were HK$5,000,000. Which journal entry correctly records the warranty expense for the year ended 31 December 20X4 in accordance with HKAS 8?

A. Dr Warranty provision HK$150,000; Cr Cash HK$150,000
B. Dr Warranty expense HK$150,000; Cr Warranty provision HK$150,000
C. Dr Retained earnings HK$150,000; Cr Warranty provision HK$150,000
D. Dr Warranty expense HK$100,000; Cr Warranty provision HK$100,000

**Question 4**

On 1 January 20X1, Harbour Logistics Limited (Harbour) acquired machinery at a cost of HK$1,800,000. At acquisition, the estimated useful life was 8 years and the residual value was HK$120,000. Harbour depreciates machinery on a straight-line basis. On 1 January 20X4, due to technological obsolescence, Harbour revised the remaining useful life to 3 years and the residual value to nil. In accordance with HKAS 8, what is the depreciation expense for the year ended 31 December 20X4?

A. HK$210,000
B. HK$350,000
C. HK$390,000
D. HK$600,000

**Question 5**

During the year ended 31 December 20X5, Victoria Textiles Limited (Victoria) discovered that, due to a counting error, goods already sold had been incorrectly included in closing inventory as at 31 December 20X4, overstating inventory by HK$800,000. The error is material. The income tax rate is 16.5%, and the error correction has a corresponding effect on accounting and taxable profits. In accordance with HKAS 8, by what amount should Victoria's retained earnings as at 31 December 20X4 be restated in the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 20X5?

A. Decrease of HK$800,000
B. Decrease of HK$668,000
C. Decrease of HK$132,000
D. No restatement is required; the error is corrected prospectively in the 20X5 financial statements.
