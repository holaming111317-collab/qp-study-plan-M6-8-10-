M10 Chapter 7: Introduction to Employment Law in Hong Kong — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

Phase 1 速查表

Learning Objectives

  1. Identify the sources of employment law in Hong Kong and determine the scope of application of the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57).
  2. Apply the control test, organisation test and multifactor test to distinguish an employee (contract of service) from an independent contractor (contract for service).
  3. Analyse the express and implied terms of an employment contract, including the implied duties of employers and employees at common law.
  4. Calculate and apply statutory entitlements on wages, MPF, rest days, holidays, leave, severance payment and long service payment under the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57) and related legislation.
  5. Apply the rules on termination (notice, summary dismissal, constructive dismissal, unlawful dismissal and remedies) and the four anti-discrimination ordinances to scenario facts using the IRAC approach.

必背考點

A. Relevant Ordinances（Cap. + section numbers，以 LP 所引為準）

1. Sources of law & scope of the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57) ('EO') - Three sources: (1) international law — ICCPR & ICESCR, implemented via Articles 39 and 153 of the Basic Law; (2) common law (formation, status, implied duties, vicarious liability); (3) domestic legislation. - Key domestic statutes: EO (Cap. 57); Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509); Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59); Employees' Compensation Ordinance (Cap. 282); Minimum Wage Ordinance (Cap. 608) ('MWO'); Mandatory Provident Fund Schemes Ordinance (Cap. 485) ('MPFSO'); Labour Tribunal Ordinance (Cap. 25). - EO s.4(2): EO applies to ALL employees regardless of nature of work, EXCEPT: (i) a family member employed by and living in the same dwelling as the employer; (ii) an overseas contract worker; (iii) a person serving under a crew agreement within the Merchant Shipping (Seafarers) Ordinance (Cap. 478) or on a ship not registered in HK; (iv) an apprentice under the Apprenticeship Ordinance (Cap. 47) (per EO s.2A); (v) a civil servant. - EO s.70: any express contractual clause that extinguishes or reduces EO rights/benefits/protection is void.

2. Who is employable - Age of Majority (Related Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 410) s.2(1): age of majority = 18. - Employment of Children Regulations (Cap. 57B) ('ECR') s.4(1): employment of a child under 13 generally prohibited; s.5: child aged 13–15 may be employed only if completed Form III AND parent consents in writing. - Employment of Young Persons (Industry) Regulation (Cap. 57C): governs employment of young persons aged 15–18. - Immigration Ordinance (Cap. 115) s.17I: offence to employ anyone not lawfully employable (no valid HK Permanent Resident Identity Card or valid working visa).

3. Contract of employment — key EO provisions - EO s.5(1): absent express agreement, every continuous contract is deemed a contract for one month renewable from month to month; onus on employer to prove otherwise. A fixed-term contract ends automatically at term expiry without notice. - EO s.6(3) (probation): within the first month of probation, either party may terminate without notice or payment in lieu; after the first month, notice of not less than 7 days (or as agreed). - EO s.6: termination by notice or payment in lieu; if contract silent, not less than one month's notice or one month's wages in lieu. Parties may not contract out but may waive at the time. - EO s.17: employee under continuous contract entitled to not less than one rest day in every seven days; substitute rest day within 30 days if worked in emergency; if rest day coincides with statutory holiday, an additional rest day must be given.

4. Remuneration and benefits - Continuous employment ('4–18 requirement', First Schedule EO): at least 18 hours per week for 4 consecutive weeks; EO s.3(2): onus on the employer to disprove continuity. - EO s.2(1): 'wages' = all remuneration, earnings, allowances (incl. travelling/attendance), attendance bonus, commission, overtime pay, tips and service charges capable of being expressed in money — subject to 11 exclusions (e.g. value of accommodation/food/medical care; employer's retirement scheme contributions; gratuitous or purely discretionary commission/bonus; non-recurrent travelling allowance; end of year payment under Part IIA; gratuity on completion/termination; gratuitous annual bonus). - EO s.23: wages due at expiry of wage period; payable as soon as practicable and no later than 7 days after the end of the wage period. EO s.22: wage period presumed to be one month. EO s.25(2): on termination, all termination payments (wages up to termination, payment in lieu of notice, unused annual leave pay, severance/long service payment, etc.) due within 7 days. - EO s.32 lawful wage deductions (10 categories) — key caps: deduction for damage/loss directly attributable to employee's neglect or default capped at the value of damage/loss or HK$300, whichever is less, per case; total deductions in one wage period ≤ one quarter of wages. Absence deductions must be proportionate to time absent. EO s.25(3): if employee quits without proper notice, employer may deduct wages in lieu of outstanding notice. - MWO (Cap. 608) s.8(2): minimum wage = total hours worked × minimum hourly rate; rate since 1 May 2019 = HK$37.5/hour (per LP). - EO s.43C: principal contractor/superior subcontractor liable for subcontractor employees' unpaid wages (site-related work); employee must serve written notice within 60 days after wages due; principal contractor pays within 30 days of notice. - EO s.49A: keep wage/employment records covering preceding 12 months, retained 6 months after employment ceases. - MPFSO (Cap. 485) s.7(1A): employer must ensure 'relevant employees' (aged 18–65, continuous employment ≥ 60 days) join a registered MPF scheme. Exempt: domestic employees; self-employed hawkers; persons under statutory pension schemes (e.g. civil servants, subsidised school teachers); overseas persons working <13 months or covered by overseas schemes; EU Office employees. - MPF contributions: 5% employer + 5% employee of relevant income; monthly income < HK$7,100 → employee pays nothing (employer still 5%); > HK$30,000 → capped at HK$1,500 each. 'Relevant income' (MPFSO s.2) excludes severance payments and long service payments.

5. Rest days, holidays and leave - EO s.39: 12 statutory holidays for every employee regardless of length of service; if required to work, a substituted holiday within 60 days before/after; payment in lieu of holiday is forbidden. General Holidays Ordinance (Cap. 149): 17 general holidays (12 overlap); binds banks, educational establishments, public offices and government departments only. - EO s.41AA paid annual leave (continuous employment ≥ 12 months): 7 days (years 1–2), 8 (yr 3), 9 (yr 4), 10 (yr 5), 11 (yr 6), 12 (yr 7), 13 (yr 8), 14 (yr 9+). Unbroken period unless employee consents: ≤10 days → up to 3 days separately; >10 days → at least 7 consecutive; payment in lieu allowed only for the part exceeding 10 days. Daily rate = average daily wages over preceding 12 months. - Sick leave (sickness allowance): continuous contract ≥ 1 month; sick leave ≥ 4 consecutive days (pregnancy check-up/post-confinement/miscarriage excepted); medical certificate; sufficient accumulated paid sick days. Rate = four-fifths of average daily wages (12-month average). Accrual: 2 days/month in first 12 months, 4 days/month thereafter, capped at 120 days. - Maternity leave: 14 weeks (Employment (Amendment) Ordinance 2020, effective 11 Dec 2020); paid if continuous contract ≥ 40 weeks before scheduled leave + notice + medical certificate; pay = four-fifths of average daily wages; additional 4-week pay capped at HK$80,000 (government reimburses employers). <40 weeks → 14 weeks unpaid leave still available with notice + certificate. Paternity leave: 5 days per confinement (notice ≥ 3 months before expected confinement, else ≥ 5 days before leave).

6. Termination of employment - Valid reasons for dismissal (EO s.32K): (a) misconduct; (b) incapability or disqualification; (c) redundancy or genuine operational requirements; (d) continued employment would contravene the law; (e) any other sufficient cause. Employee with ≥ 24 months continuous employment dismissed without valid reason → presumed dismissal to extinguish/reduce EO rights → unreasonable dismissal (consider all circumstances, EO s.32L(2)). Note: employer is NOT obliged to give a reason at the time of termination. - Summary dismissal (EO s.9(1)(a)): very serious misconduct or failure to improve after repeated warnings — (1) wilful disobedience of a lawful and reasonable order; (2) misconduct inconsistent with due and faithful discharge of duties; (3) fraud or dishonesty; (4) habitual neglect of duties. S.9(1)(b): any other common law ground justifying termination without notice. Summary dismissal = last resort / drastic measure. Dismissal for taking part in a strike is prohibited. - Statutory restrictions: unlawful to dismiss (i) a pregnant employee; (ii) an employee on paid sick leave; (iii) an employee who is a witness in court against the employer; (iv) a trade union member actively seeking to improve working conditions; (v) an injured employee before compensation settlement — UNLESS summary dismissal criteria are independently satisfied. - Remedies for unlawful/unreasonable dismissal (EO ss.32N & 33M): (1) reinstatement (same position, rights, seniority, pension — considered first); (2) re-engagement (comparable terms, by employer/successor/associated company); (3) terminal payments as just and appropriate (EO s.32O). Since the Employment (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2018, the Labour Tribunal may order reinstatement/re-engagement without employer's consent; non-compliance → additional award of 3 × average monthly salary, capped at HK$72,000, and possible criminal liability. - Constructive dismissal (EO s.10): employee may terminate without notice/payment if — (a) reasonable fear of physical danger by violence or disease not contemplated by the contract; (aa) ≥5 years' employment + certified permanently unfit for the particular type of work by a registered medical/Chinese medicine practitioner while engaged in that work; (b) ill-treatment by the employer; (c) any common law ground (employer's repudiatory/fundamental breach). Recognised grounds include: failure to provide sufficient work; failure to pay statutory remuneration/benefits; transfer without consent; failure to provide a safe and decent work environment; unlawful discriminatory conduct. - Severance payment (EO s.31B(1)): continuous contract ≥ 24 months + dismissed by reason of redundancy or laid off; voluntary resignation excluded. Redundancy (s.31B(2)): (1) employer closes/intends to close the business; (2) ceases business at the place where employee worked; (3) requirement for work of a particular kind ceases/diminishes. Lay-off: no work/no wages > half of normal working days in any 4 consecutive weeks, or > one-third in any 26 consecutive weeks. - Long service payment: continuous contract ≥ 5 years, dismissed (not summarily for serious misconduct, not for redundancy) and — (1) employee dies; (2) employee terminates due to illness; (3) employee retires at age 65 or above. - Formula (both payments): monthly-paid → 2/3 × last month's wages × reckonable years of service; daily/piece-rated → any 18 days' wages chosen out of last 30 normal working days × reckonable years. Cap: HK$390,000. - Labour Tribunal (Labour Tribunal Ordinance, Cap. 25): exclusive jurisdiction over claims arising from non-compliance with the EO, MWO or Apprenticeship Ordinance (Cap. 47); NO jurisdiction over tort claims, bankruptcy/winding-up matters, or claims under the Minor Employment Claims Adjudication Board Ordinance (Cap. 453).

7. Anti-discrimination ordinances - Four ordinances: SDO (Cap. 480); DDO (Cap. 487); FSDO (Cap. 527); RDO (Cap. 602). Equal Opportunities Commission (EOC) issues codes of practice. - Direct discrimination: less favourable treatment on the ground of sex/marital status/pregnancy, disability, family status or race. Test: objective 'but for' test — intention/motive irrelevant to liability (relevant only to remedies); claimant bears burden on balance of probabilities; where reason unclear, apply the two-part test (comparator question + causation question) per M v Secretary for Justice. - Indirect discrimination: a seemingly neutral requirement/condition which a considerably smaller proportion of the protected group can comply with, causing detriment, and not justifiable. Group-based comparison (correct comparator pool: University of Manchester v Jones). Justification = 3-step test: (1) legitimate aim; (2) rational connection; (3) necessity (EOC v Director of Education). - Harassment: unwelcome conduct a reasonable person would anticipate would offend, humiliate or intimidate. Sexual harassment: 'quid pro quo' + 'hostile environment' types; subjective unwelcomeness + objective reasonable-person assessment (Chen v Tamara Rus). Victimisation = a form of direct discrimination ('but for' test applies: Chang Ying Kwan v Wyeth (HK) Ltd). - SDO (Cap. 480): sex, marital status, pregnancy; sexual harassment; victimisation. Religion exception: s.22(1) SDO. - DDO (Cap. 487): covers disability of the person or their associates; harassment: s.3(6) DDO. Exception — s.12(2) DDO: not discrimination if the person, because of disability, cannot carry out the inherent requirements of the job, or would impose unjustifiable hardship (factors in s.4 DDO). - FSDO (Cap. 527): 'family status' = responsibility for care of an immediate family member (blood, marriage, adoption, affinity). Direct discrimination: s.5(a); indirect: s.5(b); unlawful to dismiss by reason of family status: s.8(2)(c). - RDO (Cap. 602): race discrimination, harassment (s.7(1), hostile environment s.7(2)), and vilification (public activity inciting hatred/serious contempt/severe ridicule). Religion exception: s.23 RDO. - Genuine occupational qualification (GOQ) exception: SDO/DDO/RDO, narrow; onus on employer. - Remedies (s.76(3A) SDO; s.72(4) DDO; s.54(3) FSDO; s.70(4) RDO): declaration; order to redress; employ/re-employ; promote; damages; punitive/exemplary damages; declare contract void. Court has wide discretion, global approach, but will not force an apology (Ma Bik Yung v Ko Chuen). - Discrimination Legislation (Miscellaneous Amendments) Ordinance 2020: workplace-participant harassment (s.23A SDO; s.22A DDO; s.24A RDO — covers interns, volunteers, contract workers); RDO 'near relative' replaced by 'associate' (s.5 RDO); damages available for indirect discrimination on sex/family status/race without proof of intention. - Sex Discrimination (Amendment) Ordinance 2021 (effective 19 June 2021): breastfeeding protection — direct discrimination (s.8A(1)(a) SDO), indirect discrimination (s.8A(1)(b)), harassment (s.2A(1)–(2)), victimisation; child need not be biological; no time limit.

B. Leading Cases（full citation）

  • Ready Mixed Concrete (South East) Ltd v Minister of Pensions and National Insurance (1968) 2 QB 497 — Mackenna J's three conditions for a contract of service: (1) servant provides work/skill for remuneration; (2) sufficient degree of control; (3) other provisions consistent with a contract of service. Owner-drivers held to be independent contractors.
  • Stevenson, Jordan and Harrison Ltd v Macdonald and Evans (1952) 1 TLR 101 — Lord Denning: under a contract of service the work is an integral part of the business; under a contract for services it is only accessory to it (organisation/integration test).
  • Market Investigations Ltd v Minister of Social Security (1969) 2 QB 173 — Cooke J's flexible multifactor approach.
  • Lee Ting Sang v Chung Chi-Keung (1990) 1 HKLR 764 (PC) — Privy Council endorsed the multifactor approach; control no longer sole determinant; no exhaustive list of factors.
  • Poon Chau Nam v Yim Siu Cheung (2007) 1 HKLRD 951 (CFA) — modern approach: 'a nuanced and not a mechanical approach: painting a picture from the accumulation of detail'; factors include control, own equipment, own helpers, financial risk, investment/management responsibility, opportunity to profit. Casual workers: umbrella contract vs specific engagement contract.
  • Kao Lee & Yip v Koo Hoi Yan (2003) 3 HKLRD 296 — employee's fiduciary duty; merely informing a client of departure is not solicitation; going along with a client's proposal to move business without employer's consent is breach.
  • Kuoni Travel (China) Ltd v Kelly Frances Richards, HCA 1265/2006 (unreported) — four factors for confidentiality: nature of employment; nature of information; whether employer impressed confidentiality; whether information easily isolated.
  • Deacons v White & Case Ltd Liability Partnership (2003) 3 HKLRD 670 — after termination, confidentiality protection is restricted to trade secrets only.
  • Thomas Vincent v South China Morning Post Publishers (2005) 4 HKLRD 258 (CFA) — misconduct under s.32K EO must be factual, real and relevant; ordinarily a first offender should not be instantly dismissed without warning or a further chance; dismissal without any formal disciplinary process was unreasonable.
  • Ying Kee Safes and Furniture Limited v Wong Yam Tak, HCLA 105/1995 (unreported) — lateness is unauthorised absence and misconduct; isolated lateness insufficient, but persistent and repeated lateness after warnings justifies summary dismissal.
  • Secretary for Justice v Chan Wah and Others (2000) 3 HKLRD 641 (CFA) — adopted the objective 'but for' test (from R v Birmingham City Council, ex parte Equal Opportunities Commission (1989) 1 AC 1155) for direct discrimination; motive irrelevant to liability.
  • M v Secretary for Justice (2009) 2 HKLRD 298 (CA) — two-part test: (1) comparator question (same or not materially different circumstances); (2) causation question; employer's actual knowledge of disability irrelevant — knowledge of the manifestation of disability = knowledge of disability.
  • Shamoon v Chief Constable of Royal Ulster Constabulary (2003) ICR 337 — where comparator identification is problematic, focus on the factual enquiry of the reason for the treatment (applied in Sit Ka Yin Priscilla v Equal Opportunities Commission (2010) HKEC 208).
  • Equal Opportunities Commission v Director of Education (2001) 2 HKLRD 690 — three-step justification test for indirect discrimination: legitimate aim; rational connection; necessity.
  • University of Manchester v Jones (1993) ICR 474 (CA) — correct comparator pool = all men and women with the necessary qualifications/experience, not a distorted subgroup.
  • Chang Ying Kwan v Wyeth (HK) Ltd (2001) 2 HKC 129 — pregnancy discrimination + victimisation after EOC complaint; 'but for' test applies to victimisation.
  • Chen v Tamara Rus (2001) 3 HKLRD 541 (CA) — 'unwelcome' is subjective to the victim; then objective assessment of anticipated offence/humiliation/intimidation.
  • B v King of the King Group Limited, DCEO 9/2010 — employer vicariously liable for co-worker's sexual harassment for failing to take reasonable preventive steps; HK$80,000 damages for emotional distress.
  • Ma Bik Yung v Ko Chuen (2002) 2 HKLRD 1 (CFA) — wide discretion on remedies, global approach; court will not force an unwilling respondent to apologise.

C. IRAC Application Points（常見 scenario 落點）

  • Issue: status dispute → Rule: three tests (control → organisation → multifactor, Poon Chau Nam) → Application: run through control/ownership of tools/delegation/financial risk on facts; label is not conclusive → Conclusion: employee vs independent contractor; only employees get EO/ECO protection.
  • Issue: dismissal without notice → distinguish summary dismissal (s.9 EO — employer-initiated, last resort) from constructive dismissal (s.10 EO — employee-initiated, employer's repudiatory breach); check warnings given (Thomas Vincent; Ying Kee).
  • Issue: dismissal reason → if ≥24 months continuous service, employer must justify under s.32K EO, else unreasonable dismissal (s.32L(2)); remedies ss.32N/33M/32O (reinstatement first).
  • Issue: redundancy → s.31B(1)–(2) EO; watch for refusal of suitable alternative employment and whether requirement for the work truly 'ceased or diminished' (mere cost-saving regrade ≠ redundancy).
  • Issue: discrimination → identify protected ground and ordinance; choose direct ('but for') vs indirect (neutral condition + disparate impact + 3-step justification); check exceptions (GOQ; DDO s.12(2) inherent requirements); remedies under the relevant ordinance; victimisation if complaint-triggered retaliation.
