M6 Chapter 7: Lease Accounting — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

Phase 1 速查表

Learning Objectives

  1. Determine whether a contract is, or contains, a lease in accordance with HKFRS 16 Leases.
  2. Measure right-of-use assets and lease liabilities from the lessee's perspective, at initial recognition and subsequently.
  3. Apply the lessee recognition exemptions for short-term leases and leases of low-value assets.
  4. Classify leases as finance leases or operating leases from the lessor's perspective and account for each type.
  5. Identify the presentation and disclosure requirements for lessees and lessors under HKFRS 16.

必背考點

A. Identifying a lease（Recognition gateway）

  • HKFRS 16.9: A contract is, or contains, a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration, assessed at the inception date（inception date = earlier of the date of the lease agreement and the date of commitment to the principal terms and conditions）。Recognition/initial measurement 要等到 commencement date（lessor makes the underlying asset available for use）。
  • Identified asset (HKFRS 16.B13): usually explicitly specified in the contract; no identified asset if the supplier has a substantive substitution right, which requires BOTH (HKFRS 16.B14): 1. the supplier has the practical ability to substitute alternative assets throughout the period of use; AND 2. the supplier would benefit economically from substitution (benefits > costs).
  • Substitution only upon fault/damage/technical upgrade → not substantive → identified asset 仍然存在。
  • Right to control use (HKFRS 16.B9) requires BOTH: 1. right to obtain substantially all of the economic benefits from use (directly or indirectly, incl. subleasing) (HKFRS 16.B21); AND 2. right to direct the use (how and for what purpose — type/when/where/whether & quantity of output) (HKFRS 16.B24–B26), or such decisions are predetermined.
  • Protective rights (e.g. limits on use to protect the supplier's asset/personnel) do not prevent the customer having the right to direct use (HKFRS 16.B28/B30 area）。
  • Components of a contract (HKFRS 16.12–14, B32–B33): separate lease components (asset useable on its own + not highly dependent/interrelated with other assets) from non-lease components; allocate consideration on relative stand-alone prices. Lessee may use a practical expedient to aggregate non-lease components (elect by class of underlying asset); the lessor has NO such expedient and must always separate.

B. Lessee — Recognition & initial measurement

  • Single lessee model: recognise a right-of-use (ROU) asset and a lease liability at the commencement date for ALL leases unless a recognition exemption applies (HKFRS 16.22).
  • Lease term (HKFRS 16.18) = non-cancellable period + periods covered by an extension option (only if lessee is reasonably certain to exercise) + periods covered by a termination option (only if lessee is reasonably certain NOT to exercise). Assessed at commencement considering economic incentives (below-market option rentals, significant leasehold improvements, termination penalties, importance of asset, past practice) (HKFRS 16.B37–B40). Reassess if significant events/changes within the lessee's control occur (HKFRS 16.20).
  • Lease liability — initial measurement (HKFRS 16.26–27): present value of future lease payments (i.e. excluding payments made at/before commencement), comprising: 1. fixed payments less any lease incentives receivable; 2. variable payments that depend on an index or a rate (e.g. CPI, benchmark interest rate, market rental rates) — initially measured using the index/rate at the commencement date (HKFRS 16.28); 3. amounts expected to be payable under residual value guarantees (expected payment, NOT the full guaranteed amount); 4. exercise price of a purchase option — only if reasonably certain to be exercised; 5. penalties for terminating — only if the lease term reflects the lessee exercising the termination option.
  • ❌ Variable payments linked to performance or usage (e.g. % of lessee's sales) are EXCLUDED — expensed as incurred — to prevent manipulation of the liability.
  • Discount rate (HKFRS 16.26, App A): interest rate implicit in the lease (the rate making PV of lease payments + PV of unguaranteed residual value = fair value of the underlying asset + lessor's initial direct costs); if not readily determinable → lessee's incremental borrowing rate.
  • ROU asset — initial measurement at cost (HKFRS 16.23–24):
Component Treatment
Initial measurement of the lease liability +
Lease payments made at or before commencement, less lease incentives received + / −
Initial direct costs of the lessee (incremental, e.g. commissions, legal fees) +
Estimated dismantling/removal/restoration costs when the obligation arises (HKAS 37 provision) +
- The lessee recognises the ROU asset, NOT the underlying asset itself.
- Recognition exemptions (HKFRS 16.5–6, 8) — lessee may ELECT not to recognise ROU asset/lease liability; payments expensed straight-line (or another systematic basis):
1. Short-term lease: lease term ≤ 12 months at commencement AND no purchase option; election made by class of underlying asset.
2. Low-value asset: assessed on the value of the asset when new, on an absolute basis (not materiality); IASB had in mind ≈ US$5,000 (≈ HK$40,000) or less — e.g. tablets, personal computers, small office furniture, telephones (IFRS 16.BC100; HKFRS 16.B3–B8).

C. Lessee — Subsequent measurement

  • Lease liability (HKFRS 16.36): carrying amount is increased by interest (effective interest method at the rate used at commencement), reduced by lease payments made, and remeasured for reassessments or lease modifications.
  • Changes (HKFRS 16.40, 42): e.g. change in an index/rate used to determine variable payments → remeasure the lease liability using the original (unchanged) discount rate; corresponding adjustment to the ROU asset; if the ROU asset is reduced to zero, any remaining reduction goes to profit or loss (HKFRS 16.39).
  • ROU asset (HKFRS 16.29–30): cost model → cost less accumulated depreciation less accumulated impairment losses, adjusted for remeasurements of the lease liability.
  • Depreciation (HKFRS 16.31–32, per HKAS 16):
  • If ownership transfers at end of lease term, or a purchase option is reasonably certain to be exercised → depreciate from commencement date to the end of the useful life of the underlying asset.
  • Otherwise → depreciate from commencement date to the earlier of the end of the useful life of the ROU asset and the end of the lease term.
  • Impairment: HKAS 36 applies to ROU assets (HKFRS 16.33). Revaluation model (HKAS 16) or fair value model (HKAS 40) permitted where applicable.

D. Lessor — Classification（Recognition）

  • Dual lessor model: at the inception date, classify each lease as a finance lease or an operating lease based on whether the lease transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership of the underlying asset (HKFRS 16.61–62). Substance over form.
  • Finance lease criteria (HKFRS 16.63) — normally a finance lease if ANY of: 1. ownership transfers to the lessee by the end of the lease term; 2. purchase option at a price sufficiently lower than fair value that exercise is reasonably certain at inception; 3. lease term covers the major part of the asset's economic life (even if title does not transfer); 4. at inception, PV of lease payments ≈ substantially all of the fair value of the underlying asset; 5. the asset is so specialised that only the lessee can use it without major modifications.
  • Additional indicators (HKFRS 16.64): lessee bears lessor's cancellation losses; gains/losses on residual value fluctuation accrue to the lessee; lessee can continue for a secondary period at rent substantially below market.

E. Lessor — Measurement

  • Finance lease (HKFRS 16.67–70): derecognise the underlying asset; recognise a receivable = net investment in the lease = PV of (lease payments receivable incl. fixed, index/rate-based variable, RVGs from any party, purchase option price if reasonably certain, termination penalties if applicable) + PV of any unguaranteed residual value. Discount at the interest rate implicit in the lease.
  • Two key differences vs the lessee's lease liability: (1) includes unguaranteed residual value; (2) includes RVG guaranteed by any party (not just the lessee).
  • Subsequent (HKFRS 16.75–77): recognise finance income at a constant periodic rate of return on the net investment; each receipt splits into finance income + principal repayment. Apply HKFRS 9 derecognition and impairment to the net investment; review unguaranteed residual values regularly.
  • Manufacturer or dealer lessor (HKFRS 16.71–74): treat as an outright sale at commencement:
  • Revenue = LOWER of fair value of the underlying asset and PV of lease payments discounted at a market rate of interest (anti-abuse: prevents revenue inflation via inflated FV or artificially low interest rate);
  • Cost of goods sold = cost (or carrying amount) of the underlying asset;
  • Selling profit/loss = revenue − COGS, accounted for per HKFRS 15;
  • Costs of obtaining the lease are expensed at commencement (NOT added to net investment).
  • Operating lease (HKFRS 16.81–85): lessor keeps the underlying asset in its SFP, depreciates it per HKAS 16/HKAS 38; lease income recognised straight-line (or another systematic basis); initial direct costs added to the carrying amount of the underlying asset and expensed over the lease term on the same basis as lease income; impairment per HKAS 36.

F. Presentation & Disclosure

  • Lessee — SFP (HKFRS 16.47–48): ROU assets (non-investment property) presented separately from other assets OR within the same line item as owned equivalents with disclosure of which line items include them; lease liabilities separately or with other liabilities with disclosure. ROU assets meeting investment property definition → presented as investment property.
  • Lessee — P&L (HKFRS 16.49): interest expense on the lease liability (a component of finance costs) and depreciation on the ROU asset presented separately.
  • Lessee — Statement of cash flows (HKFRS 16.50): principal portion of lease payments → financing activities; interest portion → per the lessee's HKAS 7 policy for interest paid; payments for short-term / low-value / variable-not-in-liability leases → operating activities.
  • Lessee disclosures (HKFRS 16.52–54) — single note/section, tabular format: depreciation charge for ROU assets by class of underlying asset; interest expense on lease liabilities; short-term lease expense; low-value asset lease expense; expense for variable lease payments not included in the lease liability; income from subleasing ROU assets; total cash outflow for leases; additions to ROU assets; carrying amount of ROU assets at period end by class of underlying asset.
  • Lessor disclosures: finance leases (HKFRS 16.90) — selling profit/loss, finance income on the net investment, variable-payment income; operating leases (HKFRS 16.92) — lease income, separately disclosing variable lease payments not depending on an index/rate; plus qualitative and quantitative information on leasing activities and risk management of retained rights (HKFRS 16.91, 95–96).

易混淆概念對比

Table 1 — Lessor: Finance lease vs Operating lease

Finance lease Operating lease
Classification trigger (HKFRS 16.61–63) Transfers substantially all risks and rewards incidental to ownership to lessee Risks and rewards retained by lessor
Underlying asset in lessor's SFP Derecognised; replaced by lease receivable (net investment in the lease) Remains in SFP (PPE/investment property); depreciated per HKAS 16/38
Income recognised Finance income at constant periodic rate of return on net investment Lease income straight-line (or systematic basis) over lease term
Initial direct costs Included in measuring net investment (financier); expensed at commencement (manufacturer/dealer lessor) Added to carrying amount of the underlying asset and expensed over lease term

Lessee's incremental borrowing rate 承租人增量借款利率——隱含利率搵唔到時嘅後備利率 If the interest rate implicit in the lease cannot be readily determined, the lessee uses its incremental borrowing rate to discount the lease payments.
