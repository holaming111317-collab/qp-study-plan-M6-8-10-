M6 Financial Accounting — Final Mock (Chapters 1–25, cumulative)

Mock: M6 Final Mock (v1, preloaded) Coverage: Chapters 1–25 (cumulative), weighted towards the second-half chapters and consolidation/business combination topics (Ch14–25) Time allowed: 60 minutes Total: 20 marks (1 mark per question) Format: CLOSED-BOOK. Answer ALL 20 objective test questions (OTQs). For multiple-choice questions, select the ONE best answer (A–D). For calculation/short-answer questions, state the exact figure or term required (journal entries must balance). Instructions: Manage your own time (≈3 minutes per question). If you exceed the time allowed, honestly report your actual time taken when self-marking. Retest sessions for incorrectly answered questions will be added subsequently according to the Active Retest Queue.

Questions

Q1. On 1 September 20X5, Harbour Limited acquired 75% of the ordinary share capital of Marina Limited; control passed to Harbour Limited on that date. The consideration comprised:

  • cash of HK$3,000,000 payable at settlement;
  • 200,000 ordinary shares of Harbour Limited to be issued to Marina Limited's former shareholders; and
  • a further cash payment of HK$1,000,000 if Marina Limited meets a revenue target within 12 months of acquisition.

The fair value of Harbour Limited's shares was HK$25 per share on the agreement date (1 August 20X5), HK$28 per share on 1 September 20X5, and HK$30 per share when the shares were actually issued on 5 September 20X5. The fair value of the contingent consideration at the acquisition date was assessed at HK$400,000. Harbour Limited also incurred due diligence fees of HK$150,000 and share issue costs of HK$20,000 in connection with the acquisition.

In accordance with HKFRS 3 (Revised) Business Combinations, what is the consideration transferred?

A. HK$8,400,000 B. HK$9,000,000 C. HK$9,170,000 D. HK$10,000,000

Q2. (Calculation) On 1 July 20X4, Pearl Limited acquired 80% of the ordinary share capital of Shell Limited for HK$980,000. On that date, Shell Limited's equity comprised share capital of HK$200,000, retained earnings of HK$550,000 and other reserves of HK$100,000. Pearl Limited assessed that land held by Shell Limited had a fair value HK$50,000 in excess of its carrying amount; the fair values of all other identifiable assets and liabilities equalled their carrying amounts. Pearl Limited measured the non-controlling interest (NCI) in Shell Limited at its fair value of HK$240,000 at the acquisition date.

Calculate the goodwill arising on acquisition, and prepare the consolidation adjustment (pro forma) journal entry to eliminate the investment in Shell Limited and the pre-acquisition equity as at 1 July 20X4.

Q3. SkyBuild Limited enters into a contract with a customer to construct a customised production facility on the customer's own site. The customer controls the site throughout construction. Which of the following correctly describes how SkyBuild Limited recognises revenue from this contract in accordance with HKFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers?

A. At a point in time, on completion of the facility, because control of the facility passes to the customer only on handover. B. Over time, because SkyBuild Limited's performance creates an asset on the customer's site that the customer controls as the asset is created. C. Over time, because the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefits provided by SkyBuild Limited's performance. D. At a point in time, measured progressively as each progress invoice is issued to the customer.

Q4. Summit Limited's reporting period ended 31 December 20X7. Its financial statements were authorised for issue on 15 March 20X8. The following events occurred before authorisation:

(1) On 10 February 20X8, a court case against Summit Limited was settled, confirming that Summit Limited had a present obligation at 31 December 20X7; (2) On 20 February 20X8, a fire destroyed Summit Limited's principal warehouse; and (3) On 5 March 20X8, the directors declared a final dividend for 20X7.

In accordance with HKAS 10 Events after the Reporting Period, which event(s) require(s) Summit Limited to adjust the amounts recognised in its financial statements for the year ended 31 December 20X7?

A. Event (1) only B. Events (1) and (2) only C. Events (1) and (3) only D. All three events

Q5. (Calculation) During the year ended 30 June 20X9, Falcon Limited sold equipment with a cost of HK$2,400,000 and a net book value of HK$650,000, recognising a gain on disposal of HK$120,000. At 30 June 20X9, HK$80,000 of the sale proceeds remained receivable from the buyer (opening balance of this receivable: nil). Falcon Limited also purchased new equipment of HK$1,000,000, paying the full amount in cash during the year.

Determine the amounts to be reported under cash flows from investing activities in Falcon Limited's statement of cash flows for the year ended 30 June 20X9.

Q6. Delta Limited purchases a portfolio of corporate bonds. Delta Limited's business model is to hold financial assets in order to collect contractual cash flows, and the contractual terms of the bonds give rise, on specified dates, to cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest on the principal amount outstanding. In accordance with HKFRS 9 Financial Instruments, how should the bonds be subsequently measured?

A. At fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL) B. At amortised cost C. At fair value through other comprehensive income (FVTOCI) D. At cost less impairment losses

Q7. (Calculation) Volt Limited sells electric scooters with a six-month warranty under which it will repair any manufacturing defects. For scooters sold in the period from 1 August 20X6 to 31 December 20X6, Volt Limited estimates that, if minor defects existed in all scooters sold, repair costs would be HK$2,000,000; if major defects existed in all scooters sold, repair costs would be HK$4,000,000. Based on past experience, Volt Limited estimates that 70% of the scooters sold will have no defects, 20% will have minor defects and 10% will have major defects. By 31 December 20X6, repairs costing HK$180,000 had already been made in respect of these sales.

Determine the provision for warranties to be recognised by Volt Limited at 31 December 20X6, and prepare the journal entry to record it.

Q8. Which of the following items gives rise to a deductible temporary difference (and hence a potential deferred tax asset) in accordance with HKAS 12 Income Taxes?

A. A warranty provision expensed for accounting purposes when the goods are sold, which is tax deductible only when the warranty claims are actually paid. B. Development costs capitalised as an intangible asset, where research and development costs are tax deductible in the year incurred. C. Interest income recognised when earned, which is assessable for tax only when the cash is received. D. Accrued fines and penalties, which are not deductible for tax purposes.

Q9. During 20X8, Beacon Limited reviewed the useful life of a production machine and revised its estimate of the remaining useful life from 10 years to 6 years, reflecting updated expectations of the period over which economic benefits will be consumed. The machine continues to be depreciated on a straight-line basis. In accordance with HKAS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in Accounting Estimates and Errors, how should Beacon Limited account for this revision?

A. As a change in accounting policy, applied retrospectively as if the new useful life had always been used. B. As a change in accounting estimate, recognised prospectively in the current and future periods affected. C. As a prior period error, corrected retrospectively by restating comparative amounts. D. No adjustment is required; depreciation continues based on the original useful life until the machine is disposed of.

Q10. (Calculation) For the year ended 31 December 20X6, Orbit Limited reported a profit attributable to ordinary shareholders of the parent entity of HK$9,200,000. On 1 January 20X6, Orbit Limited had 2,000,000 ordinary shares on issue. On 1 July 20X6, it issued a further 600,000 ordinary shares at their full prevailing market price. There were no other changes in ordinary shares during the year.

Calculate Orbit Limited's basic earnings per share for the year ended 31 December 20X6 in accordance with HKAS 33 Earnings per Share.

Q11. Parent P provides management services to its wholly owned Subsidiary S for a fee of HK$500,000. The income recognised by Parent P exactly equals the expense recognised by Subsidiary S, so the transaction has no net effect on consolidated profit for the year. Which of the following best explains why the transaction must nevertheless be eliminated on consolidation?

A. Because the transaction gives rise to an unrealised profit that inflates consolidated net assets. B. Because, although there is no net impact on the group's profit, not eliminating the transaction would overstate both the income and the expenses of the group and provide misleading information to users. C. No elimination is necessary, because the income and expense fully offset within profit or loss. D. Because intra-group service transactions are prohibited transactions for a group viewed as a single economic entity.

Q12. (Calculation) Apex Limited owns 75% of Zenith Limited, acquired two years ago. For the year ended 30 June 20X8, Zenith Limited reported a profit of HK$40,000,000. The following information is relevant:

  • On acquisition, a building held by Zenith Limited was assessed to have a fair value HK$10,000,000 in excess of its carrying amount; the building is depreciated on a straight-line basis over a remaining useful life of 10 years (assessed at the acquisition date), with nil residual value.
  • During the year, Zenith Limited sold inventory to Apex Limited for HK$12,000,000; the inventory had cost Zenith Limited HK$8,000,000. One quarter of this inventory remained on hand in Apex Limited's warehouse at 30 June 20X8.

Calculate the NCI's share of Zenith Limited's contribution to Apex Limited's consolidated profit for the year ended 30 June 20X8.

Q13. In accordance with HKAS 7 Statement of Cash Flows, how may a non-financial entity classify cash flows relating to interest paid?

A. Operating activities or financing activities B. Financing activities only C. Operating activities only D. Investing activities only

Q14. (Calculation) The following information relates to Meridian Limited as at 31 March 20X9:

  • A machine has a carrying amount of HK$4,200,000 and a tax base of HK$3,000,000 (tax capital allowances claimed exceed accounting depreciation).
  • A warranty provision has a carrying amount of HK$600,000. Warranty costs are tax deductible only when incurred (paid).
  • The deferred tax liability account balance at 31 March 20X8 was HK$280,000, and the deferred tax asset account balance at 31 March 20X8 was HK$60,000.
  • The tax rate is 16.5%. It is probable that sufficient future taxable income will be available.

Determine the closing balances of the deferred tax liability and the deferred tax asset at 31 March 20X9, and prepare the journal entries to record the movements for the year.

Q15. Entity A and Entity B incorporate Vehicle Co Limited, each holding 50% of the voting rights; all decisions about relevant activities require their unanimous consent. Under the terms of the contractual arrangement, Entity A and Entity B will purchase all of Vehicle Co's output in a 50:50 ratio at a price reflecting Vehicle Co's costs of operation (i.e. Vehicle Co operates on a break-even basis), and Vehicle Co cannot sell output to third parties without the agreement of Entity A and Entity B. In accordance with HKFRS 11 Joint Arrangements, how should Entity A classify this arrangement?

A. As a joint venture, because the arrangement is structured through a separate legal entity. B. As a joint operation, because the arrangement is designed such that its activities primarily aim to provide the parties with output and it depends on the parties on a continuous basis for settling its liabilities. C. As an associate, because each party holds only 50% of the voting rights. D. As a subsidiary, because Entity A shares control of Vehicle Co.

Q16. (Calculation) On 1 January 20X5, Pioneer Limited acquired 30% of the ordinary share capital of Anchor Limited for HK$3,000,000. On that date, the net fair value of Anchor Limited's identifiable assets and liabilities was HK$9,000,000, which included an unrecognised trademark with a fair value of HK$500,000 and a remaining useful life of 10 years (nil residual value, straight-line amortisation). For the year ended 31 December 20X5, Anchor Limited reported a net profit of HK$1,400,000 and declared and paid a dividend of HK$200,000.

Determine the equity accounted carrying amount of Pioneer Limited's investment in Anchor Limited at 31 December 20X5, and prepare the journal entries to record Pioneer Limited's share of Anchor Limited's profit and the dividend received. (Significant influence is assumed; the investment is an associate.)

Q17. In accordance with HKFRS 16 Leases, which of the following leases may a lessee elect to account for without recognising a right-of-use asset and a lease liability?

A. A 12-month lease of office space with no purchase option. B. A five-year lease of production machinery. C. A three-year lease of a newly delivered executive motor car. D. A ten-year lease of a plot of land.

Q18. (Calculation) Kowloon Traders Limited, whose functional currency is the Hong Kong dollar, purchased inventory for US$100,000 on credit on 1 December 20X7, when the spot exchange rate was HK$7.80 = US$1. The amount was unpaid at 31 December 20X7, when the spot rate was HK$7.75 = US$1. Kowloon Traders Limited settled the payable on 15 January 20X8, when the spot rate was HK$7.85 = US$1.

Determine the exchange difference(s) to be recognised in profit or loss for the years ended 31 December 20X7 and 31 December 20X8, and prepare the related journal entries (including the settlement).

Q19. At 31 December 20X8, an item of plant has a carrying amount of HK$500,000. Its fair value less costs of disposal is estimated at HK$430,000, and its value in use is estimated at HK$460,000. In accordance with HKAS 36 Impairment of Assets, what impairment loss (if any) should be recognised in profit or loss? (The plant is carried at cost less accumulated depreciation.)

A. HK$70,000 B. HK$40,000 C. Nil — no impairment loss is recognised D. HK$500,000

Q20. On acquisition of a subsidiary, contingent consideration was measured at its acquisition-date fair value of HK$1,500,000 and included as part of the consideration transferred. One year later, when the contingency was resolved, the acquirer was required to pay HK$3,000,000 (more than the acquisition-date fair value). In accordance with HKFRS 3 (Revised) Business Combinations, how should the difference between the amount finally transferred and the acquisition-date fair value be accounted for?

A. It adjusts goodwill retrospectively, as if the final amount had been known at the acquisition date. B. It does not form part of the business combination accounting; the post-acquisition change is recognised in accordance with the applicable HKFRS (e.g. in profit or loss). C. It reduces the consideration transferred and the gain on bargain purchase. D. It is capitalised as a separate intangible asset.
