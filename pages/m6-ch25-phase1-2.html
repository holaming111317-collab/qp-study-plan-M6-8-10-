M6 Chapter 25: Joint Arrangements — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

Phase 1 速查表

Learning Objectives

  1. Define joint control and assess whether it exists using the two-step approach (collective control + unanimous consent).
  2. Define a joint arrangement and identify its key characteristics (contractual arrangement binding two or more parties, shared control).
  3. Classify a joint arrangement as a joint operation or a joint venture by assessing the parties' rights and obligations (structure, legal form, contractual terms, other facts and circumstances).
  4. Account for an interest in a joint venture using the equity method, and account for an interest in a joint operation by recognising shares of assets, liabilities, revenues and expenses.
  5. Prepare the presentation and disclosure in respect of interests in joint ventures and joint operations (HKAS 1 / HKFRS 12 / HKAS 24).

必背考點

1. Recognition — Is it a joint arrangement? (HKFRS 11)

Definition of a joint arrangement (HKFRS 11.4–6) - An arrangement of which two or more parties have joint control; the parties are bound by a contractual arrangement, and that contractual arrangement gives two or more of those parties joint control. - HKFRS 11 applies to all parties to a joint arrangement — not just those with joint control (a mere participant must still consider HKFRS 11).

Joint control (HKFRS 11.7) = the contractually agreed sharing of control, which exists only when decisions about the relevant activities require the unanimous consent of the parties sharing control. Two-step assessment: 1. Collective control (HKFRS 11.8, B5): two or more parties must act together to direct the relevant activities; a party that can only block (but not direct) decisions does not control. 2. Unanimous consent (HKFRS 11.9, B6, B9, B10): each party sharing control can prevent any other party (or group) from making unilateral decisions about relevant activities.

Key fine points: - More than one combination of agreeing parties can reach the required voting threshold → the contract must specify which combination must agree unanimously, otherwise there is no joint arrangement (HKFRS 11.B8). - Unanimous consent over protective rights only → that investor does not have joint control (HKFRS 11.9, B6, B9, B10). - Unanimous consent is not needed on every decision (e.g. dispute-resolution mechanisms do not prevent joint control) (HKFRS 11.9, B9–B10). - Not all parties need joint control — some may be pure participants/investors (HKFRS 11.7, 11). - Reassess when facts and circumstances change — a party may gain or cease to have joint control (HKFRS 11.12–13).

2. Recognition — Classification: joint operation vs joint venture (HKFRS 11.14–16)

Classification Rights and obligations of the jointly controlling parties Parties are called
Joint operation Rights to the assets, and obligations for the liabilities, relating to the arrangement Joint operators
Joint venture Rights to the net assets of the arrangement Joint venturers

Classification uses a substance-over-form approach — the detail of the arrangement drives classification (HKFRS 11.15–16). Assessment sequence (Exhibit 25.2 decision tree, HKFRS 11.17, B15–B16, B21, B24, B33):

  1. Not structured through a separate vehicle → can only be a joint operation. ("Separate vehicle" = a separately identifiable financial structure, including separate legal entities or entities recognised by statute, regardless of legal personality — HKFRS 11 Appendix A.)
  2. Separate vehicle → does the legal form give the parties rights to net assets (e.g. a limited company normally does)? If No → joint operation.
  3. If Yes → do the terms of the contractual arrangement override the legal form, giving parties rights to assets/obligations for liabilities in specified proportions? If Yes → joint operation (HKFRS 11.B25–B26, Exhibit 25.3).
  4. If No → other facts and circumstances: is the arrangement designed so that (a) its activities primarily aim to provide the parties with output, and (b) it depends on the parties on a continuous basis for settling its liabilities? If both Yes → joint operation (HKFRS 11.B31–32). Otherwise → joint venture.

3. Measurement — Accounting by each party (consolidated / equity-accounted FS)

Joint operator (HKFRS 11.20, 23) — recognise, in relation to its interest in the joint operation: - its assets, including its share of any assets held jointly; - its liabilities, including its share of any liabilities incurred jointly; - its revenue from the sale of its share of the output arising from the joint operation; - its share of the revenue from the sale of the output by the joint operation; and - its expenses, including its share of any expenses incurred jointly.

Each element is recognised/measured under the HKFRS applicable to that element (HKFRS 11.23). A joint operator has rights to an asset (or share thereof), not a "right to use" — e.g. machinery used in a joint operation is accounted for under HKAS 16, not HKAS 38 (HKFRS 11.BC39). The share follows the contractual rights/obligations, which may differ from the ownership interest; proportionate consolidation is not permitted.

Participant in a joint operation without joint control (HKFRS 11.23): - If it has rights to assets / obligations for liabilities → account for those shares the same way as a joint operator. - If not → apply the HKFRS applicable to its interest (e.g. HKFRS 9 for a loan).

Joint venturer (HKFRS 11.24; HKAS 28.3, 28.27) — apply the equity method: - Interest initially recognised at cost, adjusted thereafter for the post-acquisition change in the venturer's share of net assets (HKAS 28.3). - P&L includes the venturer's share of profit or loss; OCI includes its share of OCI of the JV (HKAS 28.3). - The venturer's ownership interest (not its "share of" individual assets/liabilities) drives the accounting — opposite of a joint operation (HKFRS 11.24, BC41). - Unrealised gains/losses on upstream transactions (JV → venturer) are eliminated in applying the equity method (e.g. Dr Share of profit of joint venture / Cr Inventory). - JVs are often newly formed vehicles, so implicit goodwill/discount on acquisition and FV adjustments may be less common (but still possible).

Participant in a joint venture without joint control (HKFRS 11.25): - Financial asset → HKFRS 9; or - Associate-type interest (significant influence) → HKAS 28 (equity method may apply).

4. Measurement — Separate financial statements (HKFRS 11.26–27; HKAS 27.6, 27.10)

  • Separate FS may be presented only where the investor has investments in subsidiaries and/or associates and joint venture entities, and only in addition to consolidated/equity-accounted FS. HKFRSs do not require separate FS.
  • Joint venturer in separate FS — accounting policy choice (HKAS 27.10): at cost, or per HKFRS 9 (fair value), or equity method. The choice must be applied consistently to all joint ventures (not arrangement-by-arrangement), but may differ from the policy chosen for associates.
  • Joint operator in separate FS → accounts for its share of assets/liabilities/revenue/expenses exactly as in consolidated FS (HKFRS 11.26–27) — the cost/HKFRS 9/equity-method choice does not apply.
  • A party to only a joint operation (no subsidiary/associate/JV) presents "individual" (company) financial statements, not "separate financial statements".

5. Disclosure requirements

Presentation (HKAS 1.54, 82, 82A) — face of the FS must show: - the carrying amount of all investments accounted for using the equity method (JVs may be grouped with associates in one line item); - the share of profit or loss of equity-accounted JVs (and associates); and - the share of OCI of equity-accounted JVs (and associates), split between items that will / will not be reclassified to P&L. - Amounts relating to joint operations are NOT separately reported.

Disclosure (HKFRS 12.1, 12.7, 12.20, 12.21–23; HKAS 24.17) — objective: enable users to evaluate the nature of, and risks associated with, the interests and their effects on financial position, performance and cash flows: - Significant judgements and assumptions in determining whether an interest is a joint arrangement and its type (JO vs JV) when structured through a separate vehicle — disclose any changes in assessment (HKFRS 12.7); - Nature, extent and financial effects of interests in joint arrangements, incl. particulars (name, activity, place of business, ownership interest) (HKFRS 12.20); - Summarised financial information for each material JV (based on the JV's HKFRS-compliant FS, adjusted for policy differences and equity-method adjustments such as FV adjustments, but NOT adjusted for intercompany transactions with the venturer), plus a reconciliation to the carrying amount (HKFRS 12.21–23); - Significant restrictions on the JV's ability to transfer funds; unrecognised share of losses; commitments; and contingent liabilities relating to JVs (HKFRS 12.21–23); - HKAS 24.17: nature of the related party relationship and transactions/balances (incl. commitments) with JV partners; - No separate quantitative disclosure required for joint operations (qualitative general information suffices, subject to the HKFRS 12 objective).

易混淆概念對比

對比一：Joint Operation vs Joint Venture

Joint Operation Joint Venture
Rights / obligations Rights to assets; obligations for liabilities (HKFRS 11.14–15) Rights to net assets only (HKFRS 11.14, 16)
Party's name Joint operator Joint venturer
Accounting basis "Share of" each asset/liability/revenue/expense per contractual share (may differ from ownership %) — HKFRS 11.20 Ownership interest in net assets via equity method — HKFRS 11.24, HKAS 28
Balance sheet effect Gross-up: own line-by-line shares of assets/liabilities One line: "Investment in joint venture" (equity accounted)
P&L effect Share of revenue and expenses line-by-line One line: "Share of profit of joint venture"
Separate vehicle? Not structured through a separate vehicle → must be JO; but a separate vehicle can still be a JO if legal form/contract/facts give rights to assets & liabilities Requires a separate vehicle whose legal form gives rights to net assets, not overridden by contract/facts
Separate FS Same accounting as consolidated FS (share of A/L/R/E) Policy choice: cost / HKFRS 9 / equity method (HKAS 27.10)
Presentation Amounts not separately reported Separate line items with associates (HKAS 1.54, 82, 82A)

💬 點樣分辨： 唔好睇個名（"joint venture company" 都未必係 JV），要問「我直接擁有資產/孭住負債，定只係擁有淨資產嘅權益？」考試伏位： (1) 有 separate vehicle ≠ 一定係 JV——若 output 全部賣俾股東、靠股東持續找數，照樣係 JO（HKFRS 11.B31–32）；(2) JO 份額跟合約權利義務，唔係跟持股比例（60/40 權利 vs 50/50 持股 → 用 60/40）；(3) proportionate consolidation 已禁止，JO 唔係按比例合併條線咁簡單，係逐項認自己份額。

對比二：Control vs Joint Control vs Significant Influence

Control (HKFRS 10) Joint control (HKFRS 11.7) Significant influence (HKAS 28)
Definition Power over relevant activities + exposure to variable returns + ability to affect returns Contractually agreed sharing of control; decisions on relevant activities need unanimous consent of parties sharing control Power to participate in financial & operating policy decisions, but not control or joint control
Unilateral power? One party can decide alone No single party can decide alone; each can block the others Cannot decide alone, cannot block; influence only
Contractual arrangement required? No Yes — essential No
Resulting accounting Consolidation (subsidiary) Joint operation (line-by-line shares) or joint venture (equity method) Associate → equity method
Blocking rights only Not control Not joint control if rights are protective only May indicate influence

💬 點樣分辨： Control = 我一個人話事；Joint control = 冇人單獨話事，相關活動要一致同意；Significant influence = 有份參與但話唔到事。考試伏位： (1) voting threshold 可以由多於一個組合達成（如 D+E 或 D+F 都得）→ 合約必須指明邊個組合要一致同意，否則唔係 joint arrangement（HKFRS 11.B8）；(2) 只能「否決/保護性權利」≠ joint control；(3) 唔需要所有投資者都有 joint control——可以有純 participant。
