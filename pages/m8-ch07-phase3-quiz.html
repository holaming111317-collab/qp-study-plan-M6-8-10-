M8 Chapter 7: Audit Procedures for Specific Balances and Transactions — Phase 3 Quiz

Rules: Closed-book · 15 minutes · 5 questions · Submit as 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. … 5. … — 超時如實申報

Question 1 (MCQ)

You are the audit senior of Harbour Trading Limited, a Hong Kong wholesaler of kitchen appliances. You have completed a positive trade receivables confirmation exercise for the year ended 31 December 20X4. A major customer, whose year-end balance of HK$420,000 is material, has signed and returned the confirmation directly to your firm agreeing the balance in full. The audit junior concludes that no further audit work is needed on this balance.

Which of the following correctly evaluates the audit junior's conclusion?

A. The conclusion is appropriate, because a positive confirmation returned directly to the auditor provides sufficient appropriate audit evidence over existence, rights and obligations, and valuation. B. The conclusion is inappropriate, because the confirmation does not verify the valuation assertion; the auditor should perform additional procedures such as inspecting post-year-end cash receipts and assessing the expected credit loss allowance. C. The conclusion is appropriate, provided the confirmation was prepared on the client's letterhead and the customer is not a related party of Harbour Trading Limited. D. The conclusion is inappropriate, because a positive confirmation only verifies the completeness assertion; the auditor should have used a negative confirmation request to verify existence.

Question 2 (MCQ)

You are auditing Pacific Catering Supplies Limited for the year ended 31 March 20X5. Last year's letter of comment highlighted serious deficiencies in the purchases cycle, including purchase invoices being recorded late. The audit engagement partner is concerned that trade payables may be understated at the reporting date and asks you to design a substantive procedure that directly addresses this risk.

Which of the following procedures is the MOST appropriate response to the partner's concern?

A. Select a sample of trade payable balances from the year-end purchase ledger listing and agree them to supplier invoices and goods received notes. B. Select a sample of goods received notes dated immediately before the year-end and trace them to inclusion in the year-end trade payables balance and purchases records. C. Cast the trade payables listing and agree the total with the general ledger and the financial statements. D. Inspect a sample of purchase orders for evidence of authorisation by a responsible official before the order was placed.

Question 3 (Calculation / short answer)

Summit Manufacturing Limited depreciates its plant and machinery on a straight-line basis over eight years with no residual value. The total cost of plant and machinery per the non-current assets register at 31 December 20X4 is HK$1,200,000. There were no additions or disposals during the year. The depreciation charge for plant and machinery recorded in the statement of profit or loss is HK$148,000.

(a) Calculate the expected depreciation charge for the year using a proof in total approach. (b) State the difference between the expected charge and the recorded charge, and whether plant and machinery depreciation is overstated or understated. (c) State ONE appropriate audit response to the difference identified.

Question 4 (MCQ)

Golden Bowl Restaurants Limited is being sued by a customer who slipped in one of its branches in November 20X4, claiming HK$800,000 in damages. The amount is material to the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 20X4. The company's lawyers have advised in writing that, based on the evidence available, it is possible but not probable (approximately 30% likelihood) that the court will find the company liable, and the claim is being actively defended. The finance director proposes to do nothing in the financial statements on the grounds that "the claim will probably fail".

Which of the following is the correct treatment in the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 20X4?

A. Recognise a provision of HK$800,000 because a present obligation exists as a result of a past event. B. Recognise a trade payable of HK$800,000 because the claim has been quantified by the customer's lawyers. C. Disclose a contingent liability in the notes to the financial statements, because the claim is material and an outflow of economic benefits is possible but not probable. D. No recognition or disclosure is required because the likelihood of an outflow is below 50%.

Question 5 (Short answer)

The finance director of Vertex Electronics Limited refuses to allow your audit firm to send external confirmation requests to any of the company's customers, stating that the exercise "upsets customers". The audit engagement partner insists that, if confirmations cannot be sent, alternative procedures must be included in the audit programme to verify the existence and valuation of year-end trade receivables.

List FOUR substantive procedures, other than a trade receivables confirmation, that the auditor could perform to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence over the existence and/or valuation of trade receivables. For each procedure, state the assertion(s) it verifies.
