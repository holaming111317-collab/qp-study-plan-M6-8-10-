Q1

Which of the following correctly states the three-fold test for establishing a duty of care under Caparo Industries plc v Dickman (1990) 2 AC 605?

A. Sufficient proximity or neighbourhood between the parties, and the absence of any policy reason that ought to negate or reduce the scope of the duty

B. The plaintiff's damage was foreseeable by the defendant; there was a close and direct relationship of proximity; and it was fair, just and reasonable to impose a duty of care

C. The defendant owed the plaintiff a duty by virtue of a recognised category of liability, and a reasonable person would have foreseen the plaintiff's injury

D. The defendant assumed responsibility towards the plaintiff, the plaintiff relied on that assumption, and the plaintiff suffered pure economic loss as a result

Q2

On 10 June 2023, Marco was injured in a traffic accident negligently caused by Kelvin. Marco now wishes to commence a tort action against Kelvin for his personal injuries. Under the Limitation Ordinance (Cap. 347), which statement is correct?

A. Marco must commence his action within six years of the accident, i.e. by 10 June 2029, under s.4(1) of the Ordinance

B. Marco must commence his action within three years of the accident, i.e. by 10 June 2026, under s.27 of the Ordinance

C. Marco must commence his action within six years of the date he first knew of his injury, under s.27 of the Ordinance

D. No limitation period applies to personal injury claims because they concern bodily integrity, so Marco may sue at any time

Q3

Dora is considering investing her savings in shares of Target Ltd, a private company. At a dinner party, she asks her friend Kelvin, a chartered accountant who has just completed Target Ltd's audit, for his views. Kelvin replies: "Off the record — I accept no responsibility for this — but the company looks financially sound to me." Relying on this remark, Dora purchases shares in Target Ltd. The accounts in fact contain material errors that Kelvin carelessly overlooked during the audit, and Dora loses most of her investment. Dora sues Kelvin in tort. Which of the following is the best analysis?

A. Kelvin is liable: as a professional, he owes a duty of care to anyone whom he can foresee may rely on his statements about the audited company

B. Kelvin is liable: the neighbour principle in Donoghue v Stevenson (1932) AC 562 is, by itself, sufficient to found liability for his careless statement

C. Kelvin is not liable: a social remark can never give rise to tortious liability, regardless of the speaker's profession or the reliance placed on it

D. Kelvin is not liable: under Hedley Byrne & Co Ltd v Heller & Partners Ltd (1964) AC 465, liability for negligent misstatement requires a special relationship with an assumption of responsibility, and Kelvin's express disclaimer of responsibility negated any such assumption

Q4

GuardForce Ltd employs Rex as a security guard at a shopping mall. Rex is required to wear a uniform and to patrol the mall during his shifts. One afternoon, while on duty and in uniform, Rex suspects May, a shopper, of shoplifting. Without any instruction from GuardForce, Rex grabs May's arm to detain her, twisting it and causing her a fractured wrist. May sues GuardForce Ltd. Which of the following is the best analysis?

A. GuardForce is not liable: an employer is never vicariously liable for an employee's intentional torts, which are by definition outside the course of employment

B. GuardForce is not liable: under the Salmond test, an employee's tort is within the course of employment only if the employer authorised the specific wrongful act, and GuardForce gave no such authorisation

C. GuardForce is liable: under the Close Connection Test (Lister v Hesley Hall Ltd (2002) 1 AC 215), Rex's tort was so closely connected with his employment — committed on duty, in uniform, in the course of dealing with a suspected theft on the premises — that it is fair and just to hold GuardForce vicariously liable

D. GuardForce is liable only if it can be shown that GuardForce itself was negligent in hiring or supervising Rex; otherwise an employer cannot be liable for an act it did not commit

Q5

Fortune Logistics Ltd stores valuable electronic goods for its customers in its warehouse. Under its contract with the building's management company, SecureCo Ltd, SecureCo must provide overnight security guards at the warehouse. One night, the sole guard on duty falls asleep at his post, leaving the warehouse entrance unmonitored. Thieves enter and steal goods belonging to BrightTech Ltd, a customer of Fortune Logistics. BrightTech sues SecureCo in negligence. SecureCo argues that the thieves' criminal act is a novus actus interveniens that breaks the chain of causation between any negligence on its part and BrightTech's loss. Which of the following is the best analysis?

A. SecureCo's argument succeeds: a deliberate criminal act of an independent third party always breaks the chain of causation as a matter of law

B. SecureCo's argument fails: the burglary was a highly foreseeable consequence of SecureCo's failure to safeguard the premises, so the intervening criminal act does not break the chain of causation (Hui Kay Cheong, Herman v Chi Wo Properties Ltd (1992) HKDCLR 51)

C. SecureCo's argument succeeds: SecureCo owed no duty of care to BrightTech because BrightTech's loss is pure economic loss, which is never recoverable in negligence

D. SecureCo's argument fails, but only because SecureCo can be shown to have intended the theft; without such intention the criminal act would break the chain
