M10 Chapter 5: Aspects of Agency Law — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

Phase 1 速查表

Learning Objectives

  1. Analyse the essential elements of an agency relationship and the four ways an agency can be created (by written agreement, by estoppel, by ratification, by necessity).
  2. Distinguish between actual authority (express and implied), usual authority and apparent authority, and apply them to third-party scenarios.
  3. Explain the fiduciary duties, rights and liabilities of an agent and the corresponding duties and rights of a principal.
  4. Apply the disclosed principal and undisclosed principal rules to determine who may sue and be sued on the contract.
  5. Advise on an agent's personal liability, including breach of warranty of authority, with statutory and case authority.

必背考點

A. Relevant Ordinances（考試要 pinpoint 到 Cap. + section）

Ordinance Key provision Effect
Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) s.7 Every partner is an agent of the firm and of his/her other partners for the purpose of the business of the partnership; one partner's acts bind all partners.
Sale of Goods Ordinance (Cap. 26) ('SOGO') s.23 A sale by an agent without actual authority from the owner (principal) still gives the buyer (third party) good title if the sale is within the agent's apparent authority or usual authority.
Powers of Attorney Ordinance (Cap. 31) — Principal = donor of the power; agent = donee. A Power of Attorney is conclusive evidence of the agency, subject to contrary evidence (e.g. revocation).
Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41) — Principal = insurance company; agent = insurance agent.
Factors Ordinance (Cap. 48) — Principal = owner of goods; agent = mercantile agent.
Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) ('POBO') s.9 An offence for an employee (agent), without lawful authority or reasonable excuse, to solicit or accept an advantage/reward to do an act in relation to the principal's (employer's) affairs — e.g. secret money from a third party.
Estate Agents Ordinance (Cap. 511) ('EAO') s.45 An estate agent must execute an estate agency agreement with the client before the agent can pursue a claim against the client for remuneration.
Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571) — Principal = client; agent = licensed person.
Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) ('CO') — Individual directors derive authority from: (1) the company as principal, (2) the articles, (3) the CO. An individual director (except CEO/MD) has no actual authority to act for the company without board authorisation (usually evidenced by board minutes).

B. Leading cases（full citation 以 LP 所引為準）

Case Principle
Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park Property Ltd (1964) Agency by estoppel / ostensible (apparent) authority: the company held out director K as MD by paying past contracts he signed; the company was bound.
Kelner v Baxter (1866) Ratification impossible where the principal did not exist at the time of the contract (pre-incorporation); promoters personally liable.
Gimex Development Ltd v Cua Wai Tai & Another (2000) Ratification by acquiescence: principal's failure to object to appointment of a sub-agent amounted to ratification.
Great Northern Railway v Swaffield (1874) Agency by necessity — three conditions: (1) agent entrusted with principal's property; (2) emergency and principal could not be contacted; (3) agent acted bona fide in the principal's interest.
Ireland v Livingston (1872) Where a written order to an agent is ambiguous (two possible meanings) and the agent honestly acts on one, the principal cannot repudiate the act as unauthorised.
Wong Wai Hing & Another v Hui Wei Lee (2001) Where the agent (debt collector) was expressly authorised and the agreement did not restrict the class of acts, the principal was liable for the agent's acts (even criminal acts in the course of the authorised task).
Hely-Hutchinson v Brayhead Ltd (1968) Implied actual authority: board's consent/acquiescence in a chairman acting as MD over a long period gave him implied actual authority to bind the company.
Panorama Developments (Guildford) Ltd v Fidelis Furnishing Fabrics (1971) Apparent authority by holding out: a company secretary had apparent authority to hire cars on the company's credit; the company had to pay.
Chaudhry v Prabhakar (1988) Agent's duty of care and skill: even an unpaid agent friend with car knowledge breached the duty by recommending a car he should have known was accident-damaged.
Boardman v Phipps (1966) Fiduciary may not earn secret profit or allow conflict between personal interest and fiduciary duty; liable to account for the profit.
Cook v Deeks (1916) Directors must account to the company for secret profit; a self-serving resolution ratifying their own act was ineffective.
Regal Hastings Ltd v Gulliver (1942) (also cited in LP exam practice as Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver [1967] 2 AC 134) Directors/agents must not make unauthorised profit from their position; must account for the profit to the principal.
Collen v Wright (1857) Breach of warranty of authority: agent who wrongly believed he had authority was liable in damages to the third party.
Watteau v Fenwick (1893) Undisclosed principal: once the principal is established, ordinary agency rules apply; the principal is liable for the agent's acts even though the third party did not know of the agency.

C. IRAC application points

1. Formation of agency（4 ways — Issue: "how was the agency created?"） - By written agreement: express appointment (written or verbal), agent acts for remuneration/commission. - By estoppel (holding out): principal, by words or conduct, represents to a third party that the agent has authority; the third party relies on it in good faith and incurs loss. Key: the representation must come from the principal (or someone authorised by the principal), not from the agent himself. - By ratification: agent acts without/exceeding authority; principal later approves with full knowledge. Four conditions: 1. The principal must exist at the time of the transaction and the agent must have been expressly contracted as an agent. 2. The principal must have been made aware of all material facts of the transaction. 3. The principal must ratify the whole transaction, not part of it. 4. The principal must ratify within a reasonable time. Effect: contract binding on principal and third party; agent's liability absolved. - By necessity: urgent circumstances; authority presumed to protect the principal's property (three conditions in Great Northern Railway v Swaffield (1874)).

2. Types of authority（Issue: "was the principal bound?"） - Express actual authority: expressly agreed acts; scope derived from the contract. - Implied actual authority: implied from the relationship of the parties or from the agent's position (e.g. law firm charging disbursements; Hely-Hutchinson v Brayhead Ltd (1968)). - Usual authority: a type of implied actual authority that comes with the position the agent occupies in a trade/business/profession; may exceed actual authority to get the work done. - Apparent (ostensible) authority — three requirements: 1. The principal (or someone authorised) represented to the third party that the agent had authority — by words, conduct, or previous dealings. 2. The third party relied on the representation. 3. The third party relied on it to his/her detriment. Note: apparent authority can survive the end of actual authority if the principal does not notify third parties (LP analysis of the ex-MD ordering wine after leaving).

3. Duties of agent（fiduciary in nature） 1. Follow the principal's instructions (breach = breach of agency; damages, e.g. the price difference). 2. Exercise duty of care and skill (Chaudhry v Prabhakar (1988)). 3. Avoid conflict of interest — even if the principal benefits from the transaction (Boardman v Phipps (1966)). 4. Avoid secret profit — no benefit without the principal's consent (Regal Hastings Ltd v Gulliver (1942)); possible POBO (Cap. 201) s.9 offence. 5. Account for transactions — account for all money received and keep proper records (Cook v Deeks (1916)).

4. Rights of agent / Duties of principal（mirror image） - Agent's rights: remuneration (only when the transaction is complete; merely introducing a purchaser is not enough), indemnity for reasonable expenses/losses (not for unauthorised or negligent acts), and lien over the principal's goods/documents lawfully in the agent's possession until outstanding amounts are settled. - Principal's duties: pay remuneration (agreed or customary), pay indemnity (except unauthorised actions or agent's default/negligence).

5. Liabilities of agent & termination consequences - Personal liability: (1) acting for a non-existing principal (Kelner v Baxter (1866)); (2) acting in excess of authority (if not ratified → breach of warranty of authority); (3) acting for an undisclosed principal; (4) committing a tortious act outside authority. - Breach of warranty of authority (Collen v Wright (1857)): agent represents he/she has authority but has none; third party relies and suffers loss; remedy = damages (actual loss). Agent's honest but mistaken belief is no defence. - Principal's rights on breach: sue for breach of agency (agency terminated, damages per contract law) and recover any secret profit. - Disclosed principal (named or unnamed): agent drops out; only principal and third party can sue/be sued (privity of contract). - Undisclosed principal (Watteau v Fenwick (1893)): agent may sue and be sued; the third party, on discovering the principal, may elect to sue the principal instead; the undisclosed principal may intervene and sue the third party directly, whereupon the agent loses his/her right of action.

易混淆概念對比

Table 1 — Ratification vs Agency by Estoppel / Apparent Authority（Jun 2024 考生大伏位：ratification 誤答為 estoppel）

分辨點 Ratification Agency by Estoppel / Apparent Authority
Time of the key act Agent acts without authority first; principal approves afterwards Principal's representation comes first (words/conduct/past dealings); agent then acts within that appearance of authority
Who makes the representation? No prior representation needed — the principal simply adopts the act later The principal (not the agent) must have held the agent out to the third party
Third party's reliance Not required; ratification works even if the third party knew the agent had no authority Third party must rely on the representation to his/her detriment
Principal's existence Principal must exist at the time of the transaction (Kelner v Baxter (1866)) Principal exists and makes the representation before the contract
Leading case Kelner v Baxter (1866); Gimex Development Ltd v Cua Wai Tai (2000) Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park Property Ltd (1964); Panorama Developments (1971)
考試伏位（繁中） 見到「agent 越權 → principal 事後知情認可」就要答 ratification，唔好因為有「principal 嘅行為」就衝去答 estoppel 見到「principal 一早通知咗 supplier 呢個人係 MD/經理」先係 holding out；如果 representation 只係來自 agent 自己，estoppel 唔成立

Table 2 — Disclosed Principal vs Undisclosed Principal（Dec 2024 考生完全唔識 undisclosed principal）

分辨點 Disclosed Principal Undisclosed Principal
Third party's knowledge Knows the agent acts for a principal (name disclosed or existence disclosed without name) No actual notice of the principal's existence; agent appears to be the principal
Who can sue/be sued on the contract? Only principal and third party (privity of contract); agent drops out Agent may sue and be sued; third party may elect to sue the principal instead after discovering him/her
Principal's right to intervene Not needed — principal is already a party Undisclosed principal may intervene and sue the third party; the agent then loses his/her right of action
Authority analysis Actual/apparent authority in the normal way Ordinary agency rules still apply once the principal is established (Watteau v Fenwick (1893))
考試伏位（繁中） 「disclosed」唔一定要講出名——講咗「我係代人簽」但冇講名都係 disclosed 第三人發現 principal 之後係二揀一（agent 或 principal），唔係兩個都告埋；principal 介入後 agent 就冇得再告 third party

Phase 2 閃卡

Flashcard 1 / 共 8 張
Question

What are the four ways in which an agency relationship can be created?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 記口訣「W-E-R-N」：Written、Estoppel、Ratification、Necessity。考試通常第一句就問 agency 點形成，呢四個係必背框架。

Flashcard 2 / 共 8 張
Question

What are the four conditions for an effective ratification, and which case shows what happens if the principal did not exist?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 四個條件：存在、知情、全盤、及時。公司未註冊就「代公司」簽約，ratify 唔到，promoters 自己孭。

Flashcard 3 / 共 8 張
Question

When does agency by estoppel arise, and whose representation counts?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 重點係「holding out 要由 principal 做」。Agent 自己話自己有權係冇用嘅——呢個係考試最常見嘅陷阱位。

Flashcard 4 / 共 8 張
Question

What are the three conditions for agency by necessity under Great Northern Railway v Swaffield (1874)?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 三個條件：管有財產、緊急兼聯絡唔到、真誠為 principal 行事。例子：凍肉車壞雪櫃，司機自己買冰保住啲貨，可以向 principal 攞返啲使費。

Flashcard 5 / 共 8 張
Question

What are the three requirements to establish apparent (ostensible) authority?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 Apparent authority 係「third party 眼中所見嘅權限」。Employee 離職後如果 principal 冇通知 third party，apparent authority 可能仍然存在，principal 照樣被綁住。

Flashcard 6 / 共 8 張
Question

List the five duties an agent owes to the principal, with one supporting case for the fiduciary duties.

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 Fiduciary duties 係 strict obligation：就算 principal 喺交易中有著數，只要 agent 有利益衝突或者收咗 secret profit 都係 breach，利潤要全數交出。

Flashcard 7 / 共 8 張
Question

What must a third party prove to sue an agent for breach of warranty of authority, and what is the remedy?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 唔使證明 agent 存心呃人——honest mistake 都照賠。所以呢個係 third party 告唔到 principal 嗰陣嘅後備武器。

Flashcard 8 / 共 8 張
Question

In an undisclosed principal situation, who may sue and be sued on the contract?

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer

💬 Undisclosed = third party 完全唔知有 principal。佢發現之後可以「二揀一」；principal 都可以主動跳出嚟告 third party，一跳 agent 嘅告人權就冇咗。

Principal 幕後嗰個，授權人哋幫佢做嘢嘅人 The principal is bound by contracts made by the agent within actual or apparent authority.
Agent / Fiduciary duty 受託代 principal 行事嘅人；fiduciary duty 即係要百分百忠誠、唔可以為自己袋錢 An agent owes fiduciary duties to the principal and must account for any secret profit.
Ratification 事後追認：agent 越權咗，principal 知情後照單全收 By ratifying the purchase, the principal adopted the agent's unauthorised act and was bound by the contract.
Apparent (ostensible) authority 表面權限：third party 合理相信 agent 有權，因為 principal 做過啲嘢令佢咁諗 The company held the manager out as having authority, so he contracted with apparent authority.
Agency by estoppel / holding out 禁反言代理：principal 令人以為某人有代理權，之後唔准反口 Agency by estoppel arises where the principal's own representation, not the agent's, induces the third party's reliance.
Breach of warranty of authority 權限保證違反：agent 話自己有權其實冇，third party 有損失就可以告佢 The third party recovered damages from the agent for breach of warranty of authority.
Lien 留置權：principal 冇找數，agent 可以扣住佢啲貨或文件 The unpaid auctioneer exercised a lien over the cheque until his remuneration was settled.
Undisclosed principal 隱名本人：third party 根本唔知有 principal 存在 Where the principal is undisclosed, the agent may sue and be sued on the contract.
