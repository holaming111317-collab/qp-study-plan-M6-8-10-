Exam Lens — HKICPA QP Module 6 Financial Accounting (Associate Level)

Purpose: Briefing for quiz item writers (5-item OTQ chapter quizzes). Based on official past papers (Jun 2023, Jun 2024, Jun 2025), the CBE Pilot Examination Paper (published Sep 2025), and Examination Panelists' Reports (Jun 2023–Jun 2025).

1. Exam Structure

1.1 Format change alert — TWO formats in the source set

Legacy format (Jun 2023 – Jun 2025 sessions) New CBE format (Pilot Paper, published Sep 2025)
Duration 3 hours (Section B timings imply 1.8 min/mark × 100 marks = 180 min) 2 hours 30 minutes (1.5 min/mark stated on cover)
Section A 15 MCQs = 20 marks (20%). Q1–Q10 = 1 mark each (straightforward); Q11–Q15 = 2 marks each (more complex concepts/computations) Objective Type Questions (OTQs) = 63 marks (63%). 48 numbered items (Q1–Q48), mix of 1/2/3-mark items
Section B Written questions = 80 marks (80%). 4–5 compulsory questions of 10–30 marks each Scenario-based / task-based questions = 37 marks (37%). 2 scenarios (Q49 consolidation ≈ 20 marks; Q50 cash flows ≈ 17 marks), each split into sub-tasks (a)–(e)
Delivery Paper-based written answers Computer-based exam (CBE): radio-button MCQs, True/False, select-all-that-apply, drag-and-drop classification, drop-down menus, numeric fill-in fields, journal-entry construction grids, spreadsheet-style statement preparation (typed figures into pre-formatted statement templates)
Compulsory? All questions compulsory All questions compulsory
  • When the format changed: The three most recent live sessions analysed (Jun 2023, Jun 2024, Jun 2025) all used the legacy 20% MCQ + 80% written format. The Pilot Paper (copyright 2025, published September 2025) introduces the new CBE structure (63% OTQ + 37% task-based), i.e. the change takes effect for sessions after June 2025. The Jun 2025 panelists' report explicitly flags "the transition to computer-based examinations" and notes consolidation journal-entry mechanics "remain a critical area of assessment... especially with the transition to CBE."
  • Practical implication for quiz writers: OTQs now carry the majority of marks (63%). Item-writing skill in objective formats (MCQ, multi-select, True/False, numeric entry, drag-and-drop) is now the highest-leverage authoring skill for M6.

1.2 Legacy Section B composition (per session)

  • Jun 2023 (80 marks): Q1 Consolidation + associate (25) | Q2 SPLOCI + SOCIE from trial balance (20) | Q3 Related parties (10) | Q4 Impairment (10) | Q5 Revenue (15)
  • Jun 2024 (80 marks): Q1 Control + consolidation (30) | Q2 PPE revaluation + SOCIE + disclosures (20) | Q3 Conceptual Framework + provisions + borrowing costs (20) | Q4 Lessor finance lease (10)

  • Jun 2025 (80 marks): Q1 Associate definition + consolidation incl. equity method (28) | Q2 Intangibles + statement of cash flows (28) | Q3 Conceptual Framework + change in estimate (12) | Q4 EPS + related party (12)

  • Mark-to-time guide is printed per question (e.g., "28 marks – approximately 50 minutes").

  • Question 1 is always a consolidation question (25–30 marks) — the anchor of Section B in every session reviewed.

2. Question Style

2.1 Command words (legacy Section B)

  • Explain — the dominant command word. Almost always scenario-linked: "Explain, based on the information provided, why...", "Explain, with reference to HKAS 33, how...", "Explain, with computations, how...". Pure regurgitation scores poorly; application to case facts is required.
  • Define — short book-knowledge items: "Define 'liability'...", "Define 'recoverable amount'", "Define 'qualifying asset'" (2–4 marks).
  • Outline / Describe — lists of criteria or disclosure requirements: "Outline the five steps in HKFRS 15", "Describe the recognition criteria of a provision for restructuring", "Outline FOUR disclosure requirements... You are NOT required to draft the disclosure note."
  • Prepare — full statements: consolidated SFP, SPLOCI, SOCIE, statement of cash flows (indirect method). Always with the note "You have to show your workings, including supporting calculations and/or the consolidation adjustment journal entries."
  • Calculate / Compute — targeted numbers: impairment loss, depreciation, gain/loss on disposal, gross/net investment in lease, profit attributable to NCI, EPS.
  • Identify / Determine — used heavily in new-format OTQs.
  • Cross-standard hybrids are common: one scenario, two or three standards (e.g., Jun 2025 Q4 = HKAS 33 + HKAS 24; Jun 2024 Q3 = Conceptual Framework + HKAS 37 + HKAS 23).

2.2 Scenario style

  • Fictional Hong Kong companies with animal/themed names (Peacock, Salmon, Alligator, Baboons, Paddlefish, Supreme Gold...), HK$ amounts, 31 March / 30 September / 31 December year-ends.
  • Scenario length: ~0.5–1.5 pages per written question, including extracts of SFP / SPLOCI / trial balance tables.
  • Accounting policies are stated explicitly in the scenario (cost model vs revaluation model, straight-line, monthly depreciation, NCI at fair value vs proportionate share). Questions are designed so that ignoring a stated policy (e.g., eliminate accumulated depreciation on revaluation; transfer revaluation surplus to retained earnings) loses marks.
  • Numbers are engineered to test specific adjustments: mid-period acquisitions/disposals (monthly depreciation), mark-up on cost (20%) for unrealised profit, deferred consideration with a stated discount rate, options with exercise price vs average market price.

2.3 OTQ design patterns (from Pilot Paper + panelists' topic lists)

  • Single-best-answer MCQ with 4 options (A–D), majority 1 mark; 2-mark items involve multi-step computation.
  • Distractor engineering (recurring patterns):
  • Each distractor = the output of a specific error (e.g., omitting transaction costs from initial measurement; adding instead of deducting; grossing up mark-up vs margin; using full-year instead of pro-rated depreciation; ignoring the revaluation-surplus ceiling on a downward revaluation).
  • "Definition swap" distractors: e.g., NRV definition vs fair value definition (HKFRS 13 wording) offered as alternatives for an HKAS 2 definition question.
  • Boundary/threshold traps: SME-FRF qualifying criteria (banking/insurance licences, size thresholds, shareholder approval); ECL staging (Stage 1/2/3); dilutive vs anti-dilutive options (exercise price vs average market price).
  • Reversal-of-rule traps: impairment reversal capped at depreciated historical cost; revaluation decrease below surplus goes to P&L.
  • NOT-questions are common: "Identify which... would NOT give rise to a temporary difference", "which of the following statements is NOT correct", "which is NOT a measurement classification".
  • True/False items — a single statement on a precise rule (e.g., goodwill on associate acquisition is NOT separately recognised; highest-and-best-use land valuation).
  • Select-all-that-apply (2–3 marks): e.g., items qualifying as cash and cash equivalents; factors indicating finance lease classification; HKFRS 10/12 disclosure requirements.
  • Drag-and-drop classification (up to 3 marks): classify 7 properties as investment property / owner-occupied / inventory.
  • Numeric fill-in (1–3 marks): calculate FIFO inventory value, impairment write-down allocated across a disposal group, revaluation surplus balance.
  • Drop-down selection and journal-entry grid items: pick Dr/Cr accounts and amounts (e.g., FVTPL-designated bond with own-credit-risk split to OCI).
  • Every OTQ cites the governing standard in the stem ("According to HKAS 16...", "In accordance with HKFRS 9...") — mirror this in quiz items.

2.4 How calculation questions are asked

  • Calculations are embedded in a scenario, rarely standalone: the stem gives cost, dates, useful life, residual value, and requires a specific output (carrying amount, impairment loss, depreciation expense, gain/loss on disposal, EPS, lease investment).
  • Written-section calculations must be accompanied by workings / journal entries — markers award method marks via workings.
  • Dates are deliberately mid-year (e.g., acquired 1 July, disposed 1 April) to force pro-rating — monthly depreciation is the norm ("Depreciation is recorded on a monthly basis").

3.2 Takeaways for quiz coverage prioritisation

  • Tier 1 (must-have quiz coverage): Consolidation + associates + business combinations (Ch 22–24); PPE (Ch 5); Intangibles (Ch 9); Cash flows (Ch 21); FS presentation (Ch 20); Revenue (Ch 3); Financial instruments (Ch 10); Income taxes (Ch 16).
  • Tier 2 (tested every year in OTQs): Inventories (4), Investment property (6), Leases (7), Impairment (11), FV measurement (2), Provisions (15), FX (18), EPS (17), Conceptual Framework (1).
  • Tier 3 (rotating): HKAS 8 (13), HKAS 10 (14), HKFRS 5 (12), HKAS 24 (19), HKFRS 11 (25), HKAS 23 (8), SME-FRF.
  • Under the new CBE format (63% OTQ), every chapter is realistically examinable every session — Tier 3 topics can no longer be skipped in quiz banks.

4. Panelist-Reported Weaknesses (by session)

4.1 Jun 2023

  • MCQ weak spots: HKAS 40 classification (PPE vs investment property); HKAS 8 (estimate vs policy change); HKFRS 16 (purchase option impact on ROU asset and depreciation); HKAS 12 (tax base of an asset; deductible vs taxable temporary differences).
  • Q1 Consolidation: basic computations (goodwill, FV differential) fine; profit allocation to NCI was the main failure point. Equity method for associates (HKAS 28) "lower than expected — most candidates appear to not have been aware of the application of the equity method".
  • Q2 HKAS 1: many unaware of OCI statement presentation; SOCIE format unknown ("performance unsatisfactory"). Bond transaction costs — confusion whether to add or deduct from initial bond carrying amount.
  • Q4 HKAS 36: recoverable amount wrongly defined as the lower (not higher) of FVLCD and VIU; no awareness of the ceiling on impairment reversal.
  • Q5 HKFRS 15: most could not adequately outline the five-step model — "standard textbook material" not learned; weak use of HKFRS 15 terminology in journal entries (contract liability etc.).
  • General: wildly variable preparation; blank answers; inability to produce sensible calculations/journal entries; candidates must explain rationale, not state conclusions.

4.2 Jun 2024

  • MCQ weak spots: HKFRS 9 subsequent measurement of financial liabilities and assets; HKFRS 5 measurement requirements; HKAS 1 materiality concepts; HKAS 12 tax base of an asset / deferred tax recognition (repeat weakness).
  • Q1 Control (HKFRS 10): weak awareness — ownership % alone is not control. Consolidation: difficulty with deferred consideration, upstream intra-group equipment transfer, unrealised profit on equipment and its realisation through excess depreciation adjustment, NCI profit allocation (repeat weakness).
  • Q2 HKAS 16: revaluation journal entries wrong when candidates ignored the stated policy to eliminate accumulated depreciation against gross carrying amount, and ignored the transfer of revaluation surplus to retained earnings as the asset is used. SOCIE left blank or wrong format again (same complaint as Jun 2023). PPE disclosure requirements left blank by many.
  • Q3 HKAS 37/23: candidates wrongly concluded a restructuring provision should be recognised merely because the board approved the plan — no grasp of constructive obligation. HKAS 23 flagged as a consistently weak area across multiple reports (commencement of capitalisation; treatment of interest income on specific borrowings).
  • Q4 HKFRS 16 lessor: some left blank; confusion between gross vs net investment in the lease.
  • General: "common sense" approaches fail; technical accuracy and standard references essential; disclosure-note awareness expected at company level.

4.3 Jun 2025

  • MCQ weak spots: HKFRS 16 lease payment concepts (lessee); HKFRS 13 principal market; HKFRS 5 measurement (third consecutive year flagged); HKFRS 15 refund liabilities; HKAS 12 tax base of a liability / deferred tax (third consecutive year flagged).
  • Q1: candidates could identify facts for significant influence but could not link facts to accounting requirements. Consolidation: unrealised profit on downstream inventory sale poorly handled — some attributed the unrealised profit adjustment to NCI incorrectly (downstream = parent-side, no NCI impact). Equity method: most capitalised associate profits, but many failed to eliminate dividend income recorded by the parent and did not know investment in associate must be presented as a separate line in consolidated SFP.
  • Q2: conceptual HKAS 38 answers "disappointing" — could not differentiate definition vs recognition criteria; weak on internally generated brands and training costs. 5. Difficulty Calibration Guide (for a 5-item OTQ chapter quiz)

5.1 What "exam-standard" means for M6 OTQs

  • Anchor to the real paper's structure: legacy Section A = 10 easy (1-mark) + 5 harder (2-mark) items. A 5-item quiz should replicate this gradient:
  • Items 1–3 (Foundational / 1-mark equivalent): single-concept recall or one-step application — a definition, a classification, a recognition criterion, a simple one-step computation (e.g., depreciation for one year, FIFO closing inventory, basic EPS).
  • Item 4 (Intermediate / 1–2-mark equivalent): two-step application — requires selecting the correct rule AND executing a short computation or multi-factor classification (e.g., carrying amount after revaluation decrease below surplus; tax base of a liability; NRV vs cost decision).
  • Item 5 (Exam-standard challenge / 2-mark equivalent): multi-step computation or a NOT-judgement across four plausible statements — mirrors legacy Q11–Q15 and pilot 2–3-mark items. Must include distractors generated from documented candidate errors (Section 4).
  • Target facility: an exam-standard 5-item set should be answerable in ~7–8 minutes (1.5 min/mark under CBE pacing). If a prepared student cannot finish in that time, the items are over-engineered.
  • Cognitive mix: roughly 40% knowledge/definition, 60% application/computation — matching the exam's stated aim of "core foundational technical knowledge... and skills in applying the knowledge to tackle routine professional problems, including computations".

5.2 Item-writing rules to match exam style

  • Always cite the standard in the stem ("In accordance with HKAS 16...", "According to HKFRS 15...").
  • Use mini-scenarios with named HK companies and HK$ amounts, even for 1-mark items (the exam almost never asks a bare definition without a wrapper — but bare-definition True/False items do occur in the pilot).
  • Numbers must be clean but require work: mid-period dates (pro-rating), mark-up vs margin conversions, stated discount rates, stated useful lives.
  • Every distractor must correspond to a real, documented error (use Section 4: e.g., lower-of instead of higher-of for recoverable amount; adding transaction costs to bond instead of deducting; recognising restructuring provision on board approval alone; treating FV change as cash flow; attributing downstream unrealised profit to NCI).
  • Include at least one "NOT"/"except" item or multi-select per quiz occasionally to match pilot formats; vary correct-answer positions.
  • Provide answer explanations that name the standard and the precise paragraph-level rule — the panelists' core complaint is candidates asserting conclusions without rationale.
  • Difficulty ceiling: do not exceed the complexity of pilot 2–3-mark items (e.g., CGU impairment with headquarters allocation, disposal-group write-down allocation, deferred tax on multiple items). M6 is an associate-level module — no professional-level judgement scenarios.

5.3 Chapter-emphasis guidance for quiz quotas

  • Heaviest drill weight: consolidation mechanics (Ch 22–24) even in OTQ form (pilot shows consolidation OTQs: goodwill computation, adjustment JE selection, NCI effects); HKAS 12; HKFRS 9; HKFRS 15; HKAS 16/38/40 property-family; HKFRS 16; HKAS 36; HKFRS 5.
  • Include Conceptual Framework and disclosure-type OTQs in every rotation — these are where candidates actually lose marks.
