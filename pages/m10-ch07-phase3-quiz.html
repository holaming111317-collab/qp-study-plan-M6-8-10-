M10 Chapter 7: Introduction to Employment Law in Hong Kong — Phase 3 Quiz

Rules: Closed-book · 15 minutes · 5 questions · Submit as 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. … 5. … — 超時如實申報

Question 1 (MCQ)

Which of the following persons is excluded from the application of the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57) under s.4(2) of the Ordinance?

A. A shop assistant working 18 hours per week under a continuous contract with a retail chain B. A young person whose contract of apprenticeship is registered under the Apprenticeship Ordinance (Cap. 47) C. A foreign domestic helper employed under a standard two-year employment contract D. A waiter engaged by a restaurant on a monthly-paid oral contract

Question 2 (Calculation / Short Answer)

Marco has been employed by Harbour Textiles Ltd under a continuous contract for 8 reckonable years. He is monthly-paid and his last month's wages were HK$21,000. Harbour Textiles Ltd has closed its Hong Kong factory and dismissed Marco by reason of redundancy. Marco did not refuse any offer of suitable alternative employment.

Calculate the amount of severance payment payable to Marco under the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57), and state whether the statutory maximum affects your answer. Show your workings.

Question 3 (MCQ — Scenario)

Fiona has provided bookkeeping services to Bright Logistics Ltd ('Bright') continuously for two years. Her written agreement states that she is a 'freelance consultant and not an employee'. In practice: Fiona must work at Bright's office every Tuesday and Thursday from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.; she uses Bright's computer and accounting software; she must follow Bright's internal accounting manual and the instructions of Bright's Financial Controller; she is paid a fixed fee of HK$12,000 per month regardless of the volume of transactions; and she is not permitted to send anyone else to do the work in her place. Fiona has registered herself with an MPF scheme as a self-employed person.

Applying the approach endorsed by the Court of Final Appeal in Poon Chau Nam v Yim Siu Cheung (2007) 1 HKLRD 951, which of the following statements is most accurate?

A. Fiona is an independent contractor because the written agreement expressly labels her as a freelance consultant B. Fiona is an independent contractor because she has registered with an MPF scheme as a self-employed person C. Fiona is likely an employee because, looking at all the features of the relationship as a whole — the degree of control, use of Bright's equipment, no right to delegate, fixed monthly remuneration and no financial risk — the overall impression points to a contract of service, and the label is not conclusive D. Fiona's status cannot be determined because the control test is the sole determinative test and Bright does not supervise every entry she makes

Question 4 (MCQ — Scenario)

Kelvin has worked as a cashier at Daily Fresh Supermarket for three years with a clean disciplinary record. One morning, he mistakenly gave a customer HK$200 less in change. The store manager discovered the error that afternoon and immediately dismissed Kelvin without notice or payment in lieu of notice, citing 'dishonesty'. Kelvin had never received any warning before, and there was no evidence that he intended to keep the money.

Which of the following statements is most accurate under the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 57)?

A. The summary dismissal is lawful because s.9(1)(a) of the EO covers fraud and dishonesty, and Kelvin's error falls within that ground B. The summary dismissal is likely unjustified because s.9(1)(a) EO requires very serious misconduct or failure to improve after repeated warnings; a single isolated error by a first offender with no prior warning does not ordinarily justify summary dismissal, which is a measure of last resort C. The summary dismissal is lawful because an employer is under no duty to give a reason for dismissal at the time of termination D. The summary dismissal is automatically void because an employer may never dismiss an employee without first obtaining the Labour Tribunal's approval

Question 5 (MCQ — Scenario)

SecureGuard Ltd advertises for security guards, stating that all applicants must be at least 170 cm tall. Wing, a 160 cm tall woman with ten years of security experience and excellent references, applies and is rejected solely because she does not meet the height requirement. The same requirement is applied to male and female applicants alike.

Which of the following is the best analysis of Wing's position under the Sex Discrimination Ordinance (Cap. 480)?

A. Wing has no claim because the height requirement is applied equally to all applicants, so there is no discrimination B. Wing has a claim for direct sex discrimination because she was treated less favourably than the male applicants C. Wing has a claim for indirect sex discrimination because the apparently neutral height requirement is one with which a considerably smaller proportion of women can comply and it causes her detriment; SecureGuard Ltd can escape liability only if it justifies the requirement under the three-step test (legitimate aim, rational connection and necessity) in Equal Opportunities Commission v Director of Education (2001) 2 HKLRD 690 D. Wing has no claim because the height requirement automatically falls within the genuine occupational qualification exception
