Q1. Under the Sale of Goods Ordinance (Cap. 26) ('SOGO'), which section implies into a contract of sale — where the seller sells goods in the course of a business — a condition that the goods supplied are of merchantable (satisfactory) quality?

A. Section 14(1) B. Section 15(1) C. Section 16(2) D. Section 17(2)

Q2. Mei Ling signed a contract with Harbour Suites Ltd to hire a harbour-view function suite for the evening of the Lunar New Year fireworks display. The written contract stated that the suite was hired "for the purpose of viewing the fireworks display over Victoria Harbour". Three days before the event, the Government announced that, due to an unexpected safety incident at the launch site, the fireworks display was cancelled. Mei Ling now refuses to pay the hire fee, and Harbour Suites Ltd sues her for the price.

Which of the following best states Mei Ling's legal position?

A. She must pay, because the suite was still available for her use and the cancellation of the fireworks display does not affect Harbour Suites Ltd's ability to perform. B. She must pay, because her contract with Harbour Suites Ltd is void for common mistake, and a void contract still requires the price to be paid. C. She is not liable to pay, because the contract was frustrated: the happening of the fireworks display was the common foundation of the contract, and its cancellation discharged both parties automatically (applying Krell v Henry (1903) 2 KB 740). D. She is not liable to pay, because Harbour Suites Ltd committed an actionable misrepresentation by failing to warn her that the fireworks display might be cancelled.

Q3. Kelvin agreed to sell his coffee shop business to Doris. During negotiations, Kelvin told Doris: "The shop's weekly takings average about HK$80,000." Kelvin honestly believed this, but he was relying on year-old figures and had carelessly failed to check the current books; the true average was HK$50,000. Relying on this statement, Doris bought the business from Kelvin and suffered loss. Doris now wishes to claim damages from Kelvin (in addition to rescission).

Which of the following represents Doris's strongest route to damages, and why?

A. The tort of deceit at common law, because Kelvin's false statement was made without any belief in its truth. B. Negligent misstatement at common law, because the burden of proof is on Kelvin and all losses directly flowing from the transaction are recoverable regardless of foreseeability. C. Section 3(2) of the Misrepresentation Ordinance (Cap. 284), because it entitles Doris to damages as of right for a negligent misrepresentation. D. Section 3(1) of the Misrepresentation Ordinance (Cap. 284), because no special relationship need be shown, the burden is on Kelvin to prove he had reasonable grounds for his belief, and damages are assessed as if the misrepresentation had been fraudulent.

Q4. In March, Annie agreed to buy Patrick's flat in Mid-Levels for HK$6,000,000. She paid Patrick HK$600,000, described in the contract as "a deposit and in part payment of the purchase price". In June, before completion, Annie told Patrick that she would not complete the purchase. Patrick immediately put the flat back on the market and sold it two weeks later to a third party for HK$6,100,000. Patrick has therefore suffered no loss. Annie now demands the return of her HK$600,000, arguing that Patrick lost nothing and that keeping the whole sum would be an unenforceable penalty.

Which of the following best states the legal position?

A. Patrick must return the HK$600,000 because, having suffered no loss, retaining it would constitute a penalty and is therefore unenforceable. B. Patrick may forfeit the entire HK$600,000 deposit, because a deposit is an earnest of performance and is liable to forfeiture upon the buyer's default regardless of whether the seller has suffered any loss (applying Polyset Ltd v Panhandat Ltd (2002) 5 HKCFAR 234 and Howe v Smith (1884) 27 Ch D 89). C. Patrick must return the HK$600,000, less only his actual expenses, because under s.16(2) of the Law Amendment and Reform (Consolidation) Ordinance (Cap. 23) a payee may retain no more than a just sum limited to his expenses. D. Patrick must return the HK$600,000, subject only to a right to set off damages, because all advance payments are impliedly conditional upon completion of the contract (applying Dies v British and International Mining and Finance Corp Ltd (1939) 1 KB 724).

Q5. TechStyle Ltd placed online advertisements stating: "Flash Sale — tomorrow only: our flagship smartwatch at HK$999 (usual price HK$3,330)!" The advertisement said nothing about limited stock. TechStyle Ltd in fact held only five units of that model in the whole of Hong Kong, and all five were sold within the first ten minutes of the sale. Hundreds of consumers who visited TechStyle Ltd's stores and website later that day were told the model was sold out.

Identify the offence committed by TechStyle Ltd under the Trade Descriptions Ordinance (Cap. 362), citing the relevant section, and state the maximum penalty for such an offence.
