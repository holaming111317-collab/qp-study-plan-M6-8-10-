M10 Chapter 12: Listed Companies and the Regulation of Financial Markets — Phase 3 Quiz

Rules: Closed-book · 15 minutes · 5 questions · Submit as 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. … 5. … — 超時如實申報

Q1. Which of the following statements about the Listing Rules of the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong is CORRECT?

A. The Listing Rules are primary legislation enacted by the Legislative Council, and breach of them is a criminal offence. B. The Listing Rules are made pursuant to s.23 of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571); breach does not give rise to criminal or civil liability per se, but the HKEX may impose regulatory sanctions such as a public censure or delisting. C. The Listing Rules are subsidiary legislation made by the Securities and Futures Commission, enforceable through proceedings in the Market Misconduct Tribunal. D. The Listing Rules bind only companies incorporated in Hong Kong and have no application to overseas issuers listed on the HKEX.

Q2. Under Part XV of the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571), a "substantial shareholder" of a listed corporation who must disclose his interest is a person interested in what percentage of any class of voting share capital of the listed corporation?

A. 10% or more B. 25% or more C. 5% or more D. 30% or more

Q3. Wendy is the company secretary of Harvest Bright Ltd, a company listed on the Main Board of the HKEX. At a board meeting she learns that the High Court has just handed down a confidential judgment ordering Harvest Bright Ltd to pay damages equal to 40% of its net assets. No announcement has been made. Before the company announces the judgment, Wendy sells her entire holding of Harvest Bright shares through her brokerage account, avoiding a substantial loss when the price falls 30% on the announcement. Applying the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571), which of the following is the MOST accurate analysis of Wendy's conduct?

A. No liability arises because Wendy did not make any profit from the sale. B. Wendy has engaged in insider dealing under s.270 of the SFO: she is a connected person (an officer/employee) who dealt in the listed securities whilst possessing information she knew to be specific, non-public and price-sensitive, and the absence of a profit motive does not assist her because her purpose was to avoid a loss. C. Wendy has engaged in false trading under s.274 of the SFO because her sale created a misleading appearance of active trading in the shares. D. Wendy has engaged in stock market manipulation under s.278 of the SFO because her sale was likely to reduce the price of the shares.

Q4. Mr. Fu holds 18% of the voting rights of Sunrise Logistics Ltd, a listed company. His business partner Ms. Kam holds 14%. They have a long-standing arrangement under which they consult each other and vote together on all resolutions of the company, and they have just jointly acquired a further 2% through a company they jointly control. None of the acquisitions has been disclosed to the market as giving rise to any offer obligation. Applying the Codes on Takeovers and Mergers and Share Buy-backs, which of the following is the MOST accurate conclusion?

A. No obligation arises because neither Mr. Fu nor Ms. Kam individually holds 30% or more of the voting rights. B. Mr. Fu and Ms. Kam are persons acting in concert; their holdings are aggregated (34% in total), so the 30% threshold has been crossed and a mandatory general offer to all shareholders of Sunrise Logistics Ltd is triggered. C. The only obligation is for Mr. Fu and Ms. Kam to disclose their interests under Part XV of the SFO, because their individual holdings each exceed 5%. D. Sunrise Logistics Ltd must convene a general meeting to approve the acquisitions by special resolution before they take effect.

Q5. For several weeks, Kelvin bought and sold the same listed warrants every trading day using two securities accounts under his own name, selling to himself at substantially the same prices. His trades accounted for the bulk of the daily turnover in those warrants. The evidence shows Kelvin's dominant purpose was to generate commission rebates offered by his broker for high trading volume, and there is no evidence he intended to mislead the market about trading activity. Kelvin is charged with creating a false or misleading appearance of active trading contrary to ss.295(1)(a) and (6) of the SFO. Applying HKSAR v Fu Kor Kuen Patrick (2012) 15 HKCFAR 524, which of the following is the MOST accurate statement of Kelvin's position?

A. Kelvin is inevitably guilty: any wash sale is conclusively an offence and no defence is available once self-dealing is proved. B. Kelvin's conduct falls within the deeming provision (s.295(5)), but he may rely on the statutory defence (s.295(7), mirroring s.274(6)) if he proves on the balance of probabilities that the purposes of his trades did not include creating a false or misleading appearance — and a genuine economic purpose of earning rebates may satisfy that defence. C. Kelvin is not guilty because price rigging under s.275, not false trading, is the only possible charge where beneficial ownership does not change. D. Kelvin is not guilty because trading between accounts under the same name can never amount to a "transaction in securities" within the meaning of the SFO.
