M10 Chapter 6: Money Laundering, Bribery and Corruption — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

Phase 1 速查表

Learning Objectives

  1. Apply the key provisions of Hong Kong's anti-money laundering legislation (DTROPO, OSCO, AMLO, UNATMO) to factual scenarios, including the offences, disclosure obligations and penalties.
  2. Explain the obligations of accountants and other designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs) under the AMLO and the HKICPA AML Guidelines — customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring, suspicious transaction reporting and record-keeping.
  3. Analyse the role of accountants as trust or company service providers (TCSPs) under the TCSP licensing regime.
  4. Apply the offences under the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) to private-sector and public-sector scenarios, using the IRAC approach.
  5. Describe the roles and powers of the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU) and the Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC).

必背考點

A. Anti-Money Laundering 四大法例（Offences / Obligations / Penalties / Court Action）

1. Drug Trafficking (Recovery of Proceeds) Ordinance (Cap. 405) ('DTROPO') - s.25(1) — offence: a person deals with property, knowing or having reasonable grounds to believe that the whole or part of the property directly or indirectly represents proceeds of drug trafficking. - s.25(3) — penalty on conviction upon indictment under s.25(1): max 14 years' imprisonment + fine of HK$5 million. - s.25A(1) — obligation: a person must disclose to an authorised officer (e.g. police officer, Customs and Excise officer) as soon as it is reasonable if he/she knows or suspects that property represents proceeds of drug trafficking. - s.25A(5) — tipping-off offence: knowing or suspecting that a disclosure has been made, disclosing to any other person any matter likely to prejudice any investigation. - s.25A(8) — penalty for failure to disclose under s.25A(1) (and per Exhibit 6.1, tipping-off): max 3 years' imprisonment + fine of HK$500,000. - s.3 — court power to issue confiscation orders (court must specify a period of less than six months for the defendant to pay). - ss.10 and 11 — Court of First Instance may grant a restraint order (prohibiting dealing with realisable property) or a charging order (charge on realisable property to secure payment to the Government). Knowingly contravening either order: fine of HK$500,000 (or the property value if greater) + 5 years' imprisonment (ss.10(16) and 11(14)).

2. Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) ('OSCO') - Enacted 1994; mirrors DTROPO but covers proceeds of an indictable offence (defined in the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) as one not triable summarily; for DTROPO/OSCO purposes, one that may be tried either summarily or on indictment). - s.25(1) — same dealing offence as DTROPO, but for proceeds of any person's indictable offence. - s.25(3) — same penalty: max 14 years' imprisonment + HK$5 million fine. - s.25A(1) — same disclosure obligation; s.25A(5) tipping-off; s.25A(8) penalty max 3 years + HK$500,000. - s.8 — confiscation orders; s.14 — restraint order / charging order by the Court of First Instance. - 考試伏位：DTROPO 只限 drug trafficking proceeds；OSCO 涵蓋所有 indictable offence proceeds（包括 fraud、theft、tax evasion 等）。毒品案先諗 DTROPO，其他罪行諗 OSCO。

3. Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) ('AMLO') — in force 1 April 2012 - s.5 — criminal offence if a financial institution (FI) knowingly, or with intent to defraud a relevant authority (RA: HKMA / SFC / Insurance Authority), contravenes a specified provision (listed in s.5(11)). - s.5(5) — knowing contravention: max 2 years' imprisonment + HK$1,000,000 fine. - s.5(6) — contravention with intent to defraud RAs: max 7 years' imprisonment + HK$1,000,000 fine. - s.5A (added by the 2018 Amendment Ordinance, effective 1 March 2018) — imposes AML/CTF requirements on DNFBPs: accounting professionals and legal professionals (when preparing/carrying out specified transactions: buying/selling real estate; managing client money, securities or assets; managing bank/savings/securities accounts; organising contributions for creation/operation/management of corporations; creation/operation/management of legal persons or arrangements; buying/selling business entities; trust or company services), estate agents (real estate transactions), and TCSP licensees. - Schedule 2 — Part 2: customer due diligence (CDD); Part 3: record-keeping; FIs/DNFBPs must take all reasonable measures to ensure proper safeguards against contravention and to mitigate ML/TF risks. - s.21 / s.21(2) — RAs may take disciplinary action against FIs: public reprimand, remedial action, pecuniary penalty. s.23 — RAs must follow guidelines when imposing pecuniary penalties. Supervisory fines can reach HK$10 million or 3 times the profit gained / costs avoided. - DNFBPs (accountants, lawyers, estate agents) face no criminal sanctions under AMLO for non-compliance — they are penalised via their own professional mechanisms; TCSPs via the licensing system.

TCSP licensing regime (from 1 March 2018) - s.1, Part 1, Schedule 1 — statutory definition of a TCS business: forming corporations/legal persons; acting/arranging a director, secretary, partner or similar; providing registered office/business address; acting/arranging trustee of an express trust or nominee shareholder (other than for a listed corporation). - s.53B — licensing requirement does not apply to: qualified accounting professionals, qualified legal professionals, the HKSAR Government, authorized institutions (s.2(1) Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155)), licensed corporations (SFO (Cap. 571)) where TCS business is ancillary to principal business, and persons prescribed by regulation. - s.53F(1)–(2) — offence to carry on a TCS business without a licence: fine of HK$100,000 + 6 months' imprisonment. - s.53H / s.53I — fit and proper test: Registrar of Companies considers e.g. ML/TF convictions (in or outside HK), undischarged bankruptcy, failure to comply with AMLO requirements, liquidation/winding-up of a corporate applicant. - s.53O — licence valid for 3 years (or shorter as determined); Registrar may grant, renew, suspend, revoke, or impose conditions. - Registrar may publicly reprimand defaulting TCSPs, order remedial action and impose a pecuniary penalty up to HK$500,000. - 重點：qualified accountants 免攞牌，但唔免合規 — still must comply with CDD, ongoing monitoring and record-keeping under Schedule 2 of the AMLO.

4. United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance (Cap. 575) ('UNATMO') - s.7 — offence: provides or collects property with the intention, or knowing, or having reasonable grounds to believe that the property is to be used to commit terrorist acts. - s.8 — offence: offers property or financial services to benefit a terrorist or terrorist associate. - s.8A — must not, except under licence from the Secretary for Security, deal with property knowing / being reckless as to whether it is terrorist property, owned/controlled by a terrorist or terrorist associate, or held on their behalf/direction. - s.12(1) — offence to fail to report knowledge or suspicion that property is terrorist property to an authorised officer as soon as practicable → penalty s.14(5): max 3 months' imprisonment + a fine. - s.12(5) — tipping-off offence → penalty s.14(6): max 3 years' imprisonment + a fine. - s.6 — Secretary for Security may freeze funds by written notice where there are reasonable grounds to suspect they are terrorist property. - s.13 — on application by the Secretary for Justice, the Court of First Instance may order forfeiture of terrorist property. - ss.14(1) and 14(1A) — max penalty for ss.7, 8, 8A offences: 14 years' imprisonment + a fine.

B. Leading Cases（full citations + ratio）

  1. Seng Yuet Fong v HKSAR (1999) KCQ3, FAMC No. 26 of 1998 (CFA) — 'having reasonable grounds for belief' in s.25(1) DTROPO contains both an objective and a subjective element: (i) grounds that a common-sense, right-thinking member of the community would consider sufficient to lead a person to believe (objective); (ii) the defendant knew those grounds (subjective). Prosecution must prove both.
  2. Oei Hengky Wiryo v HKSAR (No. 2) (2007) 10 HKCFAR 98 (CFA) — under s.25(1) OSCO, the prosecution need NOT prove that the property dealt with was in fact the proceeds of a predicate offence; conviction is possible if the defendant had reasonable grounds to believe it represented proceeds of crime.
  3. HKSAR v Pang Hung Fai (FACC 8 of 2013) (CFA) — (i) the defendant's own perception, belief or prejudice (e.g. long-standing trust in a friend) may be taken into account when assessing 'reasonable grounds to believe'; (ii) the correct test is the 'would believe' test (would a reasonable person looking at the grounds believe the property represented proceeds of an indictable offence?) — the Court of Appeal's 'could believe' test was an inappropriately low standard.
  4. HKSAR v Yang Sigai (2016) HKEC 2068, FACV No. 8 of 2015 (CFA) — where the property came from overseas, the underlying offence need not be identified, and s.25(1) OSCO does not require proof of knowledge of the actual conduct that produced the property.
  5. HKSAR v Yeung Ka Sing Carson (FACC Nos. 5 and 6 of 2015) (CFA) — upheld Oei Hengky Wiryo (no need to prove the predicate offence) and confirmed the Seng Yuet Fong formulation: the accused had grounds for believing, and the grounds must be reasonable such that anyone looking at them objectively would so believe.
  6. HKSAR v Leung Yat Ming & Another (1999) 2 HKLRD 402 — court lifted the corporate veil (company as a cloak of deception) and convicted the defendants under s.9(3) POBO for deceiving their employer (university) by an application containing false statements.

C. Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) ('POBO')

Definitions — s.2 - Advantage: gift, loan, fee, reward or commission (money, security, property or interest); office, employment or contract; payment/release of a loan; service or favour; promise of any of these; exercise of or forbearance from rights/powers/duties. Entertainment (= food or drink, e.g. dinners, or entertainment provided with food and drink) is NOT an advantage. - Agent: a public servant and any person employed by or acting for another (directors are agents of the company; the Board is generally the principal). - Public servant: prescribed officers + employees of public bodies (e.g. MTR, hospitals).

Offences

Section Sector Offence
s.3 Public Prescribed officer soliciting/accepting advantage without the Chief Executive's permission
s.4 Public Public servant soliciting/accepting advantage as inducement/reward for performing duties (offeror also guilty)
s.5 Public Advantage for assistance/influence in relation to a contract (offeror also guilty)
s.6 / s.7 Private Advantage to withdraw/refrain from a tender / from bidding at an auction of a public body
s.8 Private Advantage offered to a public servant of a department the agent is dealing with
s.9(1) Private Agent, without lawful authority or reasonable excuse, solicits/accepts advantage as inducement to/reward for an act in relation to the principal's affairs
s.9(2) Private Offering an advantage to an agent (the mirror offence — the offeror's side)
s.9(3) Private Agent uses any receipt, account or other document with a false or erroneous statement to deceive the principal
s.9(5) Private Leeway: permission applied for and given as soon as reasonably possible after the offer/acceptance
s.10 Public Prescribed officer maintaining a standard of living / assets not commensurate with official emoluments

Other key sections - s.11 — both giver and acceptor guilty even if the purpose of the bribe is not carried out; unavailable defences: no power/opportunity to do the act; no intention to do the act; did not in fact do the act. - s.12 — penalties: ss.5 or 6 → max 10 years + HK$500,000; s.10 → max 10 years + HK$1,000,000; other sections → max 7 years + HK$500,000. - s.19 — no defence that the advantage is customary in any profession, trade, vocation or calling. - s.24 — burden of proving lawful authority or reasonable excuse lies on the accused.

D. JFIU and ICAC

Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU) — set up 1989; joint operation of the HK Police Force and Customs & Excise Department. It is the central hub for suspicious transaction reports (STRs) but has no investigative power; it refers reports to local/overseas enforcement departments. Reports arise under s.25A DTROPO, s.25A OSCO and s.12 UNATMO. 'SAFE' approach: Screen the customer/transaction; Ask questions to clarify; Find out whether the transaction matches expectations; Evaluate whether it is really suspicious.

HKICPA Guidelines on AML/CTF for Professional Accountants (February 2018) — based on guidelines published under s.7 AMLO; form part of the Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants. Breach = not liable to legal proceedings, but admissible as evidence in court proceedings under AMLO. 'must' = mandatory/statutory; 'should/would/may' = guidance/good practice. - Section 620 CDD — know the true identity of each client, business type, source/use of funds; standard CDD for all; SDD for lower risk, EDD for higher risk (e.g. PEPs — individuals with prominent public functions outside mainland China, their family members and close associates). - Section 630 Ongoing Monitoring — review client documents regularly; watch transactions inconsistent with business/risk profile/source of funds; examine abnormally large or unusual transactions and record findings in writing. - Section 640 STRs — must appoint a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO); report to the JFIU without delay; report as soon as reasonably practicable; internal controls to prevent tipping-off; suspicion is more than speculation but less than proof or knowledge. - Section 650 — targeted financial sanctions: name checks against UN Security Council sanctions lists; STR to JFIU if terrorist-related suspicion. - Section 660 Record Keeping — identify/verify beneficial owner; clear and complete audit trail; records available to RAs on a timely basis; retain records at least 5 years after the transaction or cessation of the business relationship.

ICAC — established February 1974 by Governor Sir Murray MacLehose; functions independently and is directly accountable to the Chief Executive (Basic Law). Three-pronged strategy via three departments: Operations (law enforcement — investigates offences under the ICACO (Cap. 204), POBO and ECICO (Cap. 554)), Corruption Prevention (reviews practices/procedures), Community Relations (education and public support). - Investigative powers under POBO: s.13 inspect accounts/documents, require production (s.13(3): obstruction → HK$20,000 + 1 year); s.13A CFI order for IRD material; s.14(1) require suspects to provide property/income/expenditure details; s.14C restraining order on property; s.17(1A) enter and search premises; s.17A surrender of travel documents. - Under ICACO (Cap. 204): s.10 arrest without warrant; s.10A procedure after arrest; s.10AA arrest of persons granted bail (bring before a magistrate within 24 hours); s.12 duties of the Commissioner; s.13 powers of the Commissioner.

E. IRAC Application Points（考試答題框架）

  • Issue: 先認清係 laundering（OSCO/DTROPO）定 terrorist property（UNATMO）定 bribery（POBO）——Exam Lens 指出 Jun 2026 有考生將 anti-corruption 誤判為 money laundering，issue-spotting 係成敗關鍵。
  • Rule: pinpoint section — 答 s.25(1) OSCO 要分 DTROPO（drug trafficking）定 OSCO（indictable offence）；答 PBO 要分 s.9(1)（agent 收受）、s.9(2)（offeror）、s.9(3)（假文件）。
  • Application: 將 facts 逐項套入 elements（dealing? knowledge/reasonable grounds? advantage? principal's affairs? permission?）；記住 suspicion 已足夠觸發 disclosure duty，唔使證實罪行（Oei Hengky Wiryo; HKICPA Guidelines s.640）。
  • Conclusion: 明確落結論 + 指出 penalty（如 s.25(3)：14 年 + HK$5M；s.25A(8)：3 年 + HK$500,000）。

In HKSAR v Pang Hung Fai (FACC 8 of 2013), which test did the CFA endorse for assessing 'reasonable grounds to believe' — the 'would believe' test or the 'could believe' test — and what else may the court consider?

撳卡翻面 👆

Flashcard 7 / 共 8 張
Question

State the four conditions that must all be satisfied for an offence under s.9(1) of the POBO, and the key defences/burden rules.

撳卡翻面 👆
Answer
Conditions (all must be present): 1. The person is an agent (employee or person acting for another — s.2); 2. He/she solicited or accepted an advantage; 3. As an inducement to or reward for an act in relation to the principal's affairs; 4. Without lawful authority or reasonable excuse (principal's permission).

Defences/burden: - s.9(5): permission obtained as soon as reasonably possible after offer/acceptance; - s.11: no defence that the bribe's purpose was not carried out; - s.19: no defence that the advantage is customary; - s.24: burden of proving lawful authority/reasonable excuse is on the accused.

💬 四個條件缺一不可：agent + advantage + principal's affairs + 無批准。Answer 入面要逐項對落 facts。注意「entertainment」（請食飯）唔算 advantage，係常見 distractor。
