M10 Chapter 11: Company Capital and Financing Companies — Phase 3 Quiz

Rules: Closed-book · 15 minutes · 5 questions · Submit as 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. … 5. … — 超時如實申報

Unless otherwise stated, all statutory references are to the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) ("CO").

Q1 (MCQ — 4 options)

Bright Logistics Ltd ("BLL") granted Bank M a charge over "all stock-in-trade and inventory of the company, present and future". The charge instrument expressly allows BLL to sell, replace and deal with the inventory in the ordinary course of its business until Bank M gives notice to the contrary. Which of the following BEST describes this security?

A. A fixed charge, because the instrument identifies the charged assets by description B. A floating charge, because it hovers over a changing class of present and future assets with which BLL may deal in the ordinary course of business C. A legal mortgage, because the charge extends to future assets of the company D. A pledge, because the inventory remains in BLL's possession

Q2 (MCQ — 4 options)

On 2 May, Castor Engineering Ltd created a charge over its book debts in favour of Bank N. The company secretary overlooked the matter and no documents were delivered to the Registrar of Companies until 20 June. No court order extending time has been obtained. Which of the following statements is CORRECT?

A. The charge is void against the liquidator and creditors of the company under s.337(4), although it remains valid as between Bank N and the company B. The charge is void ab initio against all persons, including the company itself C. The charge remains fully valid against everyone; late registration only gives rise to criminal liability under s.337(2) D. The charge is valid because a charge over book debts is not a "specified charge" under s.334

Q3 (MCQ — scenario)

Pelican Retail Ltd has issued ordinary shares and 8% cumulative preference shares. The board proposes to capitalise part of the company's reserves by issuing 500,000 NEW preference shares, ranking pari passu with the existing preference shares, to the existing ordinary shareholders. Ms Lau, an existing preference shareholder, objects, arguing that the proposal is a variation of class rights which requires the written consent of holders of at least 75% of the voting rights of her class or a special resolution at a class meeting under s.180. Which of the following is the BEST analysis?

A. Ms Lau is correct: any issue of new shares of the same class dilutes existing class members and therefore amounts to a variation requiring s.180 consent B. Ms Lau is wrong: following White v Bristol Aeroplane Co Ltd (1953) Ch 65, the proposal affects only the enjoyment of the existing preference shareholders' rights, not the rights themselves, so it is not a variation and no class consent is required C. Ms Lau is correct: because the new shares are issued to ordinary shareholders only, the preference shareholders are unfairly prejudiced and may apply to court under s.182(1) to have the issue disallowed D. Ms Lau is wrong: variation of class rights never requires class consent under the CO; it is governed solely by the articles

Q4 (MCQ — scenario)

Nero Holdings Ltd ("NHL") is the holding company of Nero Foods Ltd ("NFL"), its wholly owned subsidiary. Mr Chan agrees to buy all the shares in NHL from its existing shareholder for HK$8 million, funded by a bank loan. At the bank's request, NFL provides a guarantee of Mr Chan's loan. NFL's directors argue that the guarantee is lawful because NFL's business will benefit from Mr Chan's planned expansion of the group, so the assistance was given for a "larger purpose" in good faith in NFL's interest. Which of the following is the BEST analysis?

A. The guarantee is lawful: it falls squarely within the principal purpose exception in s.278 because the group's expansion is a larger purpose of NFL B. The guarantee is lawful: a subsidiary is always free to guarantee loans for the acquisition of its holding company's shares because s.275(1) only catches the issuing company itself C. The guarantee is prohibited financial assistance under s.275(1) (a guarantee being within s.274(1)); following Ricco (Int'l) Co Ltd v Uni-Harvest Int'l Ltd (2017) 6 HKC 487, the expected business benefit is merely the "reason" for giving the assistance, not a separate "larger purpose", so the s.278 exception fails — although the underlying transaction is not invalid per se (s.276) D. The guarantee is prohibited, and therefore the sale of NHL's shares to Mr Chan is automatically void and cannot be enforced by either party

Q5 (Short answer — 3–5 sentences)

Marble Arch Ltd, a Hong Kong private company, passed a special resolution under s.226 for the court to confirm a reduction of its share capital from HK$10 million to HK$4 million by returning HK$6 to each shareholder per share, on the stated basis that "the company's capital exceeds its needs". The company's latest balance sheet in fact shows accumulated losses of HK$30 million, and its auditors issued a disclaimer of opinion on the financial statements. A creditor objects to the petition. Advise whether the court is likely to confirm the reduction, citing the relevant authority and reasoning.
