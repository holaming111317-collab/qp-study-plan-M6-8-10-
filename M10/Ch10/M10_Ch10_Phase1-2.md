# M10 Chapter 10: Contentious Matters — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

## Phase 1 速查表

### Learning Objectives
1. Apply the rules on a company's contractual capacity and execution formalities (ss.121, 124–129, Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) ('CO')) and the indoor management rule (Royal British Bank v Turquand (1856) 119 ER 775; ss.117–119 CO) to decide whether a company is bound.
2. Analyse when the corporate veil may be lifted by statute (s.275, Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 32) ('CWUMPO')) or at common law (mere façade, evasion of legal obligations, fraud), applying Prest v Petrodel Resources Ltd (2013) UKSC 34, (2013) 2 AC 415.
3. Identify and apply directors' fiduciary duties (good faith, proper purposes, no-conflict, no-profit, no misappropriation) and the statutory duty of reasonable care, skill and diligence (s.465 CO).
4. Evaluate the company's remedies for breach of directors' duties, including avoidance, restitution, disgorgement of profits, equitable compensation and accessory liability (knowing receipt / dishonest assistance).
5. Distinguish and apply shareholders' remedies: the rule in Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461, common law and statutory derivative actions (ss.731–733 CO), unfair prejudice remedies (ss.724–725 CO), members' personal actions, and just and equitable winding-up.

#### B. Leading Cases（full citations as given in the LP）

**Contracting & Indoor Management**
- **Royal British Bank v Turquand (1856) 119 ER 775** — origin of the indoor management rule: outsiders in good faith may assume internal acts (e.g. authorising resolutions) were duly performed; no duty to inspect internal regularity.
- **Northside Developments Pty Ltd v Registrar-General (1990) 170 CLR 146** — rule covers three categories: (1) due appointments of directors/officers/agents; (2) conferral of authority; (3) satisfaction of conditions governing exercise of authority. Seal affixed → contract regarded as entered by the company directly. The rule presupposes actual/ostensible authority; it cannot CREATE authority (also Thanakharn Kasikorn Thai Chamkat v Akai Holdings Ltd (2010) 13 HKCFAR 479).
- **Pacific Foundation Finance Ltd v Fairyoung Holdings Ltd (1999) 3 HKC 448** — rule unavailable to a party with actual or constructive notice of the irregularity.
- **National Commercial Bank Ltd v Albert Hwang (2002) 2 HKLRD 408** — rule needs some act by the company suggesting the outsider was dealing with the company; a stranger impersonating a director does not bind the company.
- Exceptions: (1) actual/constructive notice of irregularity (**Morris v Kanssen (1946) AC 459**; **Wong Kam San v Yeung Wing Keung (2007) 2 HKLRD 267** — dramatic shareholding change put allottees on enquiry; **Bank of East Asia v Labour Buildings (unrep.) HCMP 769/2002** — bank had notice, but company ADOPTED the loan); (2) forgery renders the document a nullity (**Ruben v Great Fingall Consolidated (1906) AC 439**), unless the company held out the forger as authorised (**Lovett v Carson Country Homes Ltd (2009) 2 BCLC 196**); (3) insiders cannot rely on the rule (Morris v Kanssen).
- **Good Lion Ltd v Chung Sai Wing (2008) 6 HKC 443** — incorrect name on the seal does not invalidate execution if the seal was intended to be the company's.

**Corporate Liability (Attribution)**
- **Meridian Global Funds Management Asia Ltd v Securities Commission (1995) 2 AC 500** — three rules of attribution: primary (constitution/company law), general (agency), special (statutory interpretation — whose act/knowledge counts as the company's for that rule). 'Directing mind and will' is only a starting point, not the proper test.
- **Tesco Supermarkets Ltd v Nattrass (1972) AC 153 / Tesco Stores Ltd v Brent LBC (1997) 1 WLR 1037** — directing mind normally = board or MD; a store manager is 'another person', not the company.
- **R v Chung Yat (1978) HKLR 355** — company liable under Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) where directors/foreman acted with knowledge and recklessness.
- **R v Gateway Foodmarkets Ltd (1997) IRLR 189** — statutory duty breached at store-management level attributable to the company.
- **Bilta (UK) Ltd v Nazir (No. 2) (2015) 2 WLR 1168** — where the company is the VICTIM of its directors' fraud, their wrongdoing is NOT attributed to the company as a defence to the company's claim against them.
- **Moulin Global Eyecare Trading Ltd v Commissioner of Inland Revenue (2014) 17 HKCFAR 218** — contrast: directors' fraudulent knowledge WAS attributed to the company (to deny attribution would frustrate the tax statute).
- **Chan Kit Man v Rayable Consultants Ltd and Ajax Engineers & Surveyors Ltd (1999) 2 HKLRD 577** — corporate manslaughter; company convicted, HK$3 million fine; attribution does not exclude the individual agent's own liability.

**Lifting the Corporate Veil**
- **Hashem v Shayif (2008) EWHC 2380** — seven principles: piercing rests on established principles (NOT mere 'interests of justice'); impropriety + control BOTH required; ownership/control alone insufficient; deceptive intent at incorporation unnecessary (façade at time of transaction suffices); pierce only so far as necessary.
- **Prest v Petrodel Resources Ltd (2013) UKSC 34, (2013) 2 AC 415** — veil pierced only where a person under an EXISTING legal obligation/liability deliberately evades it by interposing a controlled company; pierce only for that purpose; only when other remedies are of no assistance.
- **Gilford Motor Co v Horne (1933) 1 Ch 935** — company formed as a 'device or stratagem' to evade a restraint-of-trade covenant → veil lifted, injunction granted.
- **Jones v Lipman (1962) 1 WLR 832** — vendor transferred land to his own company to escape specific performance → company a 'mask'; veil pierced, specific performance ordered.
- **Lee Sow Keng v Kelly McKenzie Ltd (2004) 3 HKLRD 517** — shareholders diverted business/goodwill to a new same-named company to defeat an employee's judgment debt → veils of BOTH companies pierced.
- **Toptrans Ltd v Delta Resources Co Inc (2005) 1 HKLRD 635** — common control alone insufficient; 'shenanigans' must be shown; veil NOT lifted.
- **Winland Enterprises Group Inc v Wex Pharmaceuticals Inc (2012) 2 HKLRD 757** — shared management/directors/staff within a group is irrelevant per se; illegitimate purpose must be PROVED.
- **China Ocean Shipping Co v Mitrans Shipping Co (1995) 3 HKC 123** — no piercing where the company was incorporated BEFORE the obligation arose (evasion covers existing liabilities only).
- **Re Darby (1911) 1 KB 95** — fraud: veil pierced so fraudsters behind a company bore promoters' fiduciary duties.
- **Re FG (Films) Ltd (1953) 1 All ER 615** — agency analysis: a mere nominee/agent subsidiary — technically NOT veil-piercing; separate entities maintained.
- **Re Yung Kee Holdings Ltd (2014) 2 HKLRD 313** — separate legal entity maintained within a group; subsidiary's HK business ≠ sufficient connection to wind up the BVI parent.

**Directors' Duties**
- Sources: rules of equity, CO, articles, employment contract, Listing Rules, Companies Registry Guide on Directors' Duties (non-binding). Five fiduciary duties: (1) good faith in the interests of the company; (2) proper purposes; (3) no conflict; (4) no secret profits; (5) no misappropriation of company assets.
- Who owes: de jure directors (executive AND non-executive) owe full duties; de facto directors owe duties insofar as they perform directors' functions; shadow directors do NOT automatically owe general fiduciary duties — only where circumstances justify, typically for the directions/instructions they give (**Vivendi SA v Richards (2013) BCC 771**).
- Duties owed TO THE COMPANY, not shareholders/creditors — exceptions: special facts creating direct/close contact with shareholders (**Peskin v Anderson (2001) 1 BCLC 372**); creditors' interests must be considered on insolvency, but the duty remains owed to the company (**Lonrho Ltd v Shell Petroleum Co Ltd (1980) WLR 627; Yukong Line Ltd of Korea v Rendsburg Investments Corp of Liberia (No. 2) (1998) 1 WLR 294**).
- **Re Smith & Fawcett Ltd (1942) Ch 304** — good faith duty is primarily SUBJECTIVE; but if no reasonable director could so regard the act, the court may reject the claimed honest belief (objective evidential check).
- **Regentcrest Plc v Cohen (2001) 2 BCLC 80** — breach if the director (1) entirely fails to consider the company's interest or (2) takes irrelevant matters into account / omits relevant ones.
- **Re Neath Rugby Ltd (No. 2), Hawkes v Cuddy (2009) 2 BCLC 427** — nominee directors owe duties to the COMPANY, not the nominator; may consider nominator's interests only if genuinely believing the decision is in the company's best interests.
- **Tradepower (Holdings) Ltd v Tradepower (Hong Kong) Ltd (2009) 12 HKCFAR 417** — disposition of assets for no consideration when insolvent: breach; self-ratification by wrongdoer-shareholders ineffective where company insolvent/in serious difficulty.
- **Tam Po Kei v Tam Bo Kin (No. 1) (2011) 1 HKLRD 537** — dividends paid without proper declaration → director liable to account.
- **Howard Smith Ltd v Ampol Petroleum Ltd (1974) AC 821** — proper purposes: identify the SUBSTANTIAL/DOMINANT purpose; allotting shares primarily to dilute a shareholder and facilitate a takeover = improper.
- **Wong Kam San v Yeung Wing Keung (2007) 2 HKLRD 267** — allotment whose substantial purpose is to destroy an existing majority or create a new one is unconstitutional and unlawful.
- **Primlake Ltd v Matthews Associates (2007) 1 BCLC 666** — de facto director paying himself unjustified sums = improper purpose/self-enrichment.
- **Transvaal Lands Co v New Belgium (Transvaal) Land Development Co (1914) 2 Ch 488** — no-conflict: even a small shareholding in the counterparty triggers disclosure; non-disclosure → transaction voidable (even if commercially fair); abstaining from voting does NOT cure non-disclosure.
- **Man Luen Corp v Sun King Electronic Printed Circuitry Board Factory Ltd (1981) HKC 407** — disclosure to the BOARD alone insufficient where the articles/position require informed consent of MEMBERS in general meeting.
- **Newgate Stud Co v Penfold (2008) 1 BCLC 46** — transactions with the director's close relatives/partnership require informed consent; strictly applied even if beneficial to the company.
- **Boardman v Phipps (1967) 2 AC 46** — no-profit rule: fiduciaRY liable to account for profits from opportunity/knowledge obtained in the fiduciary position (allowance for skill and work possible).
- **Boston Deep Sea Fishing and Ice Co v Ansell (1888) 39 Ch D 339** — secret commissions/bribes must be accounted for even if the company could not itself have received them.
- **Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver (1967) 2 AC 134 (HL)** — directors liable to account for profits on shares subscribed via their directorship EVEN THOUGH the company could not have taken the opportunity and they acted in good faith throughout.
- **Chinese United Establishments Ltd v Cheung Siu Ki (1997) 2 HKC 212** — opportunity arriving in a personal capacity can still breach the no-conflict rule if there is an actual conflict.
- **Bishopsgate Investment Management Ltd (in liquidation) v Maxwell (No. 2) (1994) 1 All ER 261** — giving away company assets for no consideration to a family company = prima facie improper purpose.
- **Re City Equitable Fire Insurance Co Ltd (1925) Ch 407** — care/skill/diligence: intermittent duties; no liability for mere errors of judgment; justified in trusting officials absent grounds for suspicion.
- **Dorchester Finance Co Ltd v Stebbing (1989) BCLC 498** — non-executive directors liable: signing blank cheques and total non-involvement = breach; delegation ≠ absolution from supervision.
- **Law Wai Duen v Boldwin Construction Co Ltd (2001) 3 HKLRD 430** — executive and non-executive directors bear the SAME duties; a 'technical' director cannot take a neutral role; right of inspection essential to performing duties.
- **Brumder v Motornet Service and Repairs Ltd (2013) 3 All ER 412** — director cannot hide behind the company for his own failure to ensure regulatory compliance.
- **Re Rontex Intl Holdings Ltd (unrep.) HCMP 1869/2008, 18 March 2010** — failure to carry out adequate due diligence before investments = breach of duty of care.

**Shareholders' Remedies**
- **Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461** — two limbs: (1) proper plaintiff principle (company sues for wrongs to itself; shareholders lack locus standi); (2) irregularity principle (no member action for an informality ratifiable by ordinary resolution of the majority).
- Common law derivative action grounds: (1) ultra vires or illegal conduct (e.g. unlawful return of capital against s.212 CO; unlawful financial assistance against s.275 CO); (2) fraud on the company / fraud on the minority (equitable fraud — covers breach of fiduciary duty even in good faith; negligence alone insufficient UNLESS directors profited; requires wrongdoer CONTROL preventing the company suing — deadlock suffices, no need for 50%+).
- **Smith v Croft (No. 2) (1988) Ch 114** — minority has locus standi for ultra vires recovery, but the court considers the views of INDEPENDENT shareholders; action struck out where independent majority genuinely opposed it.
- **Waddington Ltd v Chan Chun Hoo (2008) 11 HKCFAR 370** — plaintiff in a (multiple) derivative action must show a prima facie case that the company is entitled to relief AND that the action falls within a Foss v Harbottle exception (usually fraud on the minority); reflective loss objection rejected.
- **Re Li Chung Shing Tong (Holdings) Ltd (2011) 5 HKLRD 274** — s.733 leave thresholds are LOW: prima facie in the company's interest + serious question to be tried; court slow to refuse unless prospects are hopeless.
- **O'Neill v Phillips (1999) 1 WLR 1092** — unfairness ordinarily requires breach of the terms on which the member agreed the affairs would be conducted; mere breakdown of trust and confidence without exclusion from management ≠ right to a buy-out.
- **Re Ching Hing Construction Co Ltd (unrep.) HCCW 889/1999** — excessive director remuneration assessed by objective commercial criteria; equitable understandings can generate unfairness even if not legally enforceable.
- **Rayfield v Hands (1960) Ch 1** — articles enforceable member v member (s.86); directors qua members ordered to buy the plaintiff's shares at fair value.
- Members' personal actions arise from: constitution (e.g. right to transfer shares, pre-emption/class rights, proper notice, right to vote, exclude improper votes), common law (e.g. special-resolution compliance, declared dividends, restrain improper dilution/alteration of articles), contract, and statute (s.116; ss.739–743; s.430; s.179 winding-up petition as contributory — see Chapter 13 / CWUMPO (Cap. 32)).

#### C. IRAC Application Points（答題框架）

**Issue 1: Is the company bound by the contract?**
- Rule: s.121 CO (mode of contracting) → authority (actual/ostensible, agency law) → indoor management rule (Turquand; ss.117–119 CO) → exceptions (notice, forgery, insider; note s.117(2)(c) knowledge ≠ bad faith).
- Application: ask (1) was there actual/apparent authority at all (National Commercial Bank v Albert Hwang — the rule cannot create authority); (2) is the third party in good faith/put on enquiry (Wong Kam San); (3) any forgery (Ruben).
- Conclusion: bound / not bound; note company may still ADOPT the transaction (Bank of East Asia v Labour Buildings).

**Issue 2: Should the veil be pierced?**
- Rule: separate legal entity is the default, even in groups (Yung Kee); pierce only for control + impropriety (Hashem v Shayif); evasion must be of an EXISTING obligation (Prest v Petrodel; China Ocean Shipping — timing!).
- Application: identify the specific existing obligation evaded / fraud perpetrated; check timing of incorporation; check whether remedies short of piercing suffice.
- Conclusion: pierce only so far as necessary for that wrong.

**Issue 3: Director in breach?**
- Rule: identify duty (good faith — subjective test (Re Smith & Fawcett; Regentcrest); proper purpose — dominant purpose (Howard Smith); no-conflict — s.536 disclosure; no-profit (Regal (Hastings)); care/skill/diligence — s.465(2) mixed standard).
- Application: status of director (de jure / de facto / shadow — Vivendi); informed consent of members in general meeting cures conflict; company insolvent → creditors' interests (Tradepower).
- Conclusion: breach + company's remedies (avoidance (voidable; void if no authority), restitution/constructive trust, disgorgement, equitable compensation, accessory liability via knowing receipt / dishonest assistance, common law damages for duty of care only).

**Issue 4: What can the shareholder do?**
- Rule: Foss v Harbottle two limbs → wrong to company = derivative action (common law grounds; or statutory ss.731–733 with s.733(1) leave + written notice); wrong to member personally = personal action (s.86 articles; statute); majority abuse prejudicing minority qua member = unfair prejudice s.724 → s.725 orders (buy-out most common); breakdown/substratum lost = just and equitable winding-up.
- Application: capacity! (member qua member, not employee/creditor); control by wrongdoers; alternative relief available? (court may strike out petition); reflective loss not recoverable.
- Conclusion: pick the remedy whose plaintiff, wrong and relief match the facts — exam 最常考 derivative action vs unfair prejudice 嘅揀擇。

### 易混淆概念對比

#### 對比 1: Common Law Derivative Action vs Statutory Derivative Action vs Unfair Prejudice Petition

| 維度 | Common Law Derivative Action | Statutory Derivative Action (ss.731–733 CO) | Unfair Prejudice Petition (ss.724–725 CO) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature of wrong | Wrong done TO THE COMPANY — only ultra vires/illegal conduct OR fraud on the company (equitable fraud; needs wrongdoer control) | 'Misconduct' against the company = fraud, negligence, breach of duty or default in compliance with any Ordinance or rule of law (s.731) — WIDER, covers pure negligence | Conduct of the company's affairs unfairly prejudicial to MEMBERS' interests qua members (or an actual/proposed prejudicial act/omission, s.724(1)) |
| Whose claim / who benefits | Company's cause of action; shareholder sues ON BEHALF of the company; recovery goes to the company | Same — company's claim; relief goes to the company | Member's OWN claim; relief benefits the member (e.g. buy-out of his/her shares, damages to the member) |
| Court gatekeeping | No leave procedure, but court may strike out if a majority of INDEPENDENT members oppose (Smith v Croft (No. 2)) | Leave of court mandatory: s.733(1)(a)–(c) — company's interest (low threshold), serious question to be tried, company has not sued, written notice served on the company | No leave; but court may strike out if a readily available alternative relief (e.g. offer to buy shares) gives all the petitioner can reasonably expect |
| Typical trigger facts | Controlling directors loot the company and block board action | Same, plus negligence-based claims and intervention in pending proceedings (s.732(3)) | Exclusion from management in a quasi-partnership, excessive director remuneration, share dilution, long-term no-dividend policy |
| 點樣分辨 / 考試伏位 | Negligence ALONE is not 'fraud on the company' unless directors profited — 呢個係分界位 | 見到 negligence 或 statutory default → 行 statutory 路線；記住 leave + notice 係程序伏位；唔可以用嚟逼公司做日常商業決定或處理股東私人恩怨 | 受損必須係 qua member（員工/債權人身份受損唔計）；純粹 trust breakdown 而無 exclusion ≠ unfair prejudice (O'Neill v Phillips)；reflective loss 唔 recoverable |

#### 對比 2: Common Law Indoor Management Rule vs Codified Rule (ss.117–119 CO)

| 維度 | Common Law Rule (Royal British Bank v Turquand (1856) 119 ER 775) | Codified Rule (ss.117–119 CO) |
|---|---|---|
| Protection | Outsider in good faith may assume internal acts (resolutions, appointments, conferral of authority) were duly performed | Directors' power to bind the company regarded as free of any limitation under any relevant document, in favour of a person dealing in good faith (s.117(1)) |
| Effect of NOTICE of irregularity | Rule FAILS if the outsider has actual or constructive notice (Morris v Kanssen; Wong Kam San) | Knowledge alone that the act exceeds directors' powers ≠ bad faith (s.117(2)(c)); no duty to enquire (s.117(2)(d)) — wider protection |
| Burden of proof | Outsider effectively must show good faith and absence of notice | Good faith PRESUMED unless the company proves the contrary (s.117(2)(b)) |
| Prerequisites | Presupposes actual or ostensible authority under normal agency principles — cannot create authority (National Commercial Bank v Albert Hwang) | Applies where the transaction is entered by the board or an agent acting under the board's authority |
| 點樣分辨 / 考試伏位 | Forgery = nullity (Ruben v Great Fingall); insiders 唔可以用 | 考生常伏：以為知情的 third party 一定唔受保護 —— 普通法下係，但 s.117(2)(c) 下單純知情唔係 bad faith；答題時兩條路線都要行一次 |

## Phase 2 閃卡 (exactly 8 cards)

### Card 1
**Front:** State the indoor management rule and its case origin. Name its three common law exceptions.
**Back:**
- Persons dealing with a company in good faith may assume that acts within its constitution and powers have been properly and duly performed; they are not bound to enquire into internal regularity.
- Origin: Royal British Bank v Turquand (1856) 119 ER 775.
- Exceptions: (1) actual or constructive notice of the irregularity; (2) forgery (document is a nullity — Ruben v Great Fingall Consolidated (1906) AC 439); (3) insiders cannot rely on it (Morris v Kanssen (1946) AC 459).
💬 白話：同公司做嘢嘅外人，唔使揭開公司「房門」查佢內部手續辦妥未。但如果你本身知有問題、文件係偽冒、或者你係「自己人」，就唔受保護。

### Card 2
**Front:** Under s.465(2) CO, what is the standard for a director's duty of care, skill and diligence?
**Back:**
- Standard of a reasonably diligent person with BOTH:
  - (a) objective element — the general knowledge, skill and experience reasonably expected of a person carrying out that director's functions; and
  - (b) subjective element — the general knowledge, skill and experience that director actually has.
- Shadow directors are also caught (s.465(5)).
- Mere errors of judgment are NOT breaches (Re City Equitable Fire Insurance Co Ltd (1925) Ch 407); non-executive and executive directors bear the same duties (Law Wai Duen v Boldwin Construction Co Ltd (2001) 3 HKLRD 430).
💬 白話：標準係「合理勤奮嘅人」做你個位應有嘅本事（客觀），再加你自己實際有嘅本事（主觀）—— 兩者取其高。所以會計師出身嘅 NED 會被要求高啲（Dorchester Finance）。

### Card 3
**Front:** Why were the directors in Regal (Hastings) Ltd v Gulliver (1967) 2 AC 134 (HL) liable even though the company could not have taken the opportunity and they acted in good faith?
**Back:**
- The no-profit rule is STRICT: a director must not profit by reason of, or through an opportunity/knowledge resulting from, his/her position as director.
- Good faith, absence of loss to the company, and the company's inability to take the opportunity are ALL irrelevant.
- Purpose: to completely eliminate the possibility of abuse of the fiduciary position.
- Escape route: informed consent of the company in general meeting after full disclosure.
💬 白話：「公司都食唔到單生意，我自己食有咩問題？」—— 有問題。只要機會係因你董事身份而嚟，賺咗就要呕返出嚟（account of profits），善意都冇用。想安全食，先攞股東大會知情同意。

### Card 4
**Front:** State the test for breach of the duty to exercise powers for proper purposes, with authority.
**Back:**
- Rule: directors must exercise each power for the purpose for which it was conferred (ascertained from the articles).
- Test: if exercised for multiple purposes, breach occurs if the SUBSTANTIAL or DOMINANT purpose was improper (Howard Smith Ltd v Ampol Petroleum Ltd (1974) AC 821).
- Classic example: allotting shares primarily to dilute a shareholder / facilitate a takeover rather than to raise capital = improper (also Wong Kam San v Yeung Wing Keung (2007) 2 HKLRD 267 — allotment to destroy an existing majority is unconstitutional).
💬 白話：唔好問「董事有冇權配股」，要問「佢配股嘅主要目的係乜」。權力本身合法，目的唔正都係 breach。

### Card 5
**Front:** State the two limbs of the rule in Foss v Harbottle (1843) 2 Hare 461.
**Back:**
- Proper plaintiff principle: where a wrong is done to the company (e.g. directors' breach of duty), the company is the proper plaintiff; shareholders lack locus standi.
- Irregularity principle: a member cannot sue over a mere informality or irregularity that the majority can cure/ratify by ordinary resolution in general meeting.
- Main escape routes: derivative actions (common law: ultra vires/illegal conduct; fraud on the company) and statutory derivative action (ss.731–733 CO).
💬 白話：公司先係受害人，所以公司先係原告；小事大多數可以追認，法庭唔會管。多數派控制晒公司唔肯告自己？先輪到小股東用 derivative action 代公司出頭。

### Card 6
**Front:** What must a member show to obtain leave of the court to bring a statutory derivative action under s.733(1) CO?
**Back:**
- (a) Interest of the company: on the face of the application it appears to be in the company's interests that leave be granted — LOW threshold (s.733(1)(a); Re Li Chung Shing Tong (Holdings) Ltd (2011) 5 HKLRD 274).
- (b) Serious question to be tried — at least a probability of success, not certainty (s.733(1)(b)).
- (b) The company has not itself brought the proceedings.
- (c) The member has served WRITTEN NOTICE on the company of the intention to apply for leave (s.733(1)(c)).
💬 白話：四部曲 —— 對公司有利（表面睇落係就得）、有實質可審嘅問題、公司自己冇告、事先書面通知公司。門檻刻意設得低，但程序（notice）唔做得漏。

### Card 7
**Front:** List the elements of an unfair prejudice claim under s.724(1) CO and the key orders available.
**Back:**
- Elements: (1) the complaint concerns the company's AFFAIRS (widely interpreted); (2) it affects the member's interest QUA MEMBER; (3) the conduct is UNFAIR (assessed liberally but ordinarily requires breach of the agreed terms or inequitable reliance on strict legal powers — O'Neill v Phillips (1999) 1 WLR 1092); (4) it is PREJUDICIAL (injury/detriment, financial or otherwise); (5) assessed OBJECTIVELY (reasonable bystander).
- Orders (s.725): restrain the conduct; order proceedings in the company's name; appoint receiver/manager; regulate future affairs; BUY-OUT of a member's shares (most common); damages (no reflective loss).
💬 白話：五個元素記「Affairs–Member–Unfair–Prejudice–Objective」。最關鍵伏位：受損要係股東身份受損，做員工/債主蝕底唔計；常見結局係多數派買走小股東啲股。

### Card 8
**Front:** When will a court pierce the corporate veil at common law? (State the control + impropriety framework.)
**Back:**
- Default: separate legal entity is maintained, even within groups (Re Yung Kee Holdings Ltd (2014) 2 HKLRD 313).
- Requirements (Hashem v Shayif (2008) EWHC 2380): (1) CONTROL of the company by the wrongdoer AND (2) IMPROPRIETY — use of the company as a façade to evade an EXISTING legal obligation or perpetrate fraud; both must be shown.
- Piercing only so far as necessary for that wrong, and only when other remedies fail (Prest v Petrodel Resources Ltd (2013) UKSC 34, (2013) 2 AC 415).
- Mere control / common management is insufficient (Toptrans; Winland); company formed before the obligation arose → no evasion (China Ocean Shipping Co v Mitrans Shipping Co (1995) 3 HKC 123).
- Statute: fraudulent trading — s.275 CWUMPO (Cap. 32).
💬 白話：「揭面紗」要有兩樣嘢齊：控制 + 不當目的（走數/呃人），而且要走嘅數必須係已經存在嘅法律責任。單憑「兩間公司同一班人」係唔夠嘅。
