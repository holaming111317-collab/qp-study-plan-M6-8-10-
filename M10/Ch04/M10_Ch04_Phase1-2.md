# M10 Chapter 4: The Law of Tort and Negligence — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

## Phase 1 速查表

### Learning Objectives
1. **Analyse** the legal meaning of tort and distinguish the three types of tortious liability (intentional tort, negligence, strict liability).
2. **Apply** the three elements of negligence — duty of care, breach of duty, and causation/remoteness of damage — to novel fact patterns using the IRAC method.
3. **Evaluate** the availability of defences (contributory negligence, voluntary assumption of risk) and statutory limits on excluding liability.
4. **Assess** the tortious liability of professionals (accountants and auditors) for negligent misstatement to clients and third parties.
5. **Distinguish** the special liability regimes: employers' liability, vicarious liability, occupiers' liability and defamation.

#### B. Leading Cases (full citation as given in the LP)

**Duty of care**
- *Donoghue v Stevenson* (1932) AC 562 — the **neighbour principle**: reasonable care to avoid acts/omissions you can reasonably foresee would injure persons so closely and directly affected that you ought to have them in contemplation. Manufacturer owed duty to ultimate consumer (snail in ginger-beer).
- *Anns v Merton London Borough Council* — **two-fold test** (proximity + policy negating the duty); later criticised.
- *Yuen Kun Yeu v Attorney General of Hong Kong* (1988) AC 175 PC — Privy Council: Anns first stage too ambiguous; **foreseeability of harm** must not be neglected.
- *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* (1990) 2 AC 605 — **three-fold test**: (1) foreseeability of damage; (2) close and direct relationship of proximity; (3) fair, just and reasonable to impose a duty. Auditor owes duty to **shareholders as a body**, not to non-shareholders contemplating investment.
- *Palsgraf v Long Island Railroad Co* (1928) 248 NY 339 — no duty to a plaintiff whose injury was unforeseeable to a person of ordinary vigilance.
- *Bourhill v Young* (1943) AC 92 — plaintiff 45–50 feet away, outside the range of foreseeable harm; no liability for nervous shock.
- *Barrett v Enfield London Borough Council* (2001) 2 AC 550 — policy balancing: promoting individual autonomy, preventing indeterminate liability, protecting the vulnerable.

**Breach / standard of care**
- *Blyth v Birmingham Waterworks Co* (1856) 11 Exch 781 — the **reasonable person** test (objective).
- *Roe v Minister of Health* (1954) 2 QB 66 — standard assessed by knowledge **at the time of the incident** ("must not look at the 1947 accident with 1954 spectacles" — Denning LJ).
- *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* (1957) 1 WLR 582 — **Bolam test**: not negligent if acting in accordance with a practice accepted as proper by a responsible body of skilled professionals; **inexperience is no defence** — the test focuses on the activity, not the person.
- *Ho Ying Wai v Keliston Marine (Far East) Ltd* (2003) 1 HKLRD 343 — GP who accepted a commercial-diver fitness retainer was expected to acquire the competency of a reasonably competent practitioner in that field; liable.
- *Bolton v Stone* (1951) AC 850 — very low likelihood of risk + high cost of prevention → no breach (cricket ball).
- *Paris v Stepney Borough Council* (1951) AC 367 — greater seriousness of potential harm (one-eyed worker) → higher standard; goggles should have been provided.
- *Latimer v A.E.C. Ltd* (1953) AC 643 — employer not required to eliminate risk at disproportionate cost (sawdust on flooded floor sufficient; no duty to close factory).
- *Rimmer v Liverpool City Council* (1984) 1 All ER 930 — **codes of practice** alert the defendant to appropriate standards; failure to follow may constitute breach.

**Causation / remoteness**
- **'But for' test** — plaintiff must prove on the balance of probabilities that but for the defendant's act/omission, the injury would not have occurred.
- *Barnett v Chelsea Hospital Management Committee* (1969) 1 QB 428 — hospital negligent, but victim would have died anyway → causation failed.
- *McGhee v National Coal Board* (1973) 1 WLR 1 — where precise cause is unknown, **material contribution to the risk** of injury suffices.
- **Thin skull rule** — defendant takes the victim as he finds him: *Smith v Leech Brain & Co* — liable for full extent (death from cancer triggered by foreseeable burn).
- **Novus actus interveniens** — an intervening act breaks the chain of causation only if it was **not reasonably foreseeable** to the defendant: *Hui Kay Cheong, Herman v Chi Wo Properties Ltd* (1992) HKDCLR 51 (foreseeable robbery did NOT break the chain) vs *Leung Sin Fai v Perfect World Co Ltd* (1998) 2 HKLRD 115 (unforeseeable inflammable liquid DID break the chain).
- **Res ipsa loquitur** — *Sanfield Building Contractors Ltd v Li Kai Cheong* (2003) 3 HKLRD 48 (CFA): accident of unknown cause that would not normally happen without negligence, where defendant controlled the object/activity → inference of negligence unless defendant offers an acceptable explanation consistent with reasonable care (essence per Lord Radcliffe in *Barkway v South Wales Transport Co Ltd* (1950) 1 All ER 392).

**Defences to negligence**
- **Contributory negligence** — onus on the defendant; partial defence leading to apportionment under s.21 LARCO (Cap. 23). *Chan Wing Kin & Anor v Fonnie Co* (1983) HKLR 102 — pregnant front-seat passenger not wearing seat belt NOT contributory negligent in the circumstances (fault, not mere conduct, must cause the injury).
- **Volenti non fit injuria** — complete defence; requires proof of negligence + plaintiff's **full knowledge of the nature and extent of the risk + consent** (explicit or implied agreement to waive liability); mere knowledge is insufficient. *Simms v Leigh Rugby Football Club* (1969) 2 All ER 923 — rugby player willingly accepted risks of a compliant ground.

**Accountants/auditors & negligent misstatement**
- *Hedley Byrne & Co Ltd v Heller & Partners Ltd* (1964) AC 465 — negligent misrepresentation actionable only where a **'special relationship'** exists (assumption of responsibility, equivalent to contract; relationships such as solicitor/client, banker/customer); disclaimer 'without responsibility' negated liability.
- *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* (1990) 2 AC 605 — auditor's duty limited to shareholders' class rights, not potential investors.
- *James McNaughton Paper Group Ltd v Hicks Anderson & Co* (1991) 2 QB 113 — **six criteria** for duty to a third party: (1) purpose for which the statement was made; (2) purpose for which it was communicated; (3) relationship between advisor, advisee and third party; (4) size of the class to which the advisee belongs; (5) advisor's knowledge of the purpose; (6) reliance by the advisee. Accountant not liable to prospective purchaser who relied on draft accounts without consulting his own accountant.

**Employers' liability / vicarious liability**
- *McDermid v Nash Dredging & Reclamation Co Ltd* (1987) AC 906 — employer's duty is **personal and non-delegable** (delegation to a competent person is no defence). Four heads: safe place of work; competent employees + supervision; adequate plant and appliances; safe system of work.
- *Davie v New Merton Board Mills* (1959) 1 All ER 346 / *Pearce v Round Oak Steel Works Ltd* (1969) 1 WLR 595 — duty discharged by buying from reputable supplier + reasonable inspection; latent defect not discoverable by reasonable care excuses.
- *Yuen Chung Shun v On Ascent Ltd* (2016) 4 HKLRD 250 — complete failure of supervision → employer liable (with employee's contributory negligence).
- *Cathay Pacific Airways Ltd v Wong Sau Lai* (2006) 2 HKLRD 586 — unsafe system of work (no stopping mechanism on drawer); foreseeable momentary lapse under pressure → employer liable.
- **Vicarious liability** (strict liability for employee's torts in the course of employment): *Dubai Aluminium Co Ltd v Salaam* (2003) 2 AC 366 — employer's innocence irrelevant once employee's liability established. **Salmond test** (authorised wrongful act, or unauthorised mode of an authorised act); **Close Connection Test** from *Lister v Hesley Hall Ltd* (2002) 1 AC 215; applied in HK in *Ming An Insurance Co (HK) Ltd v Ritz Carlton Ltd* (2002) 3 HKLRD 844 (doorman's unauthorised food run in limousine still within course of employment).

**Occupiers' liability**
- *Chu Wo Heung & Anor v Hui Lai Wa* (2002) 3 HKLRD 209 — building manager with control over external wall via DMC was an **occupier** of the awning; control, not ownership.
- *Cooke v Midland Great Western Rly of Ireland* (1909) AC 229 — unlocked turntable = allurement; occupier liable to child trespassers it knew played there. Contrast *Latham v R Johnson & Nephew Ltd* (1913) 1 KB 398 — ordinary heaps of stones, no trap/allurement → not liable.
- *Wood v Wah Tung (E&M) Ltd* (2000) HKEC 277 — frequent flooding/power failure → general safety reminder inadequate; specific warnings required.
- *Roles v Nathan* (1963) 1 WLR 1117 — chimney sweeps warned of gas risk; risk incidental to their calling → occupier not liable (s.3(3)(b) OLO equivalent).
- *Tomlinson v Congleton BC* (2004) 1 AC 46 — no duty to warn of **obvious dangers**.
- *Luen Hing Fat Coating & Finishing Factory Ltd v Waan Chuen Ming* (2011) 2 HKLRD 223 — occupier liable for own negligence in failing to engage/supervise a competent independent contractor.
- Trespassers: occupier owes **no duty** of reasonable care, even for concealed danger, unless harm deliberately intended (common law; OLO does not govern trespassers).

**Defamation**
- Elements: (1) defamatory statement (lowers the person in the estimation of right-thinking members of society / hatred, contempt or ridicule); (2) reference to the plaintiff (a purely personal action; a corporation may sue for statements disparaging its goodwill — *Tung Lo Court (IO) v Tsui Wai Yip* (2015) 4 HKLRD 397); (3) publication to a third party (not the defendant's spouse).
- Libel = permanent form; slander = transient form. Defendant's **intention is irrelevant**; natural and ordinary meaning to ordinary reasonable viewers; legal innuendo must be pleaded separately.
- *Cheung Ng Sheong Steven v Eastweek Publisher Ltd* (1995) 3 HKC 601 — article attacking professor's discharge of duties = libel.
- *Charles Sin Cho Chiu v Tin Tin Publication Development Ltd* HCA 6662/1997 — group reference ('7 Honourable Men', 'tainted elements') can identify an individual plaintiff.
- Defences: **justification** (substantial truth of the imputation; s.26 Defamation Ordinance (Cap. 21)); **fair comment** (public interest + recognisable comment + based on true fact + honest opinion; defeated by **malice**); **absolute privilege** (judicial/quasi-judicial proceedings, public officers, Legislative Council); **qualified privilege** (duty/interest to communicate; lost if malicious); **consent** (must not exceed scope given).
- Remedies: damages (nominal / general compensatory / aggravated / exemplary / special) and injunction.

#### C. IRAC Application Points (duty → breach → causation → remoteness → defences)

- **Issue**: identify which tort and which relationship (driver/passenger, professional/client, employer/employee, occupier/visitor, publisher/subject). Do not confuse negligent misstatement with ordinary negligence.
- **Rule**: state the correct test with authority — Caparo three-fold test for duty; Bolam test for professional standard; but-for test for factual causation; s.21 LARCO for apportionment; s.3 OLO for occupiers.
- **Application**:
  1. **Duty of care** — run all three Caparo limbs in order: foreseeable? proximity? fair, just and reasonable? For pure economic loss or psychiatric harm, policy (indeterminate liability) usually narrows the duty. For statements, add Hedley Byrne 'special relationship' / James McNaughton six criteria.
  2. **Breach** — objective reasonable person; adjust for skill (professionals held to their profession's standard; inexperience no defence); weigh likelihood (*Bolton v Stone*), seriousness (*Paris v Stepney*), cost of avoidance (*Latimer*), codes of practice (*Rimmer*).
  3. **Causation** — but-for first (*Barnett*); if scientifically uncertain, material contribution (*McGhee*); if the accident's cause is unknown but speaks of negligence, res ipsa loquitur (*Sanfield*).
  4. **Remoteness** — foreseeable type of injury → liable for full extent (thin skull, *Smith v Leech Brain*); unforeseeable intervening act breaks the chain (novus actus); foreseeable third-party crime does not (*Hui Kay Cheong*).
  5. **Defences** — contributory negligence (apportionment; onus on defendant) vs volenti (complete bar; needs full knowledge + consent); check statutory restrictions on exclusion clauses (CECO s.7; CO s.415(2)/(3)).
- **Conclusion**: give a decisive one-sided answer (e.g. "Therefore, X is liable in negligence, subject to a reduction for contributory negligence") — never sit on the fence when facts are clear.

### 易混淆概念對比

**Table 1: Negligence (ordinary) vs Negligent Misstatement**

| 比較點 | Negligence (ordinary negligence) | Negligent Misstatement / Misrepresentation |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of wrong | Careless **act or omission** causing physical injury or property damage | Careless **statement/advice** relied upon, causing (usually) **pure economic loss** |
| Basis of duty | Neighbour principle / Caparo three-fold test (foreseeability + proximity + fair, just and reasonable) | Caparo test **plus** a 'special relationship' with assumption of responsibility — *Hedley Byrne v Heller* (1964) AC 465 |
| Duty to third parties | Proximity judged by physical closeness, cause and effect, or control | Only if *James McNaughton* six criteria support it (purpose, communication, relationship, class size, knowledge, reliance); auditor's duty stops at shareholders — *Caparo* |
| Typical claimant | Anyone foreseeably injured by the act | Client or a third party the maker knew would rely on the statement |
| Effect of disclaimer | Cannot exclude death/personal injury liability at all (CECO s.7(a)) | An express 'without responsibility' disclaimer can negate assumption of responsibility (*Hedley Byrne*) |

點樣分辨：見到「講錯嘢／出錯 report 令人蝕錢」→ 一定諗 **negligent misstatement**，第一步係搵 **special relationship / assumption of responsibility**，唔好直接套 neighbour principle。考試伏位：Jun 2025 Panelists' Report 明確指出考生將 negligent misstatement 當普通 negligence 答，直接跌晒 application 分。留意純經濟損失（pure economic loss）本身係 policy 上收緊 duty 嘅訊號（preventing indeterminate liability）。

**Table 2: Contributory Negligence vs Voluntary Assumption of Risk (Volenti Non Fit Injuria)**

| 比較點 | Contributory Negligence | Voluntary Assumption of Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory basis | s.21 LARCO (Cap. 23) | Common law; OLO s.3(5) for occupiers |
| Effect | **Partial defence** — damages reduced by apportionment as just and equitable (s.21(2): record total first, then reduce) | **Complete defence** — no liability at all |
| What must be proved | Plaintiff's **fault** contributed to his own injury (plaintiff owes no duty to defendant); onus on defendant | Defendant was negligent **and** plaintiff had **full knowledge of the nature and extent of the risk** and **consented** (explicit/implied agreement to waive liability) |
| Standard of proof | Elements similar to negligence (causation, standard of care) | Much more stringent — mere knowledge of the risk is **insufficient** |
| Leading case | *Chan Wing Kin v Fonnie Co* (1983) HKLR 102 (pregnant passenger — no contributory negligence) | *Simms v Leigh Rugby Football Club* (1969) 2 All ER 923 (rugby player accepted inherent risks) |

點樣分辨：淨係「佢知道有風險」→ 頂多 contributory negligence（扣減賠償）；要「知道 + 同意放棄追究」先係 volenti（完全免責）。考試伏位：唔好見到受害人冇戴 seat belt 就自動話 contributory negligence — 要證明係佢嘅 **fault** 造成傷害（*Chan Wing Kin*：孕婦唔戴反而合理）。另外 CECO s.7(c) 話明「同意豁免條款」唔等於自願承受風險。

## Phase 2 閃卡

### Card 1
**Front:** What are the three elements of the modern tort of negligence, and what test is applied to establish a duty of care?

**Back:**
- Three elements: (1) duty of care; (2) breach of that duty; (3) damage caused by the breach.
- Duty of care — Caparo three-fold test (*Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* (1990) 2 AC 605): (1) damage foreseeable by the defendant; (2) close and direct relationship of proximity; (3) fair, just and reasonable to impose a duty.

💬 記住口訣「Duty–Breach–Damage」三步走；duty 嗰關要用 Caparo 三摺測試，三個 limbs 次序唔好亂：可預見 → 夠近 → 公平合理。少講一個 limb 都會失分。

### Card 2
**Front:** State the neighbour principle from *Donoghue v Stevenson* (1932) AC 562 and its significance.

**Back:**
- You must take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions which you can reasonably foresee would be likely to injure your 'neighbour' — persons so closely and directly affected by your act that you ought reasonably to have them in contemplation.
- Significance: created a **general test** for duty of care without relying on recognised categories; negligence is an open concept — categories of liability are not closed.

💬 即係「薑汁啤酒蝸牛案」：製造商對最終消費者有 duty，即使冇合約關係。重點係佢打破咗「逐類案件先有人身責任」嘅舊框架，變成通用原則。

### Card 3
**Front:** What is the Bolam test, and can a defendant rely on inexperience as a defence?

**Back:**
- *Bolam v Friern Hospital Management Committee* (1957) 1 WLR 582: a professional is not negligent if he acted in accordance with a practice accepted as proper by a responsible body of skilled persons in that art; mere existence of a contrary body of opinion does not establish negligence.
- **Inexperience is NOT a defence** — the test focuses on the activity, not the person; the defendant is held to the standard of an ordinarily competent practitioner professing that skill.

💬 一個新牌醫生／會計師做專業嘢，就要即刻達到該專業嘅普通勝任標準，「我冇經驗」唔係藉口。*Ho Ying Wai* 案：普通科醫生接咗潛水員體檢單，就要補足相關知識。

### Card 4
**Front:** Explain the 'but for' test, and when does the 'material contribution' approach apply instead?

**Back:**
- But-for test: plaintiff must prove on the balance of probabilities that **but for** the defendant's act/omission, the injury would not have occurred. If the injury would have happened anyway → causation fails (*Barnett v Chelsea Hospital Management Committee* (1969) 1 QB 428).
- Material contribution: where the precise cause is not scientifically known and there are multiple possible causes, it suffices that the defendant's negligence **materially increased the risk / materially contributed** to the injury (*McGhee v National Coal Board* (1973) 1 WLR 1).

💬 舉證責任先喺原告（balance of probabilities），之後先轉去被告。*Barnett* 係必考位：醫院明明疏忽，但病人點都會死 → 冇 causation → 唔使賠。

### Card 5
**Front:** What is the thin skull rule, and which case illustrates it?

**Back:**
- The defendant **takes the victim as he finds him**: once the type of injury is foreseeable, the defendant is liable for its **full extent**, even if aggravated by the plaintiff's peculiar pre-existing condition.
- *Smith v Leech Brain & Co*: a foreseeable burn triggered cancer in a pre-malignant tissue → liable for the death; unnecessary to show death by cancer was foreseeable.
- Do not confuse with foreseeability at the duty stage: the rule applies only **after** foreseeable harm is established; defendant need not better the plaintiff's original position.

💬 「蛋殼頭骨」原則：你撞親個人，就要接受佢本身脆弱。考試鍾意將佢同 duty 階段嘅 foreseeability 撈亂 — duty 階段係問「普通健康人會唔會受傷」，thin skull 係問「受咗傷之後賠幾多」。

### Card 6
**Front:** Distinguish novus actus interveniens from res ipsa loquitur.

**Back:**
- Novus actus interveniens: a subsequent independent act that **breaks the chain of causation** — but only if the intervening event was **not reasonably foreseeable** to the defendant. Foreseeable third-party criminal acts do NOT break the chain (*Hui Kay Cheong v Chi Wo Properties* (1992) HKDCLR 51); unforeseeable ones do (*Leung Sin Fai v Perfect World* (1998) 2 HKLRD 115).
- Res ipsa loquitur: a rule of **evidence** — where an accident of unknown cause would not normally happen without negligence and the defendant controlled the object/activity, the court infers negligence unless the defendant gives an acceptable explanation consistent with reasonable care (*Sanfield Building Contractors Ltd v Li Kai Cheong* (2003) 3 HKLRD 48).

💬 一個係「因果鏈斷咗 → 唔使賠」，一個係「事實本身就說明有疏忽 → 推定要賠」。Dec 2025 考生 novus actus 掌握差，記住關鍵字係 intervening event 可唔可以 **reasonably foresee**。

### Card 7
**Front:** Compare contributory negligence and volenti non fit injuria as defences to negligence.

**Back:**
- Contributory negligence: partial defence; plaintiff's fault contributed to his own injury; damages **reduced** by apportionment under s.21(1) LARCO (Cap. 23); court must record total damages first (s.21(2)); onus on the defendant.
- Volenti: complete defence; requires defendant's negligence + plaintiff's **full knowledge of the nature and extent of the risk** + **consent** to waive liability; mere knowledge insufficient; e.g. *Simms v Leigh Rugby Football Club* (1969) 2 All ER 923.

💬 記住：CN = 扣分（賠少啲）；volenti = 出局（唔使賠）。Volenti 門檻高好多，「知有風險」唔等於「同意承受」。

### Card 8
**Front:** When is a professional liable to a third party for a negligent misstatement?

**Back:**
- Base rule: a 'special relationship' with assumption of responsibility is required (*Hedley Byrne v Heller* (1964) AC 465); an auditor owes a duty to shareholders as a body but NOT to potential investors relying on published accounts (*Caparo v Dickman* (1990) 2 AC 605).
- Third-party duty assessed by the six *James McNaughton Paper Group Ltd v Hicks Anderson & Co* (1991) 2 QB 113 criteria: purpose of the statement; purpose of communication; relationship between advisor/advisee/third party; size of the class; advisor's knowledge of purpose; reliance by the advisee.
- A 'without responsibility' disclaimer may negate the duty.

💬 會計師／核數師章節重中之重：見到「第三方攞住份 report 去投資/借錢」即刻諗 Caparo + James McNaughton 六個因素，逐個 apply 落 facts，唔好齋背。

| Negligent misstatement | 疏忽失實陳述：喺 special relationship 下講錯嘢令人蝕錢，要孭純經濟損失 | Because the auditors never assumed responsibility to the potential investor, no duty of care arose for the alleged negligent misstatement (Caparo v Dickman). |
