**Q1.** Harbour Bright Ltd is a private company limited by shares incorporated in Hong Kong. Its accounting reference period ended on **31 March 2025**. The directors ask you what is the **latest date** by which the company must hold its annual general meeting ('AGM') in respect of that financial year, assuming the AGM is not dispensed with under s.612 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) ('CO'). Which of the following is correct?

A. 30 June 2025
B. 30 September 2025
C. 31 December 2025
D. 31 March 2026

**Q2.** The board of directors of Clear Water Trading Ltd, a private company, is planning the company's agenda and wishes to know which of the following matters **requires a special resolution** of the members under the CO. Which one is it?

A. Re-appointing the existing auditor at the AGM
B. Changing the company's name
C. Removing a director before the end of the director's term of office
D. Appointing a person to fill a casual vacancy in the office of auditor

**Q3.** Ivy and Ken hold 45% and 25% respectively of the voting shares in Golden Meadow Ltd, a private company. Leo holds the remaining 30% and is one of its three directors. Ivy and Ken wish to remove Leo from the board before the expiry of his term. They sent a notice to members on 1 May giving notice of a general meeting to be held on **15 May**, at which an ordinary resolution to remove Leo was passed by 70% of the votes cast. No notice of the proposed resolution to remove Leo was given to the company or members in advance of the meeting notice, no copy of the notice was sent to Leo, and Leo was given no opportunity to make representations. Leo challenges the validity of his removal. Which of the following best states the position?

A. The removal is valid: s.462 of the CO allows members to remove a director by ordinary resolution notwithstanding anything in the articles, and 70% is well above a simple majority.
B. The removal is valid: under the irregularity principle, the intention of the majority was clear and the result would have been the same had the correct procedure been observed.
C. The removal is prima facie invalid: a resolution to remove a director requires special notice of at least 28 days before the meeting (s.462(4) and s.578(1) of the CO), and Leo was denied his statutory right to receive a copy of the notice and to be heard and make representations (s.463), which goes beyond a mere informality.
D. The removal is invalid: removing a director before the expiry of his term requires a special resolution supported by at least 75% of the votes cast.

**Q4.** M & Co, a firm of certified public accountants (practising), has audited the financial statements of Jade Garden Restaurant Ltd for several years. During the latest audit, M & Co came across a number of **altered purchase invoices** which had the effect of inflating the company's reported profit. M & Co raised the matter with the managing director, Owen, who assured the engagement partner that the alterations were routine corrections of suppliers' errors. M & Co had always regarded Owen as a man of the highest integrity, accepted his explanation without further enquiry, and issued an **unqualified auditor's report**. The fraud was later discovered and the company went into liquidation. The liquidator sues M & Co for negligence. Which of the following best states the likely outcome?

A. M & Co is not liable: an auditor is a watch-dog but not a bloodhound, and was justified in relying on the managing director's certificates and assurances (Re Kingston Cotton Mill Co (No. 2) (1896) 2 Ch 279).
B. M & Co is likely liable: having discovered the altered invoices, the auditors were put on enquiry and had a duty to make an exhaustive enquiry and to inform the board; they were not entitled to rest content with Owen's bare assurances (Re Thomas Gerrard & Son Ltd (1968) Ch 455).
C. M & Co is not liable: an auditor is not an insurer and does not guarantee that the accounts correctly show the true position of the company's affairs (Re London and General Bank (No. 2) (1895) 2 Ch 673).
D. M & Co is liable because it committed a criminal offence under s.408 of the CO by omitting a required statement from the auditor's report, and criminal liability automatically establishes civil liability.

**Q5.** (Short answer — answer in no more than 120 words.) Pearl River Logistics Ltd ('PRL') is a private company with two formally appointed directors, Sam and Tina. Fiona has **never** been appointed as a director, but over the past three years she has negotiated settlements with PRL's creditors, countersigned company cheques, signed supply contracts describing herself as 'Director of PRL', and has been introduced to suppliers as the person running the company. Meanwhile, Sam and Tina have, on every major decision, acted strictly in accordance with the instructions of Fiona's husband, Gordon, who is a retired accountant giving the instructions in a private capacity.

Answer the following:
(a) Identify Fiona's and Gordon's respective status under the CO, citing the relevant statutory provision and/or case authority for each, and state one consequence that follows for Fiona.
(b) Assuming Fiona is treated as a director, can the members of PRL remove her before the expiry of her term by way of a **written resolution** instead of holding a general meeting? Give the reason and the statutory basis.
