**Q3 (MCQ — scenario)**

Pelican Retail Ltd has issued ordinary shares and 8% cumulative preference shares. The board proposes to capitalise part of the company's reserves by issuing 500,000 NEW preference shares, ranking pari passu with the existing preference shares, to the existing ordinary shareholders. Ms Lau, an existing preference shareholder, objects, arguing that the proposal is a variation of class rights which requires the written consent of holders of at least 75% of the voting rights of her class or a special resolution at a class meeting under s.180. Which of the following is the BEST analysis?

A. Ms Lau is correct: any issue of new shares of the same class dilutes existing class members and therefore amounts to a variation requiring s.180 consent
B. Ms Lau is wrong: following White v Bristol Aeroplane Co Ltd (1953) Ch 65, the proposal affects only the enjoyment of the existing preference shareholders' rights, not the rights themselves, so it is not a variation and no class consent is required
C. Ms Lau is correct: because the new shares are issued to ordinary shareholders only, the preference shareholders are unfairly prejudiced and may apply to court under s.182(1) to have the issue disallowed
D. Ms Lau is wrong: variation of class rights never requires class consent under the CO; it is governed solely by the articles

**Q4 (MCQ — scenario)**

Nero Holdings Ltd ("NHL") is the holding company of Nero Foods Ltd ("NFL"), its wholly owned subsidiary. Mr Chan agrees to buy all the shares in NHL from its existing shareholder for HK$8 million, funded by a bank loan. At the bank's request, NFL provides a guarantee of Mr Chan's loan. NFL's directors argue that the guarantee is lawful because NFL's business will benefit from Mr Chan's planned expansion of the group, so the assistance was given for a "larger purpose" in good faith in NFL's interest. Which of the following is the BEST analysis?

A. The guarantee is lawful: it falls squarely within the principal purpose exception in s.278 because the group's expansion is a larger purpose of NFL
B. The guarantee is lawful: a subsidiary is always free to guarantee loans for the acquisition of its holding company's shares because s.275(1) only catches the issuing company itself
C. The guarantee is prohibited financial assistance under s.275(1) (a guarantee being within s.274(1)); following Ricco (Int'l) Co Ltd v Uni-Harvest Int'l Ltd (2017) 6 HKC 487, the expected business benefit is merely the "reason" for giving the assistance, not a separate "larger purpose", so the s.278 exception fails — although the underlying transaction is not invalid per se (s.276)
D. The guarantee is prohibited, and therefore the sale of NHL's shares to Mr Chan is automatically void and cannot be enforced by either party
