# M10 Chapter 6: Money Laundering, Bribery and Corruption — Phase 1 速查表 + Phase 2 閃卡

## Phase 1 速查表

### Learning Objectives
1. **Apply** the key provisions of Hong Kong's anti-money laundering legislation (DTROPO, OSCO, AMLO, UNATMO) to factual scenarios, including the offences, disclosure obligations and penalties.
2. **Explain** the obligations of accountants and other designated non-financial businesses and professions (DNFBPs) under the AMLO and the HKICPA AML Guidelines — customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring, suspicious transaction reporting and record-keeping.
3. **Analyse** the role of accountants as trust or company service providers (TCSPs) under the TCSP licensing regime.
4. **Apply** the offences under the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) to private-sector and public-sector scenarios, using the IRAC approach.
5. **Describe** the roles and powers of the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU) and the Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC).

### 必背考點

#### A. Anti-Money Laundering 四大法例（Offences / Obligations / Penalties / Court Action）

**1. Drug Trafficking (Recovery of Proceeds) Ordinance (Cap. 405) ('DTROPO')**
- **s.25(1)** — offence: a person **deals with property**, **knowing or having reasonable grounds to believe** that the whole or part of the property directly or indirectly represents **proceeds of drug trafficking**.
- **s.25(3)** — penalty on conviction upon indictment under s.25(1): max **14 years' imprisonment + fine of HK$5 million**.
- **s.25A(1)** — obligation: a person must **disclose to an authorised officer** (e.g. police officer, Customs and Excise officer) **as soon as it is reasonable** if he/she knows or suspects that property represents proceeds of drug trafficking.
- **s.25A(5)** — **tipping-off** offence: knowing or suspecting that a disclosure has been made, disclosing to any other person any matter **likely to prejudice any investigation**.
- **s.25A(8)** — penalty for failure to disclose under s.25A(1) (and per Exhibit 6.1, tipping-off): max **3 years' imprisonment + fine of HK$500,000**.
- **s.3** — court power to issue **confiscation orders** (court must specify a period of **less than six months** for the defendant to pay).
- **ss.10 and 11** — Court of First Instance may grant a **restraint order** (prohibiting dealing with realisable property) or a **charging order** (charge on realisable property to secure payment to the Government). Knowingly contravening either order: fine of HK$500,000 (or the property value if greater) + 5 years' imprisonment (ss.10(16) and 11(14)).

**2. Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) ('OSCO')**
- Enacted 1994; mirrors DTROPO but covers proceeds of an **indictable offence** (defined in the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) as one not triable summarily; for DTROPO/OSCO purposes, one that may be tried either summarily or on indictment).
- **s.25(1)** — same dealing offence as DTROPO, but for proceeds of **any person's indictable offence**.
- **s.25(3)** — same penalty: max **14 years' imprisonment + HK$5 million fine**.
- **s.25A(1)** — same disclosure obligation; **s.25A(5)** tipping-off; **s.25A(8)** penalty max **3 years + HK$500,000**.
- **s.8** — confiscation orders; **s.14** — restraint order / charging order by the Court of First Instance.
- 考試伏位：DTROPO 只限 drug trafficking proceeds；OSCO 涵蓋所有 indictable offence proceeds（包括 fraud、theft、tax evasion 等）。毒品案先諗 DTROPO，其他罪行諗 OSCO。

**3. Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) ('AMLO')** — in force 1 April 2012
- **s.5** — criminal offence if a **financial institution (FI)** knowingly, or with intent to defraud a relevant authority (RA: HKMA / SFC / Insurance Authority), contravenes a specified provision (listed in s.5(11)).
  - **s.5(5)** — knowing contravention: max **2 years' imprisonment + HK$1,000,000 fine**.
  - **s.5(6)** — contravention with **intent to defraud** RAs: max **7 years' imprisonment + HK$1,000,000 fine**.
- **s.5A** (added by the 2018 Amendment Ordinance, effective 1 March 2018) — imposes AML/CTF requirements on **DNFBPs**: accounting professionals and legal professionals (when preparing/carrying out specified transactions: buying/selling real estate; managing client money, securities or assets; managing bank/savings/securities accounts; organising contributions for creation/operation/management of corporations; creation/operation/management of legal persons or arrangements; buying/selling business entities; trust or company services), **estate agents** (real estate transactions), and **TCSP licensees**.
- **Schedule 2** — **Part 2: customer due diligence (CDD)**; **Part 3: record-keeping**; FIs/DNFBPs must take all reasonable measures to ensure proper safeguards against contravention and to mitigate ML/TF risks.
- **s.21 / s.21(2)** — RAs may take disciplinary action against FIs: public reprimand, remedial action, pecuniary penalty. **s.23** — RAs must follow guidelines when imposing pecuniary penalties. Supervisory fines can reach **HK$10 million or 3 times the profit gained / costs avoided**.
- DNFBPs (accountants, lawyers, estate agents) face **no criminal sanctions under AMLO** for non-compliance — they are penalised via their own professional mechanisms; TCSPs via the licensing system.

**TCSP licensing regime (from 1 March 2018)**
- **s.1, Part 1, Schedule 1** — statutory definition of a TCS business: forming corporations/legal persons; acting/arranging a director, secretary, partner or similar; providing registered office/business address; acting/arranging trustee of an express trust or nominee shareholder (other than for a listed corporation).
- **s.53B** — licensing requirement **does not apply** to: qualified accounting professionals, qualified legal professionals, the HKSAR Government, authorized institutions (s.2(1) Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155)), licensed corporations (SFO (Cap. 571)) where TCS business is ancillary to principal business, and persons prescribed by regulation.
- **s.53F(1)–(2)** — offence to carry on a TCS business **without a licence**: fine of **HK$100,000 + 6 months' imprisonment**.
- **s.53H / s.53I** — **fit and proper test**: Registrar of Companies considers e.g. ML/TF convictions (in or outside HK), undischarged bankruptcy, failure to comply with AMLO requirements, liquidation/winding-up of a corporate applicant.
- **s.53O** — licence valid for **3 years** (or shorter as determined); Registrar may grant, renew, suspend, revoke, or impose conditions.
- Registrar may publicly reprimand defaulting TCSPs, order remedial action and impose a pecuniary penalty **up to HK$500,000**.
- 重點：qualified accountants **免攞牌，但唔免合規** — still must comply with CDD, ongoing monitoring and record-keeping under Schedule 2 of the AMLO.

**4. United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance (Cap. 575) ('UNATMO')**
- **s.7** — offence: **provides or collects property** with the intention, or knowing, or having reasonable grounds to believe that the property is to be used to commit **terrorist acts**.
- **s.8** — offence: **offers property or financial services** to benefit a **terrorist or terrorist associate**.
- **s.8A** — must not, except under licence from the **Secretary for Security**, deal with property knowing / being reckless as to whether it is terrorist property, owned/controlled by a terrorist or terrorist associate, or held on their behalf/direction.
- **s.12(1)** — offence to **fail to report** knowledge or suspicion that property is terrorist property to an authorised officer as soon as practicable → penalty **s.14(5): max 3 months' imprisonment + a fine**.
- **s.12(5)** — **tipping-off** offence → penalty **s.14(6): max 3 years' imprisonment + a fine**.
- **s.6** — Secretary for Security may freeze funds by written notice where there are reasonable grounds to suspect they are terrorist property.
- **s.13** — on application by the **Secretary for Justice**, the Court of First Instance may order **forfeiture** of terrorist property.
- **ss.14(1) and 14(1A)** — max penalty for ss.7, 8, 8A offences: **14 years' imprisonment + a fine**.

#### B. Leading Cases（full citations + ratio）

1. **Seng Yuet Fong v HKSAR (1999) KCQ3, FAMC No. 26 of 1998** (CFA) — 'having reasonable grounds for belief' in **s.25(1) DTROPO** contains **both an objective and a subjective element**: (i) grounds that a common-sense, right-thinking member of the community would consider sufficient to lead a person to believe (objective); (ii) the defendant **knew** those grounds (subjective). Prosecution must prove both.
2. **Oei Hengky Wiryo v HKSAR (No. 2) (2007) 10 HKCFAR 98** (CFA) — under **s.25(1) OSCO**, the prosecution need **NOT prove** that the property dealt with was **in fact** the proceeds of a predicate offence; conviction is possible if the defendant had reasonable grounds to believe it represented proceeds of crime.
3. **HKSAR v Pang Hung Fai (FACC 8 of 2013)** (CFA) — (i) the defendant's **own perception, belief or prejudice** (e.g. long-standing trust in a friend) may be taken into account when assessing 'reasonable grounds to believe'; (ii) the correct test is the **'would believe' test** (would a reasonable person looking at the grounds believe the property represented proceeds of an indictable offence?) — the Court of Appeal's **'could believe' test** was an inappropriately low standard.
4. **HKSAR v Yang Sigai (2016) HKEC 2068, FACV No. 8 of 2015** (CFA) — where the property came from **overseas**, the underlying offence need **not be identified**, and s.25(1) OSCO does not require proof of knowledge of the actual conduct that produced the property.
5. **HKSAR v Yeung Ka Sing Carson (FACC Nos. 5 and 6 of 2015)** (CFA) — upheld **Oei Hengky Wiryo** (no need to prove the predicate offence) and confirmed the **Seng Yuet Fong** formulation: the accused had grounds for believing, and the grounds must be reasonable such that anyone looking at them objectively would so believe.
6. **HKSAR v Leung Yat Ming & Another (1999) 2 HKLRD 402** — court **lifted the corporate veil** (company as a cloak of deception) and convicted the defendants under **s.9(3) POBO** for deceiving their employer (university) by an application containing false statements.

#### C. Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) ('POBO')

**Definitions — s.2**
- **Advantage**: gift, loan, fee, reward or commission (money, security, property or interest); office, employment or contract; payment/release of a loan; service or favour; promise of any of these; exercise of or forbearance from rights/powers/duties. **Entertainment** (= food or drink, e.g. dinners, or entertainment provided with food and drink) is **NOT** an advantage.
- **Agent**: a public servant and any person **employed by or acting for another** (directors are agents of the company; the Board is generally the principal).
- **Public servant**: prescribed officers + employees of public bodies (e.g. MTR, hospitals).

**Offences**
| Section | Sector | Offence |
|---|---|---|
| s.3 | Public | Prescribed officer soliciting/accepting advantage **without the Chief Executive's permission** |
| s.4 | Public | Public servant soliciting/accepting advantage as inducement/reward for performing duties (offeror also guilty) |
| s.5 | Public | Advantage for assistance/influence in relation to a **contract** (offeror also guilty) |
| s.6 / s.7 | Private | Advantage to withdraw/refrain from a **tender** / from **bidding at an auction** of a public body |
| s.8 | Private | Advantage offered to a public servant of a department the agent is dealing with |
| **s.9(1)** | Private | Agent, **without lawful authority or reasonable excuse**, solicits/accepts advantage as inducement to/reward for an act in relation to the **principal's affairs** |
| s.9(2) | Private | **Offering** an advantage to an agent (the mirror offence — the offeror's side) |
| **s.9(3)** | Private | Agent uses any **receipt, account or other document** with a **false or erroneous statement** to **deceive the principal** |
| s.9(5) | Private | Leeway: permission applied for and given **as soon as reasonably possible after** the offer/acceptance |
| s.10 | Public | Prescribed officer maintaining a **standard of living / assets not commensurate with official emoluments** |

**Other key sections**
- **s.11** — both giver and acceptor guilty **even if the purpose of the bribe is not carried out**; unavailable defences: no power/opportunity to do the act; no intention to do the act; did not in fact do the act.
- **s.12** — penalties: ss.5 or 6 → max **10 years + HK$500,000**; s.10 → max **10 years + HK$1,000,000**; other sections → max **7 years + HK$500,000**.
- **s.19** — **no defence** that the advantage is **customary** in any profession, trade, vocation or calling.
- **s.24** — **burden of proving** lawful authority or reasonable excuse lies on the **accused**.

#### D. JFIU and ICAC

**Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU)** — set up **1989**; joint operation of the HK Police Force and Customs & Excise Department. It is the **central hub for suspicious transaction reports (STRs)** but has **no investigative power**; it refers reports to local/overseas enforcement departments. Reports arise under **s.25A DTROPO, s.25A OSCO and s.12 UNATMO**. **'SAFE' approach**: **S**creen the customer/transaction; **A**sk questions to clarify; **F**ind out whether the transaction matches expectations; **E**valuate whether it is really suspicious.

**HKICPA Guidelines on AML/CTF for Professional Accountants (February 2018)** — based on guidelines published under **s.7 AMLO**; form part of the **Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants**. Breach = not liable to legal proceedings, but admissible as evidence in court proceedings under AMLO. '**must**' = mandatory/statutory; 'should/would/may' = guidance/good practice.
- **Section 620 CDD** — know the true identity of each client, business type, source/use of funds; standard CDD for all; **SDD** for lower risk, **EDD** for higher risk (e.g. **PEPs** — individuals with prominent public functions outside mainland China, their family members and close associates).
- **Section 630 Ongoing Monitoring** — review client documents regularly; watch transactions inconsistent with business/risk profile/source of funds; examine abnormally large or unusual transactions and record findings in writing.
- **Section 640 STRs** — must appoint a **Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO)**; report to the JFIU **without delay**; report **as soon as reasonably practicable**; internal controls to prevent **tipping-off**; **suspicion is more than speculation but less than proof or knowledge**.
- **Section 650** — targeted financial sanctions: name checks against UN Security Council sanctions lists; STR to JFIU if terrorist-related suspicion.
- **Section 660 Record Keeping** — identify/verify beneficial owner; clear and complete audit trail; records available to RAs on a timely basis; retain records **at least 5 years** after the transaction or cessation of the business relationship.

**ICAC** — established **February 1974** by Governor Sir Murray MacLehose; functions independently and is **directly accountable to the Chief Executive** (Basic Law). Three-pronged strategy via three departments: **Operations** (law enforcement — investigates offences under the ICACO (Cap. 204), POBO and ECICO (Cap. 554)), **Corruption Prevention** (reviews practices/procedures), **Community Relations** (education and public support).
- Investigative powers under **POBO**: **s.13** inspect accounts/documents, require production (s.13(3): obstruction → HK$20,000 + 1 year); **s.13A** CFI order for IRD material; **s.14(1)** require suspects to provide property/income/expenditure details; **s.14C** restraining order on property; **s.17(1A)** enter and search premises; **s.17A** surrender of travel documents.
- Under **ICACO (Cap. 204)**: **s.10** arrest without warrant; **s.10A** procedure after arrest; **s.10AA** arrest of persons granted bail (bring before a magistrate within **24 hours**); **s.12** duties of the Commissioner; **s.13** powers of the Commissioner.

#### E. IRAC Application Points（考試答題框架）
- **Issue**: 先認清係 laundering（OSCO/DTROPO）定 terrorist property（UNATMO）定 bribery（POBO）——Exam Lens 指出 Jun 2026 有考生將 anti-corruption 誤判為 money laundering，issue-spotting 係成敗關鍵。
- **Rule**: pinpoint section — 答 s.25(1) OSCO 要分 DTROPO（drug trafficking）定 OSCO（indictable offence）；答 PBO 要分 s.9(1)（agent 收受）、s.9(2)（offeror）、s.9(3)（假文件）。
- **Application**: 將 facts 逐項套入 elements（dealing? knowledge/reasonable grounds? advantage? principal's affairs? permission?）；記住 **suspicion 已足夠觸發 disclosure duty**，唔使證實罪行（Oei Hengky Wiryo; HKICPA Guidelines s.640）。
- **Conclusion**: 明確落結論 + 指出 penalty（如 s.25(3)：14 年 + HK$5M；s.25A(8)：3 年 + HK$500,000）。

### 易混淆概念對比

**Table 1 — AML offences: Dealing vs Failure to Disclose vs Tipping-off**

| | **Dealing offence** (s.25(1) DTROPO / s.25(1) OSCO / ss.7–8A UNATMO) | **Failure to disclose** (s.25A(1) DTROPO/OSCO / s.12(1) UNATMO) | **Tipping-off** (s.25A(5) DTROPO/OSCO / s.12(5) UNATMO) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Actually **deals with** the tainted property | Knows or **suspects** property is tainted, but stays silent | A disclosure/STR **has been made**, then leaks information |
| Mens rea | Knowing, or **reasonable grounds to believe** (Seng Yuet Fong two-element test) | Knowledge or **suspicion** (less than proof, more than speculation) | Knowing or suspecting **that a disclosure has been made** + disclosure likely to prejudice investigation |
| Max penalty | 14 years + HK$5M (s.25(3)); UNATMO ss.7/8/8A: 14 years + fine (ss.14(1)/(1A)) | 3 years + HK$500,000 (s.25A(8)); UNATMO: **3 months** + fine (s.14(5)) | 3 years + HK$500,000 (s.25A(8)); UNATMO: 3 years + fine (s.14(6)) |
| 點樣分辨／考試伏位 | 被告自己有份「處理」黑錢 | 被告只係「知情不報」，無處理黑錢都可以中 | 前提係**已經有人報咗** STR——若無人報告，tipping-off 唔成立（只可能中 failure to disclose）。警告客戶「有人查緊你」先係 tipping-off |

**Table 2 — POBO: s.9(1) vs s.9(2) vs s.9(3); Advantage vs Entertainment**

| | **s.9(1)** | **s.9(2)** | **s.9(3)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who commits | **Agent** soliciting/accepting advantage | **Offeror** giving advantage to an agent | **Agent** using false/erroneous receipt, account or document |
| Gist | Advantage as inducement/reward for act in relation to **principal's affairs** | Mirror image of s.9(1) — the giver's side | Intent to **deceive the principal** — no advantage need change hands |
| Key authority / notes | Advantage 包括 loan（s.2）；permission 要由 Board 全體批（s.9(5) 只係事後補救） | 雙方都罪成，即使目的未達成（s.11） | Leung Yat Ming：揭穿公司面紗後用虛假申請呃僱主 = s.9(3) |
| **Advantage vs Entertainment** | **Advantage** (s.2): gifts, loans, fees, rewards, commissions, contracts, services, favours, promises | **Entertainment** (s.2): food or drink (e.g. dinners) and entertainment provided with it | 考試伏位：請食飯 **唔係 advantage**，所以單純接受晚宴唔觸犯 s.9；但 cash rebate、gift、loan 就係 |
| 點樣分辨 | 有冇「利益」轉移 + 有冇 principal permission | 邊個係主動一方（收 vs 送） | 有冇**假文件**去呃僱主——Jun 2024 考生將 s.9(3) 同 s.9(1) 答案重複，被扣分 |

## Phase 2 閃卡

### Card 1
**Front:** What are the four principal pieces of anti-money laundering legislation in Hong Kong, and what does each primarily target?

**Back:**
- Drug Trafficking (Recovery of Proceeds) Ordinance (Cap. 405) — proceeds of **drug trafficking**
- Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) — proceeds of any **indictable offence**
- Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) — regulatory regime: CDD/record-keeping for FIs and DNFBPs; TCSP licensing
- United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance (Cap. 575) — **terrorist property** and terrorist financing

💬 記法：「毒品 405、重罪 455、監管 615、反恐 575」。首兩條係刑事化洗黑錢，615 係合規框架，575 專門對付恐怖分子資金。

### Card 2
**Front:** Under Seng Yuet Fong v HKSAR (1999) KCQ3, what two elements must the prosecution prove for 'having reasonable grounds for belief' in s.25(1) DTROPO?

**Back:**
- **Objective element**: grounds that a common-sense, right-thinking member of the community would consider sufficient to lead a person to believe the property represents proceeds of crime; AND
- **Subjective element**: the defendant **knew** those grounds.
- Both elements must be proved.

💬 「有合理理由相信」= 客觀上一般人睇到呢啲跡象都會信（objective）+ 被告本人真係知悉呢啲跡象（subjective）。兩樣齊先定罪。

### Card 3
**Front:** What did the CFA decide in Oei Hengky Wiryo v HKSAR (No. 2) (2007) 10 HKCFAR 98 about proving the 'predicate offence' under s.25(1) OSCO?

**Back:**
- The prosecution does **NOT** have to prove the property dealt with was **in fact** the proceeds of a predicate offence.
- It is enough that the defendant had **reasonable grounds to believe** the property represented the proceeds of crime.
- Upheld in HKSAR v Yeung Ka Sing Carson (FACC Nos. 5 and 6 of 2015).

💬 即係話：就算啲錢最後查唔到／證唔到係黑錢，只要被告「有合理理由相信」係黑錢而照樣處理，都已經罪成。呢點係 AML 部分最常考嘅案例原則。

### Card 4
**Front:** In HKSAR v Pang Hung Fai (FACC 8 of 2013), which test did the CFA endorse for assessing 'reasonable grounds to believe' — the 'would believe' test or the 'could believe' test — and what else may the court consider?

**Back:**
- The **'would believe' test**: would a reasonable person, looking at the grounds, believe that the property represented the proceeds of an indictable offence.
- The Court of Appeal's **'could believe' test** was an inappropriately **low** standard.
- The defendant's **own perception and evaluation** of the facts (e.g. 30 years of friendship and trust in Kwok) may be taken into account.

💬 「Would believe」要求高過「could believe」：唔係「可能會信」就夠，而係一個合理嘅人「真係會咁信」。被告個人嘅信任背景都可以考慮。

### Card 5
**Front:** Which bodies are DNFBPs under s.5A of the AMLO, and what must they do under Schedule 2?

**Back:**
- DNFBPs: **accounting professionals** and **legal professionals** (when handling specified transactions, e.g. real estate, managing client money, trust or company services), **estate agents**, and **TCSP licensees**.
- **Schedule 2, Part 2**: customer due diligence (CDD) requirements.
- **Schedule 2, Part 3**: record-keeping requirements.
- Must take all reasonable measures to ensure proper safeguards and mitigate ML/TF risks.
- Breach: no criminal sanctions under AMLO for DNFBPs — dealt with via professional disciplinary mechanisms / TCSP licensing regime.

💬 2018 年修訂之後，會計師、律師、地產代理同 TCSP 同銀行一樣要做 CDD 同保存紀錄。記住：會計師違規唔會坐監，但會被專業團體紀律處分。

### Card 6
**Front:** Under the TCSP licensing regime in the AMLO: (a) who is exempt from holding a licence; (b) what is the penalty for carrying on a TCS business without a licence; (c) how long is a licence valid?

**Back:**
- (a) Exempt under **s.53B**: qualified accounting professionals, qualified legal professionals, HKSAR Government, authorized institutions (Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155)), licensed corporations (SFO (Cap. 571)) where TCS is ancillary to principal business, prescribed persons.
- (b) **s.53F(1)–(2)**: fine of **HK$100,000 and 6 months' imprisonment**.
- (c) **s.53O**: **3 years** (or shorter as determined by the Registrar of Companies).
- Fit and proper test: **ss.53H–53I** (ML/TF convictions, undischarged bankruptcy, non-compliance with AMLO, liquidation of corporate applicant).

💬 考試陷阱：會計師做公司秘書服務**唔使攞 TCSP 牌**，但 **Schedule 2 嘅 CDD/record-keeping 一樣要做足**——「免牌唔等於免責」。

### Card 7
**Front:** State the four conditions that must all be satisfied for an offence under s.9(1) of the POBO, and the key defences/burden rules.

**Back:**
Conditions (all must be present):
1. The person is an **agent** (employee or person acting for another — s.2);
2. He/she **solicited or accepted an advantage**;
3. As an **inducement to or reward for** an act in relation to the **principal's affairs**;
4. **Without lawful authority or reasonable excuse** (principal's permission).

Defences/burden:
- **s.9(5)**: permission obtained as soon as reasonably possible after offer/acceptance;
- **s.11**: no defence that the bribe's purpose was not carried out;
- **s.19**: no defence that the advantage is customary;
- **s.24**: burden of proving lawful authority/reasonable excuse is on the **accused**.

💬 四個條件缺一不可：agent + advantage + principal's affairs + 無批准。Answer 入面要逐項對落 facts。注意「entertainment」（請食飯）唔算 advantage，係常見 distractor。

### Card 8
**Front:** What is the JFIU, what are its limits, and what is the 'SAFE' approach?

**Back:**
- **Joint Financial Intelligence Unit** — set up **1989**, jointly run by the HK Police Force and the Customs and Excise Department.
- Central hub receiving **suspicious transaction reports (STRs)** made under s.25A DTROPO, s.25A OSCO and s.12 UNATMO.
- It has **no investigative power** itself — it refers reports to local and overseas enforcement departments for action.
- **SAFE**: **S**creen the customer/transaction; **A**sk the customer questions; **F**ind out if the transaction matches expectations; **E**valuate whether it is truly suspicious.

💬 JFIU 係「收件中心」而唔係「偵查機關」——佢收集 STR，再轉介畀執法部門。考試問「JFIU investigates…」多數係陷阱選項。

## 本章急救詞彙

| EN Term | 繁中白話 | English Model Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| money laundering | 洗黑錢——將犯罪得嚟嘅錢「洗」到好似合法收入咁 | Money laundering is the process by which criminals change the identity of money or property so that it appears to be derived from a legitimate source. |

| reasonable grounds to believe | 有合理理由相信——客觀跡象 + 被告知悉，兩者齊備 | The Seng Yuet Fong test requires both objective grounds and the defendant's subjective knowledge of those grounds. |
| tipping-off | 通水——已有人舉報後向當事人洩露風聲，妨礙調查 | Warning a client that a suspicious transaction report has been filed may constitute tipping-off under s.25A(5) of the OSCO. |
| suspicious transaction report (STR) | 可疑交易報告——向 JFIU 舉報可疑資金 | The practice must file an STR with the JFIU as soon as reasonably practicable once suspicion is formed. |
| customer due diligence (CDD) | 盡職審查——開戶/接客前核實客戶身份、資金來源 | Under Schedule 2 to the AMLO, accountants must perform CDD before carrying out specified transactions for clients. |
| politically exposed person (PEP) | 政治人物——高官及其家人、密切聯繫人，屬高風險客戶 | Enhanced due diligence is required where a client or its beneficial owner is a PEP. |
| advantage (POBO s.2) | 利益——禮物、貸款、佣金、服務等都算，但請食飯唔算 | An interest-free loan from a supplier is an 'advantage' under s.2 of the POBO, whereas a dinner invitation is mere entertainment. |
