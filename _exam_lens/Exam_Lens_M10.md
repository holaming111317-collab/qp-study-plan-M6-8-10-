# Exam Lens Briefing — HKICPA QP Module 10: Business & Company Law

**Purpose:** Intelligence briefing for quiz item writers (chapter-level OTQ quizzes). Based on the Pilot Paper (2019, CBE), Jun 2024 / Jun 2025 / Jun 2026 question + answer papers, and Panelists' Reports for Jun 2024, Dec 2024, Jun 2025, Dec 2025, Jun 2026.

---

## 1. Exam Structure

- **Duration:** 3 hours (180 minutes), single section.
- **Format:** 100% written questions — **no MCQ, no separate case-study section**. All questions compulsory.
- **Current format (Jun 2024 → Jun 2026):** **SIX questions, 100 marks**, all compulsory.
  - Jun 2024: Q1 15 / Q2 10 / Q3 15 / Q4 20 / Q5 25 / Q6 15.
  - Jun 2025: Q1 10 / Q2 20 / Q3 10 / Q4 20 / Q5 20 / Q6 20.
  - Jun 2026: Q1 15 / Q2 15 / Q3 15 / Q4 25 / Q5 20 / Q6 10.
- **Format change history:** The 2019 Pilot Paper had **SEVEN** compulsory questions (40/10/15/15/10/5/5) with one dominant 40-mark integrated mega-case. From at least Jun 2024 onwards the paper standardised to **six questions with no single question above 25 marks** — flatter mark distribution, broader topic coverage per sitting. Quiz writers should calibrate to the 6-question era, not the pilot.
- **Timing discipline built into the paper:** every question states "X marks – approximately Y minutes" at roughly **1.8 minutes per mark** (5 marks ≈ 9 min; 15 marks ≈ 27 min; 25 marks ≈ 45 min).
- **Question anatomy (post-2024 papers):** each question = short "Case background" scenario (150–350 words) + 1–3 sub-requirements (a)/(b)/(c), each worth 2–15 marks. Sub-parts routinely **mix topics within one scenario** (e.g. Jun 2026 Q2 = employment + anti-corruption + long service payment; Jun 2024 Q4 = agency + negligence).
- **Verbs used:** "Analyse whether…" (application marks), "Explain…" (knowledge marks), "Explain … and analyse whether…" (hybrid). Roughly half the marks on any paper are "analyse" marks tied to the scenario facts.
- **Open-book reality:** M10 is the **only open-book paper at Associate Level**; delivered as **CBE on a BYOD basis** (candidates bring their own laptop). The Jun 2026 paper is structured with an explicit "Answer Area" under each sub-question, consistent with typed CBE responses. Practical implications confirmed by the answers/reports:
  - Suggested answers cite **precise Ordinance sections** (e.g. s.9(3) PBO Cap.201; s.31R EO Cap.57; s.24 SOGO Cap.26; s.177(1) CWUMPO Cap.32; ss.723–727 CO Cap.622) and **named cases with year/citation** (Roscorla v Thomas [1842], Salomon v Salomon [1897], Gilford Motor v Horne [1933]) — open-book means candidates are expected to *locate and deploy* authority, not memorise it, but the exam still rewards accurate citation.
  - Panelists repeatedly warn that candidates who "write what they know" (i.e. transcribe notes/textbook) instead of applying law to the scenario score poorly — open-book is not copy-book.
  - Suggested answers carry the standing caveat: "longer than what candidates are expected to give… credit will be awarded for valid answers not fully covered."

## 2. Question Style

- **Scenario-based throughout.** Every question embeds the law in a named-character fact pattern ("Tommy is an artist…", "Billy was employed as Purchasing Manager…"). Pure bookwork questions do not appear; even "explain" parts are framed as advising a character.
- **IRAC-pattern expected answers.** Model answers consistently follow: **Issue** ("This question required candidates to discuss X") → **Rule** stated with authority → **Application** to the named facts → **Conclusion** (e.g. "Therefore, Elegant Goods is not bound to give Tommy the free gift"). Sub-conclusions are decisive and one-sided when facts are clear.
- **Citation density expected:**
  - **Statute first:** pinpoint sections with chapter numbers — Employment Ordinance (Cap.57), Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap.201), Sale of Goods Ordinance (Cap.26), Companies Ordinance (Cap.622), Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap.32), Misrepresentation Ordinance (Cap.284), Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap.455). A 5-mark answer typically cites 1–2 sections; a 15-mark answer several sections across a statutory scheme.
  - **Case law:** landmark/common-law-governed areas (contract, tort, veil-piercing, unfair prejudice) expect named cases — often with full citation in model answers (e.g. Hong Kong Fir Shipping [1962], Krell v Henry [1903], Aluminium Industrie Vaassen v Romalpa [1976], Re Taiwa Land Investment [1981] HKLR 297, Wong Man Yin v Ricacorp [2003]). Panelists single out missing case citation as a weakness even when the conclusion was right (Dec 2024 Q2(b); Jun 2026 Q5(a)).
- **Hybrid explain+analyse parts are the norm** (e.g. "explain … and analyse whether…"), so a quiz stem that only tests recall under-represents exam demand.
- **Issue-spotting is the discriminator:** the most common failure mode across all five reports is **misidentifying the legal issue** (e.g. past consideration misread as misrepresentation or promissory estoppel; revocation of offer not spotted; ratification misread as estoppel; negligent misstatement answered as ordinary negligence; deregistration answered as liquidation).
- **Cross-topic scenarios are standard** — one storyline can span 2–3 LP chapters, and sub-parts explicitly signpost the syllabus area ("In relation to agency law…", "In relation to the tort of negligence…").

## 4. Panelist-Reported Weaknesses (by session)

**Recurring across ALL five sessions:**
- Candidates write out everything they know on the topic instead of identifying and answering the specific issue ("write what they know" syndrome — fatal in an open-book paper).
- Insufficient application of legal principles to the scenario facts; conclusions without analysis.
- Missing Ordinance section references and/or case citations even when the conclusion is correct.
- Shallow syllabus coverage — candidates bank on 2–3 "popular" topics and collapse on anything less common.
- Some candidates leave parts unanswered (time management at the back of the paper — Q5/Q6 suffer).

## 5. Difficulty Calibration Guide (for 5-question chapter OTQ quizzes)

**What "exam-standard" means for M10:**
- The exam is 100% scenario-based written answers; there are no MCQs in the real paper. An OTQ quiz is therefore a *training proxy* — it should test the same **issue-spotting + rule-selection + application** skills, not just recall.
- An "exam-standard" item = one where a candidate who merely copied their open-book notes would get it **wrong**, and a candidate who spotted the correct issue and matched the correct rule/exception gets it right.

**Difficulty tiers observed in the real paper (use as anchor):**
- *Easy (candidates "performed well"):* straight recall of a popular framework — creation of agency, elements of negligence, summary dismissal grounds, sufficient-not-adequate consideration, dormant status, disclosure thresholds. ≈ 40–50% of exam marks.
- *Medium ("fair"):* application of a known rule to facts with one twist — ratification vs estoppel, PBO s.9(2) vs s.9(3), apparent authority limits, misrepresentation remedies. ≈ 30–40%.
- *Hard ("poor"):* less-popular sub-topic OR multi-layer analysis — past consideration exceptions, novus actus interveniens, voluntary winding-up of solvent company, veil-lifting in family-property context, just-and-equitable winding-up, undisclosed principal, vicarious liability. ≈ 15–25%.

**Recommended mix for a 5-item chapter quiz:**
- 2 × easy (rule recall + one-step identification; tests the core framework of the chapter).
- 2 × medium (mini-scenario, 2–4 sentences, requiring selection of the correct rule/exception and the correct conclusion; distractors = the misidentifications panelists actually reported, e.g. estoppel when answer is ratification; s.9(1) when answer is s.9(2)).
- 1 × hard (longer mini-scenario or a less-popular sub-topic; requires recognising the issue before the rule — mirror the paper's "poor performance" items for that chapter).

**Item-writing rules derived from the exam:**
- Every medium/hard item must be a **mini-scenario with named parties**, matching the paper's voice ("In relation to agency law…").
- Distractors should be **plausible neighbouring doctrines** (the exact confusions in Section 4), not random wrong answers.
- Where the chapter is statute-driven, at least one item should test **pinpoint section knowledge** (correct Ordinance + section number), reflecting the exam's citation expectations.
- For case-law-driven chapters (contract, tort, veil, unfair prejudice), at least one item should test **which landmark case establishes which principle**.
- Calibrate time: an exam-standard medium OTQ ≈ 2–3 minutes; a 5-item quiz should be completable in ~12–15 minutes (proportional to the paper's 1.8 min/mark pace).
- Never make "copy from the book" sufficient — if the correct answer is a verbatim LP sentence with no application step, it is below exam standard for medium/hard items.
